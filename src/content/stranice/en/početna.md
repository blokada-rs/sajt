  - title: Citizens’ assemblies
    text: |-
      On March 15, 2025, students called on citizens to **self-organize** through citizens’ assemblies. This form of direct democratic organization is recognized by the Constitution of the Republic of Serbia and the Law on Local Self-Government. The letter to the people of Serbia, which students sent to citizens at that time, encouraged people to stand shoulder to shoulder with students and to start a new form of struggle in their local communities.

      Citizens’ assemblies have become gathering places for people in settlements, cities, local communities, and villages. They are open in nature—they are held in neighborhoods, squares, sports fields, and parks. They have become a space for exchanging opinions, joint planning, decision-making, and taking action.

      Following the example of student **plenary assemblies**, citizens’ assemblies have adopted the principles of direct democracy as an integral part of their discussions and decision-making processes. Every citizen, regardless of political affiliation, socioeconomic status, or religion, has the right to express their opinion and have their voice equally respected. In this way, the community builds a culture of **dialogue in which everyone can, and should, participate**.

      At a time when many have lost confidence in their ability to have an effect on processes that shape their lives, citizens’ assemblies reminded us that democracy does not begin and end on Election Day. It is built every day through conversation, mutual respect, taking responsibility, and willingness to act for the common good.

      Citizens’ assemblies are **not and must not be a space for party influence**. A citizens' assembly is not a political party—it is a community. Its strength does not come from hierarchy but from people who are ready to get involved in solving problems that concern us all.

      Today, there are **more than a hundred** active citizens’ assemblies in Serbia, which, through their local activities, contribute to the common struggle for a more responsible and just society. Every problem solved in a neighborhood, every initiative launched, and every voice given the opportunity to be heard confirms that citizens have the power to change the environment in which they live.

      Together we are witnessing the **development of a different form of civic organizing**, in which **direct** **democracy** is alive, present, and evolving daily. It is a reminder that community is not something we are given but something we build anew through solidarity, trust, and willingness to listen to one another.

      **The citizens' assembly is not one person. The citizens' assembly is all of us**.

  - title: Civic Front
    text: |-
      The **Civic Front** is a form of cooperation among workers, trade unions, professional associations, farmers and the Student Movement, founded on the principles of solidarity, **direct democracy** and **self-organisation**.

      It brings together different social groups with the aim of fulfilling the students' demands and achieving the objectives set out in the **Social Contract** and the **Minimum Programme**, documents adopted within the Student Movement.

      The Front operates independently of political parties and other organisations, advocating for the promotion of labour rights, the strengthening of workers' organising and greater citizen participation in decision-making processes.

      It places particular emphasis on amendments to the **Labour Law** and the **Strike Law**, prepared through **cooperation between students and representative trade unions**, as well as on other measures that serve the broader public interest.

      Its goal is to build a fairer and more accountable society in which **institutions operate in the interests of citizens rather than privileged individuals** or interest groups.
