**Education is a public good and the right of every child, regardless of where they live and how much their parents earn.** In practice, however, it increasingly behaves like a market: the cost of textbooks, workbooks, school trips and even consumables in nurseries falls on families, conditions in school depend on the municipality where the child lives, and weak oversight leaves room for abuse. Education's share of GDP has fallen from 4.3% in 2009 to around 3.4% today, and almost 90% of that money is spent on salaries, leaving almost nothing for buildings, equipment and development. Headmasters and staff are often chosen by the ruling party, and curricula are changed without planning, by ministry directive, without teachers. That is why we propose a clear change of course: **within three years, education staff pay will reach 2.5 times the minimum wage, textbooks will become free, a school allowance will reach children at risk, and investment will rise towards 4.4% of GDP**, headmasters and staff will be chosen through public competitions, and the state bodies that run education will be subject to the same evaluation they currently demand of schools.

## Pay and employment of education staff

**We are tying basic pay in nurseries, primary schools and secondary schools to the minimum wage, with a target of 2.5 times the minimum wage for full-time work.** This will be reached in phases, **within three years at most**, in line with the rules on public sector pay. Unlike the base salary, the minimum wage is regularly adjusted to prices, so pay will no longer depend on discretionary decisions. This link applies to all staff in an institution, not only teachers: it also covers nursery teachers, professional associates, assistants, and kitchen and technical staff, whose low pay currently leaves nurseries without cooks and cleaners.

Without the need for a new competition, **anyone who has worked continuously on fixed-term contracts for more than 24 months, the maximum the Labour Law allows, and who meets the requirements of the post, will move to a permanent contract.** This abolishes the approval mechanism that turned from a temporary austerity measure into a permanent source of uncertainty. As a rule, teachers will make up their full teaching load in one school, and where that is not possible, in a predefined network of nearby schools, not in several distant institutions. Promotion to a higher title will bring a higher pay coefficient and will be open to anyone who meets the requirements, with no percentage quotas per title. **We will introduce pay supplements for work in rural, remote and difficult areas.**

Low, unpredictable pay and years on fixed-term contracts are driving people out of the profession, so schools already struggle to find teachers for certain subjects, and interest in teacher training programmes is falling. That is why **we are introducing scholarships for students on teacher training programmes, with an obligation to work in a school after graduating**, with the participation of municipalities that are short of staff. Every new teacher will get a mentor in their first years of work, and trainees in nurseries will work only under a proper employment contract and on full pay. **Professional development will be organised and paid for by the system**, according to institutions' actual needs, without being tied to paid seminars run by publishers.

We support amending the Law on Strikes: "minimum service" must not mean that everyone works as usual, because that **cancels out the right to strike** and pushes employees towards unlawful forms of protest.

## Less paperwork, more teaching

**We will abolish handwritten pupil record books, registers and after-school logs, paper records that duplicate the electronic school gradebook (esDnevnik), monthly plans written for the sake of form, unnecessarily bulky lesson preparation and the mandatory portfolio for everyone.** We will link information systems so that **every piece of data is entered only once**. The number of teams in a school will be limited and evenly distributed, and **work at weekends, at competitions and outside working hours will be paid**. We will align the standards on class and group sizes with the actual workload, especially where there are children who need additional support.

Objections to grades will be considered by the subject panel, according to clear deadlines and procedures, with the teacher required to give reasons. **Grading is a professional decision, not a political or business one, and teachers must be protected from pressure.**

## Free textbooks and a school allowance

**All required textbooks in primary and secondary school will be free.** Today, schools choose the textbooks and parents pay for them, so no one involved in the decision pays attention to the price. The market, estimated at around 120 million euros a year, is held by four publishers with an 87% share, three of which belong to the same group, as the Commission for Protection of Competition also found in 2020. That is why **schools will choose a set for four years, the ministry will set a maximum price per grade and pay for the purchase, and pupils will return the books at the end of the year.** Pupils entitled to material support will receive **a school allowance of 100 euros, indexed to inflation**, at the start of the school year. **Publishers will be banned from influencing schools through gifts and free seminars.**

Through amendments to the Law on Pupil and Student Standards and the rulebooks on scholarships, **socio-economic status will carry more weight in scoring for scholarships and loans.** Today the main condition is an average grade of at least 4.50, and up to 10% of scholarships go to vulnerable groups; under us, that share will grow. **School trips, excursions and outdoor education will become part of the educational programme, funded and organised within the public system**, so that no child stays at home because their parents cannot pay.

## Pre-school education

The laws on nurseries are clear – the problem is that they are not respected. Groups are overcrowded, "group-and-a-half" arrangements and "two groups in one room" are being introduced, and consumables are increasingly bought by parents, which reports describe as "cooperation with the family". At the same time, nursery premises are handed over during working hours to private language, sports and dance schools, so children whose parents cannot pay are left out. Kitchens are falling into disrepair, and the shortage of cooks is opening the way to handing these jobs over to private firms.

**Compliance with the standards on the number of children, space and staff will become the founder's obligation**, with new nurseries opened where places are lacking. Commercial courses will not be held during the time set aside for educational work, and **catering, maintenance and technical services will remain in the public system**. **We will introduce nationally prescribed minimum funding standards for pre-school institutions**, which do not exist today, so conditions depend solely on the municipality's budget and will. **We are keeping the "Godine uzleta" (Years of Ascent) programme, created with broad professional consensus, and giving it systematic support** instead of new reforms. Through a strategy, we will expand pre-school coverage from birth to the start of school, investing more in underdeveloped areas. The public system is the main provider; private accredited nurseries play a supplementary and temporary role, with publicly disclosed subsidies, the same standards and regular inspections.

## Investing in education

**Spending on education will rise by one percentage point of GDP over the next three years, from the current 3.4% to 4.4% of GDP**, and then gradually towards the EU average. This applies to all levels below university, including adult education. Money today does not follow actual needs, so the gaps between schools are growing, most of all to the detriment of rural and less developed areas. That is why **we will first analyse the network of institutions and the state of buildings**, for which no reliable data exists, and direct investment to where conditions are worst.

Municipalities will adopt multi-year education funding plans and report publicly on how they are implemented. **A municipality will not be able to buy tablets, robots and promotional programmes until it has met its legal obligations** – routine maintenance, safety, teaching materials and professional development. In Belgrade, we will clearly divide responsibilities between the city and the city municipalities, so that schools are not left caught between two levels of government. Schools and nurseries will be able to keep their own revenue, for example from renting out premises, for clearly defined purposes, and we will also introduce grants for development projects.

## Depoliticisation: headmasters, boards and hiring

**Headmasters of nurseries and schools will be chosen through public competitions on professional criteria**, with the participation of the staff and the governing bodies. Candidates will present a development programme for the institution, and the selection decision will be justified publicly. **The ministry will only check the lawfulness of the procedure and may annul a competition if it finds irregularities, but it will have no right to choose the candidate.** **All hiring in institutions will go through public competitions**: the requirements, candidates' CVs, the scoring criteria and the final ranking list will be published, with the participation of the institution's professional bodies and without discretionary decision-making. The same rules apply to the ministry, the school administrations and the institutes.

**Staff and parent representatives on School Boards and Management Boards, as well as members of Parents' Councils, will be chosen through public candidacy and secret ballot, without the headmaster's influence**, because it is precisely the board that oversees the headmaster's work. Municipal representatives on boards must meet clear criteria of expertise. The Pupils' Parliament will gain the right of initiative, and the school will be obliged to respond to it. We will provide staff with confidential channels for reporting pressure – from collecting signatures and "safe votes" to bullying over criticism – and protection from retaliation, linked to the existing whistleblower protection. **The National Education Council will regain its full powers**, and the institutes for the improvement and evaluation of education will become independent of the executive and gain the right to publish their findings freely.

## Curricula and grading

Getting through the syllabus has become the main focus of school, because curricula are overloaded and are changed piecemeal without the teachers who deliver them. The reform begun in 2018 reduced the number of lessons in some subjects and brought new administrative obligations, while the formative and humanistic dimension of school is being pushed aside in favour of narrow labour market needs. Primary and secondary curricula will be reformed through a public dialogue about what kind of education we want, with the participation of teachers, academics and professionals. **We will relieve curricula of unnecessary factual knowledge in favour of a real understanding of the material.** **We will abolish the mandatory number of summative grades**, so that teachers have room to monitor pupils' development more thoroughly.

**We are abolishing the formal classification of overall achievement ("excellent", "very good" and the rest)**, because only the grade average counts for further education anyway, and the race for titles leads to grades being handed out and to conflicts between parents and schools. **We are abolishing numerical grading in the first three grades of primary school, instead of only in the first.** Children will receive descriptive assessments, showing their abilities and guidance for further development, which gives parents a real picture and preserves the curiosity that today's ranking from the second grade often extinguishes.

## Evaluation and inspection

External evaluation of schools today is control, not support: institutions' ratings drop sharply without explanation, staff report that this happens even after strikes, and some advisers use their visits to recommend paid seminars. We will combine external evaluation and inspection into a single, coordinated system in which evaluators have public CVs and experience of working in a school or nursery. **Evaluation will begin with an assessment of the conditions the state and the municipality are required to provide, and they, not the staff, will be responsible for shortcomings.** **The education inspectorate will have clearly limited powers and an effective right of appeal, and must not be an instrument of pressure.** The ministry, the institutes and the school administrations will be subject to the same public evaluation.

## Inclusion and gifted pupils

Inclusion today mostly exists in the law as decoration. That is why we will revise the regulations on inclusion: **we will clearly distinguish the categories of individual education plans, precisely define the roles of personal assistants and teaching assistants, and simplify procedures.** Funding for support will become flexible and follow the institution's actual needs: the municipality will pay for the support proposed by the inter-sectoral committee, in the amount required, and **municipalities in the third and fourth development categories will receive earmarked transfers from the national budget for assistants, personal assistants, transport and snacks.** Teachers working with children who need additional support will receive financial compensation, and institutions will get more special education teachers and other professional associates, as well as the assistive technology they need.

We are expanding the support network outside school through new resource centres and mobile teams, the renewal of developmental counselling services and more day care centres, so that parents do not have to choose between their job and caring for their child. The system will not turn its back on young people with developmental disabilities once they finish school: **we will provide vocational training with job coaches, day centres and supported housing.**

**Work with gifted pupils will be governed by separate regulations**, distinct from those on developmental and learning disabilities and difficulties. **Accelerated progression and enriched programmes will be possible in regular teaching**, with closer cooperation between schools and the Talent Centre and the Petnica Science Center. We are keeping and improving specialised schools and classes for the gifted.
