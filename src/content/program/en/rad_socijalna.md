Serbia's labour legislation is among the least favourable to workers in Europe, and the rights that exist on paper mean little with unscrupulous employers, because violating them goes almost unpunished. This is the result of a "race to the bottom". Foreign investment is attracted by cheap and unprotected labour, and in particular by the fact that working outside an employment relationship has become normal; investors disappear when their subsidies do, while conscientious domestic employers carry the burden.

Starting from our position that work is **not** the purpose of human life, and that pay serves to meet people's needs so that workers and their families can live in dignity, **we are bringing work back into the employment relationship.**

**After twelve months of work, a permanent contract will be signed with the employee, annual leave allowance and a meal allowance will be paid, social assistance will stop forcing people into humiliation, and a social pension will be introduced for people over 65 with no record of service.** Before regulations are adopted, we will align them with the standards of the UN, the International Labour Organization and the EU.

## An economy that values work and equal rights for all workers

**We will abandon the policy that presents Serbia as a country of cheap labour.** We will direct state support towards production that brings good-quality jobs, technological development and wages on which a family can live decently.

**By broadening the definition of an employee, we will give people engaged under work contracts all the rights that come with an employment relationship**, so that workers on temporary and occasional jobs and those working through youth cooperatives have guaranteed basic rights. For work with the lasting characteristics of dependent employment, we will require an employment contract, while we will limit agency work to justified needs and prevent collective agreements from being avoided through subcontractors.

We will give self-employed people without employees protection appropriate to their situation, and **we will protect platform workers from disguised dependent employment and from being denied their rights.** We will regulate seasonal work in the Labour Law and limit such engagements to **a maximum of nine months in a calendar year.**

We will support youth employment through traineeships and work placements with real educational content, a mentor appointed in advance and a training plan. Such placements will last **no longer than six months with the same employer.** We will return youth cooperatives to their non-profit purpose and protect pupils from exploitation. By monitoring the employment of foreign workers, we will prevent labour costs from being pushed down, and through legal aid and interpreting support we will protect them from abuse.

## Job security and protecting workers from arbitrary dismissal

**We will shorten the general maximum for fixed-term work to 12 months.** An employer will be able to conclude no more than four such contracts with the same worker for the same job, while a contract to replace a temporarily absent employee will last until that employee returns, for a maximum of 36 months. For projects with a clearly defined content and duration, the same maximum will apply, together with an obligation to submit documentation on the timeframe and funding in advance. **During justified sick leave, the contract will be extended until the incapacity for work ends.**

Disciplinary measures will follow a procedure with the right to a defence and to trade union representation, while **termination agreements signed under coercion will be null and void**, and the employer will have to prove that an agreement was voluntary once the worker shows that coercion is likely.

A declaration of redundancy will be followed by **a six-month ban on hiring another person for the same work**, with statutory exceptions and priority for the previously dismissed worker when the need arises again. We will allow transfers of up to 20 kilometres where there is a justified need and the travel time remains roughly the same.

## Fair wages, guaranteed allowances and tax relief for lower incomes

We will bring the minimum wage closer to the actual cost of living and adjust it to inflation more often, while through collective bargaining we will develop more favourable sectoral minimums and regulate the relationship between pay and employees' qualifications and responsibilities. **Through progressive taxation and tax relief for dependent family members, we will reduce the tax burden on lower incomes**, so that the benefit goes to the worker through higher net income.

For the meal allowance, we will guarantee **at least 10% of the employer's average wage in the previous month and at least 20% of the national minimum wage**, while for the annual leave allowance we will set **at least one such average wage, with a floor equal to the minimum wage.**

Supplements to basic pay will be **at least 150% for work on a public holiday, 50% each for night work and overtime, 110% for Sundays and the weekly rest day, 30% for shift work and 26% for field work.** For every full year of employment, with any employer, seniority pay will add **at least 0.5% on top of the base salary**, and by publishing pay ranges when hiring and making data on comparable jobs available, we will make it easier to detect pay discrimination.

## Limited working hours and secure income during absence

**We will limit overtime to eight hours a week and total daily working time to 12 hours**, while employers will record and pay for every additional hour and give their employees their work schedule **at least seven days in advance.** Through a right to disconnect after working hours, we will protect free time from constant work demands.

Employers who choose to will be able to introduce **a four-day working week, with 32 full-time hours, while keeping employee salaries unchanged.**

**We will raise sick pay for illness or injury outside work during the first 30 days to at least 75% of the relevant average, with the minimum wage protected**, and we will use the more favourable three-month average to calculate sick pay and annual leave pay. We will penalise cutting basic pay on performance grounds and punishing justified absence with the loss of bonuses.

Through accessible public care services and counting family care towards pensions, we will ease the burden on people who leave paid work because of caring responsibilities.

## Protecting health and keeping jobs through effective oversight of employers

We will strengthen the capacity of the labour inspectorate and introduce external oversight of its work, while unannounced inspections will be backed by tougher sanctions for violations of workers' rights. **When a worker takes a dismissal to court and asks for protection, the inspector will be able to suspend the decision that violated their rights.** We will also speed up court protection for employees by establishing dedicated labour courts.

We will give occupational medicine a greater role in preventing damage to health, and we will make it easier for persons with disabilities to have their working capacity assessed by linking the competent services and collecting documentation in one place, and **we will require employers to consider reasonable adjustments to the job before considering ending someone's employment.** Through budget support, we will help implement measures that keep people in work. **We will provide workers assigned to night work with prior and regular medical check-ups paid for by the employer.**

## Free trade unions and real worker participation in setting working conditions

**We will make trade union membership available to all workers, including the self-employed and those in non-standard work.** We will lower the representativeness threshold at the territorial and sectoral level to 5%. By protecting employee representatives from retaliation, we will make it possible to represent workers' interests more effectively. **We will protect trade union representatives while they hold office and for two years after it ends.**

## Simpler strike organisation and clear limits on restricting the right to strike

**We will make it easier to organise a strike by shortening the general notice period to two days.** We will extend warning strikes to a maximum of 24 hours, and we will explicitly recognise solidarity strikes and the possibility of gathering outside business premises.

We will limit the special regime to activities defined by law as vital to life, health and safety, and for strike committee members to work as part of the minimum service, we will require the committee's prior consent. In these special activities the notice period will be six days, for a general strike five days, and warning strikes will be announced 24 hours in advance, so that employees and employers know in advance the obligations that come with each form of work stoppage.

**Workers who strike because their wages have not been paid will keep their right to pay during the stoppage.** We will strictly monitor and penalise coercion and the hiring of replacements for participants in a lawful strike.

## Maternity leave and child allowance

**We will guarantee maternity leave to every woman who worked before her pregnancy, regardless of the type of contract.** After six months of health insurance, we will guarantee compensation of at least two thirds of previous earnings, with a floor equal to the minimum wage, and after twelve months, full compensation. We will abolish the calculation that counts months without income as zeros in the eighteen-month average. **For fathers, we will introduce three months of leave after the birth of a child and a non-transferable share of parental leave during the child's first year.**

**We will increase the child allowance and extend it to more families.** We will abolish the limit on the number of children for whom the allowance can be claimed, and automatically link the entitlement to free school supplies, meals and transport. We will align the regulations on protection from violence with the Istanbul Convention, and explicitly recognise femicide in legislation so that the murders of women can be prevented more effectively.

## Social assistance

**We will raise financial social assistance above the absolute poverty line.** We will set the base at least 10% above that line, which according to the programme's data is around 17,000 dinars per household member, and every adult and secondary school pupil will be entitled to the full base, and every other child to at least half of it. We will link its adjustment equally to price increases and average wage growth.

We will remove the restrictive asset conditions that deny support to people who own more than half a hectare of land or movable property worth more than six times the base, around 600 euros. **We will bring all financial benefits together in one place**, while social work centres will use outreach work to find people entitled to support.

We will reform soup kitchens and develop food banks, vouchers and free school meals. **We will tackle energy poverty by protecting vulnerable households and investing in energy efficiency.** We will provide emergency financial support to households hit by a sudden loss of income, illness, violence, divorce or a natural disaster. **People with experience of poverty will take part in designing and evaluating all these measures.**

## Long-term care

**We will introduce a personal care budget from which users will choose and pay an assistant, a service or a family member themselves**, and we will regulate the engagement of carers through contracts with guaranteed labour rights. We will organise support through a joint social and health care programme.

Through a binding multi-year programme, we will begin **the gradual closure of certain residential institutions for persons with disabilities**, with priority given to support for community living for **5,000 to 6,000 residents of seventeen institutions.** We will develop supported housing and specialised foster care according to users' needs. Before leaving, every person will be given an individual housing and support plan. We will reorganise care homes for older people for the sake of residents' safety and quality of life, and bring private care homes under continuous supervision.

## Veterans' rights

War veterans, military war invalids and the families of fallen soldiers have been neglected for decades, and their rights reduced to a question of social assistance. **We will thoroughly reform the law on veterans and ensure that veterans' associations take part in drafting it and in the work of the bodies that implement it.**

**We will introduce a veterans' allowance in recognition of their contribution**, and this entitlement will be independent of the recipient's financial situation and of the allowance set according to the degree of disability. We will provide veterans with systematic recognition and treatment of post-traumatic stress disorder. **We will establish Vidovdan as War Veterans' Day**, dedicated to remembering the fallen and honouring living war veterans.

## Pensions – a dignified old age and support for those on the lowest pensions

**We will introduce two pension adjustments a year, so that pensions follow wages and prices in good time and better preserve purchasing power.** We will directly fund targeted support for pensioners in financial need, and give those on the lowest pensions an additional 5% on top of the regular adjustment.

We will top up survivors' pensions below the minimum to the relevant statutory minimum, and introduce **a targeted supplement of up to 15% of the deceased spouse's pension.**

**Through a social pension, we will provide basic security for people over 65 with insufficient years of service or no pension entitlement.** The full amount will be equal to the farmers' minimum pension.

For parents and carers who provide intensive care for a child or an adult family member dependent on the care of others, we will pay contributions from the budget and **recognise up to five years of service over their working life.**

## Fairer pension insurance for farmers and lower penalties

After an audit of their debts, **we will write off farmers' penalty interest and allow them to settle the principal through long-term repayment, by voluntarily buying back missing years of service, or through a partial write-off in exchange for giving up the corresponding unrecognised years of service.** We will provide small market-oriented farms with a subsidised contribution base, while low-income and non-commercial farms will be fully covered by the social pension.

**We will reduce the cuts for early retirement according to the length of effective service.** For pensioners with at least 42 years of such service, we will abolish the penalties once they reach 65, while for those with at least 40 but fewer than 42 years of service, we will halve the penalties after 65 and abolish them completely after 70. We will apply the rules gradually to both existing and future pensioners.
