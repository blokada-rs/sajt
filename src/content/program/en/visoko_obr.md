For years, universities and institutes in Serbia have worked with too little money, outdated equipment and management chosen by politics. Higher education increasingly resembles a market, and science is treated in the budget as a cost. Fixed-term contracts that last right up to election to the rank of professor teach academic staff to keep quiet. The recently introduced SPIRI system has taken away faculties' control over the money they earn themselves. **We are also building a programme to bring researchers back from abroad.**

## The university chooses its own leadership

**We will propose amendments to the law so that rectors, deans and institute directors are elected and dismissed only by the institution's own academic body.** Councils and management boards with external members, through which the Government today appoints between four and seven members of institutes' management boards, will no longer decide who runs an institution. We are introducing the same rule for the bodies that govern the system as a whole. **The academic community will gain a majority on the National Council for Higher Education and on the Management Board of the National Accreditation Body**, for which the ministry will no longer nominate candidates. Elections to academic ranks will be carried out by independent committees, at least 40% of whose members will come from other universities and from abroad, according to national minimum criteria, so that the same conditions apply to the same rank at all universities. **Anyone who meets targets set in advance, with an annual evaluation, will receive a permanent contract** instead of a new fixed-term contract until the next election.

## The State University of Novi Pazar and the University of Pristina, temporarily based in Kosovska Mitrovica

The University of Pristina, temporarily based in Kosovska Mitrovica, is under threat from outside: political pressure, security risks, legal uncertainty over its property and young people leaving. **Its survival is a question of preserving Serbia's educational and cultural presence in Kosovo and Metohija.** That is why we will give it **a clear legal status and stable funding**, a special programme of institutional and security protection with plans for continuing teaching in crisis conditions, support for keeping teaching staff and researchers, investment in buildings and laboratories, scholarships and other measures to attract and keep students, as well as international cooperation and mobility, with protection from all undue outside influence.

In recent years, the State University of Novi Pazar has declined in every respect, from the number of students and accredited programmes to the number of teaching staff. The figures are currently below the thresholds of sustainability, while research is in decline. The causes are internal: centralised and opaque management, a selective staffing policy, pressure on employees and suspicions of misuse of funds. It needs **interim governing bodies with staff participation and a new statute that returns decision-making to collective bodies**, full transparency of its work, a review of decisions on hiring and elections to academic ranks with the annulment of unlawful acts and the reinstatement of unfairly removed teaching staff, clear promotion criteria for its own staff, limits on engaging retired people, **an external financial audit, urgent re-accreditation of programmes and an independent determination of ethical responsibility.**

## Tuition fees and own revenue

**We will separate the own revenue of faculties and institutes from their budget funding, and the institution alone will decide on it.** We will consider reducing or possibly abolishing tuition fees for self-funded students as soon as possible, and instead of tuition fees we will give universities back property as a source of income. We will align enrolment with the needs of the labour market and the regions. **We are introducing institutional funding for science**, from which salaries, overheads and research work will be paid, allocated according to the number of researchers and professors. **We will exempt scientific equipment and consumables from VAT and customs duties, or reduce them.** In the government budget, we are introducing a Science Fund and an Investment Fund that will provide stable funding researchers can count on. **Ranking lists in funding calls will not be able to be changed by political decision.**

## Student living standards

Scholarships and loans today do not provide enough income to cover even a small part of the cost of studying, so we will introduce a combined model: **non-repayable scholarships for students from less well-off backgrounds, and for others, loans whose repayment begins only once they are employed.** The budget line for student living standards, which in 2026 amounts to 14.76 billion dinars and covers around 30% of the capacity needed, will get a larger increase, after which it will continue to grow steadily. **For students enrolled under affirmative measures because of disability, low socio-economic status, coming from an underdeveloped region or belonging to the Roma community, we are introducing a Support Centre at every university.**

## Quality of teaching and research

Research today is measured in points, which produces co-authorships with no real contribution and papers in journals that will publish anything for money. **Through new rulebooks and amendments to existing ones, we will give the lead author more weight than co-authors**, introduce a national list of recognised journals, and **ban publishing in predatory journals at the expense of state projects.** Besides the number of published papers, peer review, patents and public policy proposals will also be valued. **Moving between academic and professional (applied) studies will be possible again**, with clear national standards for progression through public procedures.

## Science and industry

**We will introduce industrial doctorates**, in which doctoral candidates work on a problem a company actually has.

## Returning researchers and international students

**We will offer researchers returning from abroad a national programme with a competitive salary, a start-up budget, one or two team members and laboratory space.** Recognition of a foreign diploma will take no more than 60 days and will be carried out digitally through the ENIC/NARIC network. International students today come to Serbia without any joint national presentation, and each institution presents itself separately. **All universities will present themselves together under a national stand**, and a fund for scholarships for the best international students, as well as welcome centres for international students and researchers, will be considered.
