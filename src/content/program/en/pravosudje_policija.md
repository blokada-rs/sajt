**The rule of law in Serbia today exists largely on paper.** The Constitution proclaims the independence of the judiciary and the autonomy of the prosecution service. Yet political influence over the selection and work of judges and prosecutors, a closed judiciary, years-long waits for a judgment and poor conditions for staff have left many citizens without trust in the judicial system. **The police, who ought to protect the constitutional order, at key moments act as an extended arm of the ruling party, oversee themselves and write rules the public cannot see**, while the electoral process is burdened by an unverified voter register, pressure on employees and institutions that fail to react.

These problems are not separate: **without an autonomous prosecution service there is no police accountability, without accountable police there are no fair elections, and without fair elections there is no government that will defend the independence of the judiciary**. That is why we are tackling them as a whole.

## An independent and effective judiciary

Although the Constitution guarantees the independence of the judiciary and the autonomy of the prosecution service, political influence over the selection and work of judges and prosecutors is still present. The composition of the High Judicial Council and the High Prosecutorial Council, in which some members are elected by the National Assembly as "prominent lawyers", leaves room for political structures to influence who will judge and who will prosecute. Senior officials and pro-government organisations publicly attack judges and professional judicial associations, and judicial reform is obstructed year after year. Citizens name political and media pressure, inefficiency and corruption as the main reasons for their distrust. **Our answer is therefore not just better-written rules, but rules whose application can be verified – who selects, by which criteria and benchmarks, who allocates cases and who decides about money.**

## An autonomous budget and professional court management

The judiciary cannot be independent and autonomous while the executive decides on its money. Today, the funds for the work of courts and prosecutor's offices sit in the budget of the Ministry of Justice, so court presidents depend on the ministry for every computer, every post filled and every building repair. **That is why we will develop an autonomous budget for the courts and public prosecutor's offices**: funds will be transferred from the Ministry of Justice to the High Judicial Council and the High Prosecutorial Council respectively, and court presidents and the councils will take part in drafting the budget. At the same time, we will review the funds that come from notaries public and how they are used. **The funding of the judiciary must not depend on court fees or on arbitrary government decisions.** Part of that budget must go towards improving the material position of judges and prosecutors, whose pay is considerably lower than in the region and the European Union, which increases the risk of corruption and of staff leaving.

Court presidents today spend most of their time on procurement, building maintenance and staffing tables instead of organising trials. **That is why we are keeping and expanding the institution of the court administrator**: it will become mandatory in smaller courts too, and a corresponding post will be introduced in public prosecutor's offices. The administrator takes over staff planning, public procurement, contracts, maintenance and building security, technical systems and budget execution, while court presidents and chief public prosecutors keep responsibility for organising professional work and representing their body. The administrator is selected by the High Judicial Council or the High Prosecutorial Council, after a public competition and a professional exam, for a limited term, with re-election possible only on the basis of a public and measurable performance assessment.

## Judges and prosecutors are chosen by the profession

The selection of judges and prosecutors today depends to a large extent on the councils' general acts, recommendations and impressions that no one can verify. **That is why selection becomes the exclusive competence of the High Judicial Council and the High Prosecutorial Council, through public competitions.** The Judicial Academy keeps training and professional development, which may also be provided by accredited faculties and other professional centres, while its trainees apply for competitions under the same conditions as assistants, trainees and other candidates. Separating training from selection reduces the room for centralised influence over who will judge.

The law will set uniform and public benchmarks for first-time selection. **Candidates' written work will be marked independently by randomly selected assessors, without information about the author**, and once the competition is over, the task, the scoring benchmarks and the marks will be published, so that every candidate can check the result and make effective use of a legal remedy. As a rule, the highest-ranked candidate is selected, and any proposal to select someone else must be justified by the published benchmarks.

**The promotion of judges and public prosecutors is based on the quality of the reasoning in their decisions, not on the outcome of proceedings**, and on performance adjusted to their actual workload and staff shortages. The rate of overturned decisions or acquittals may prompt a closer review, but never on its own be a reason for a negative assessment. They will be assessed by randomly selected assessors from other courts, outside the candidate's line of hierarchy, without the involvement of court presidents and chief public prosecutors. In this way, promotion depends less on the opinion of superiors and more on the quality of the work.

## Enough people to try cases on time

Serbian courts are among the most overburdened in Europe, and citizens wait for years for their disputes to be resolved. One of the causes is uneven professional support: the number of judicial assistants is set by the president of each court according to the available budget, so in some courts judges do all the work themselves. At the same time, courts lack court recorders and administrative staff, technical equipment and suitable premises, so even judges who want to work faster do not have the conditions to do so. Slow courts are not only a problem for the parties to proceedings: uncertainty over collecting claims and protecting property also holds back economic development. **The law will set a mandatory minimum ratio between the number of assistants and the number of judges or public prosecutors**, so that no court is left without the professional support it needs. Assistants will be selected through a single national competition, held regularly by the competent high council on the basis of a public staffing plan. They will work in department teams, not tied to a single judge, which reduces their personal dependence and spreads the work more evenly.

An assistant's salary will be tied to the base salary of a judge or prosecutor. An assistant works on a fixed-term contract for the first year, then moves to a permanent contract, and all current associates with two or more years of service automatically receive permanent contracts. The title is transferable on moving to another court, and after three years an assistant may apply for a higher level. **Traineeships in all legal professions will be paid as a rule**, because unpaid work by young lawyers drives the best out of the profession.

To relieve overburdened courts, **we are introducing mobile court teams**: judges and assistants from a less burdened court are temporarily sent to an overburdened court, with the judge's written consent and appropriate financial compensation. When a backlog results from a temporary increase in incoming cases or a judge's prolonged absence, the mobile team takes over a predetermined group of new cases. A mobile judge keeps their position in their home court and cannot receive cases outside the established plan. To preserve the principle of immediacy and the right to a natural judge, the team may not take over proceedings in which the original judge has already directly examined evidence. If an analysis shows that a staff shortage is permanent, a competition is announced or a vacant judicial post transferred at the same time, so that the mobile team remains strictly a transitional solution.

## A case cannot secretly change judges

Cases will be allocated evenly, according to inflow, complexity and duration, with weighting, so that a complex case does not count the same as a simple one. **A technical log will automatically record every change in case allocation** – the original assignment, reassignment, withdrawal and any manual intervention by an administrator – together with the legal basis for the change. A judge's or prosecutor's legal remedy against the withdrawal of a case will have suspensive effect: the case stays with the original office-holder until an independent panel rules on the lawfulness of that decision. Without suspensive effect, a new judge could take key steps and render a successful legal remedy meaningless.

## A judiciary open to the public

When courts and prosecutor's offices stay silent, the space is filled by speculation and tabloid pressure. **The decisions of all courts, with personal data protected, will become searchable in public databases**, so that citizens and lawyers can see how a court ruled on the same matter, which helps to harmonise case law.

We will introduce electronic case files and electronic filing and service, with data protection and accessibility for persons with disabilities. Data on the number of cases received, resolved and pending for a long time will be verified by an independent Judicial Data Audit Board, whose members may not manage the systems they audit. **Trials in corruption and organised crime cases will be broadcast live.**

## Accountability and protection from pressure

Today's disciplinary system is at once too weak and too elastic: offences are described so broadly that they can be applied selectively against those who are out of favour, while complaints against the powerful are often dismissed without reasons the public could verify. **The disciplinary offences of judges and prosecutors will be precisely defined**, so that it is known in advance which conduct leads to which proceedings, and the disciplinary bodies will gain greater functional independence. Decisions to dismiss complaints must give reasons, and statistics on proceedings will be public: how many complaints were filed, how many dismissed, how long proceedings lasted and how many ended in a sanction. **At the same time, we are introducing a register of cases of undue influence on judges and prosecutors, and protection for those who report them**, because attacks by senior officials on judicial associations and individual judges currently go unpunished.

## Accountability of officials and the fight against high-level corruption

Since the introduction of the multi-party system, Serbia has had a systemic problem with corruption, abuse of office and human rights violations by public officials, who are rarely sanctioned. The student movement addressed demands to institutions that under the Constitution ought to be independent and autonomous, but it turned out that almost every one of them had been captured. In some cases, the police refused to act on prosecutors' orders when people close to the government were involved. A change of government will not change prosecutor's offices, courts and administrative bodies overnight, and organised crime and high-level corruption are by their nature the hardest to prove.

**That is why we are establishing a prosecutorial police – a dedicated investigative service separated from the command structure of the Ministry of Internal Affairs.** The prosecutor handling a case directly directs the work of the police investigators on it, and their assignment, assessment and disciplinary responsibility are regulated within the prosecution service. This removes the current situation in which the prosecutor leads the investigation while the police answer to the minister.

Many human rights violations are not criminal offences, even though they have destroyed institutions and citizens' trust in the state for years. **That is why we are introducing a mechanism for holding officials accountable for abuse of office and human rights violations – lustration:** a procedure that establishes whether a person who holds, or is standing for, public office or public authority has violated human rights, and whether they should therefore be temporarily or permanently barred from exercising it. **Lustration is not a criminal procedure and does not serve revenge, but the protection of the public interest.** It is applied strictly on an individual basis, on precise statutory criteria, with a complete ban on collective punishment, and in line with the Constitution and the standards of the European Court of Human Rights.

**We will review, amend or repeal the laws adopted as special laws (Latin: lex specialis) for individual projects, which bypass regular procedures and open the door to corruption.**

The Law on Determining the Origin of Assets and Special Tax exists, but it is hardly applied: in four years only three proceedings have been completed and around two million euros collected, even though in early 2023 more than 13,500 people had seen their assets grow above the threshold of 150,000 euros over three years. **That is why we will increase the number of trained staff in the Tax Administration, improve its cooperation with the prosecution service**, amend the provisions that allow proceedings to be dragged out indefinitely, and require the Tax Administration to keep the public regularly informed about the progress of proceedings. Regular checks on the origin of assets will also be mandatory for police managers.

## Enforcement: a home is not a commodity

Bailiffs, once court employees, have become private entrepreneurs with public authority whose income depends on the amount of debt collected and the costs of proceedings. As a result, exercising public authority has become a business in which earnings grow with the debtor's costs, and the harshest measures, such as selling a home and eviction, are also the most profitable. Small utility debts multiply because of costs, while workers with final judgments in their favour often cannot enforce them because a bailiff is too expensive. A creditor can replace a bailiff who hesitates and hire one who will carry out the outcome regardless of legal obstacles, while third parties, often victims of fraud, have no effective protection. Enforcement does not stop even when other disputes are being conducted over the same property, when an action declaring enforcement inadmissible has been filed, or when an extraordinary legal remedy or an application to the European Court of Human Rights has been lodged, so in practice the bailiff prejudges court decisions. Properties are also sold through electronic auctions at prices far below market value, so the debtor is often left both without a flat and with a debt.

**That is why we are amending the Law on Enforcement and Security.** **We will extend the protection of the sole home, so that every home that meets a household's basic housing needs is exempt from enforcement.** Every action with irreversible consequences will be subject to judicial review, and **a legal remedy against the sale of a home or an eviction will have suspensive effect until the court rules.** Third parties will gain an effective objection with suspensive effect when they show that their right is probable. Costs must correspond to the actions actually needed, be proportionate to the debt and be visible in the electronic log of the proceedings. The state will guarantee compensation for damage caused by unlawful enforcement and recover it from the responsible bailiff, and repeated or serious violations of the right to a home will lead to a ban on practising. The Ministry of Justice inspectorate for bailiffs will be strengthened with staff, carrying out random checks on sales and costs and publishing quarterly public reports.

Through thorough reform, we will establish a new enforcement system based on humanity and fairness, under the direct responsibility and oversight of the state, without a return to the dysfunctional model of court bailiffs.

## An administration accountable to the citizen

A citizen who turns to a state body often waits months for an answer, and when they receive a decision and successfully appeal, the case is sent back to the same body for a new decision, sometimes more than once. Proceedings before the Administrative Court, the only one for the whole country, take a long time, a lawsuit as a rule does not stop enforcement, and the court usually just annuls the decision and returns the case to the body, which sometimes issues the same decision again. Citizens and businesses thus go years without a final answer, and they have to claim the damage caused by an unlawful decision in separate civil proceedings.

That is why we are introducing clear deadlines and effective protection against administrative silence, as well as an assurance act on which citizens can rely. **A second-instance administrative body will have to decide a well-founded appeal itself, instead of returning the case.** An appeal will suspend collection of the disputed part of a public levy, and if an unlawful decision is annulled, the citizen or business will be entitled to compensation for proven damage with interest.

We will also begin reforming the administrative judiciary by gradually introducing territorially distributed first-instance administrative courts, and **the current Administrative Court will become an appellate court, so that administrative disputes finally have two instances.** Over time, we will make it possible for courts to decide administrative matters themselves far more often, instead of returning a case to the body that has already got it wrong.

## Police in the service of citizens

The police should protect the constitutional order and every citizen equally. Recent years have shown how dangerous police without effective external oversight can be: law enforcement has on several occasions acted in the service of party interests, and citizens often do not know who gave the order, under which rule force was used and who is responsible for it. The Ministry of Internal Affairs today oversees itself and governs its work through acts that are not available to the public. **That is why we are giving the police back to the citizens – as a professional, accountable and demilitarised service, under clear rules and real oversight.**

## A new identity card and personal identification number

Without a reliable identification system, it is not possible to clear the voter register of "phantom voters", and the existing Unique Master Citizen Number reveals a person's date of birth, sex and region from the number itself, which is not in line with data protection standards.

**Since identity documents are mandatory, they will be issued free of charge.** Identity cards will be issued only with a chip and to the latest anti-counterfeiting standards, and all existing cards will be replaced free of charge within a transition period.

**We will replace the Unique Master Citizen Number (JMBG) with a Personal Identification Number (LIB)**, in line with the Law on Personal Data Protection and EU rules. The new identity card will be issued with a LIB, and registration for a LIB will become the basis of a new, reliable voter register. No citizen will lose the right to vote because they have not yet replaced their document: until it is replaced, they remain registered and vote with a valid identity card or passport.

## Police oversight and accountability

Police who oversee themselves cannot be accountable. **That is why we will professionalise the Internal Control Sector, completely separate it from political influence, remove it from the Ministry of Internal Affairs and attach it to the Ministry of Justice.** Disciplinary proceedings for abuse of authority will be swift, effective and public.

**Police officers will be given body cameras and cameras in official vehicles.** They protect citizens from excessive use of force and police officers from unfounded complaints. **The Ministry of Internal Affairs' contracts and tenders will be publicly available**, and the origin of police managers' assets, especially at the strategic management level, will be checked regularly. The job classification of managerial posts will be public, and the names and CVs of senior and strategic-level managers will be available on the ministry's website, modelled on the "Who's Who" of the Serbian Armed Forces.

## The police as a service to citizens

**The police will work as a service to citizens, focused on building trust, not on repression.** They will report to the public regularly and clearly on their work and results. We will develop community policing, in which police officers work actively with citizens and local government, and local safety councils will become the place where problems that threaten safety in the local community are solved together.

Police education will focus on human rights, communication with citizens and non-violent conflict resolution. Police officers will continually improve their knowledge, especially in the areas of cybercrime, human rights and international standards. We will improve the selection of candidates and abolish the possibility of having the psychological test reviewed at the Basic Police Training Centre, and we will provide professional support to police officers exposed to stress and to the risk of using excessive force.

## Demilitarisation of the police

The Decree on the Special and Specialised Police Units has allowed the police to use heavier means of coercion and equipment that looks more military than police. Through amendments to the Law on Police, the Law on the Serbian Armed Forces and that decree, **we will ensure that police equipment and weapons serve the protection of public safety, not warfare.** The competence and purpose of police intervention units will be regulated by a publicly available regulation.

## A new organisation

**We will separate the police as a professional service from the ministry as a political body.** Police officers on special duties will become regular civil servants, and the posts of authorised officers outside the Police Directorate will be abolished. By amending the Rulebook on Job Classification, we will reduce the size of the Ministry of Internal Affairs' headquarters in favour of the regional police directorates, bringing the police closer to citizens, and abolish assistant managers at all levels as unnecessary positions.

The ministry currently issues a large number of autonomous acts that do not fall into any type of regulation permitted by law and often contradict one another. We will bring these "quasi-regulations" into line with the Law on State Administration or remove them, so that citizens and police officers alike can rely on the same publicly available rule.

## Changing the electoral conditions

**Trust in the electoral process is restored by removing the factors that can affect the final election result: an unverified voter register, uncontrolled voting outside polling stations and electoral commission software that no one can inspect.**

**We will establish an independent State Electoral Commission as a permanent institution with its own administration, budget and professional capacity.** An institution set up in this way would have the human and material resources to work on improving the quality of elections outside the electoral process as well: through analysis, education, training, and better electoral conditions and regulations. In parallel, we will carry out a comprehensive overhaul of the voter register and modernise the ways in which the right to vote can be exercised. The Commission's permanent members will be chosen from among experts in the electoral process and electoral legislation – for longer terms, with mechanisms that ensure institutional independence.

**We will set up a modern electoral administration portal that allows election results from polling stations to be published in real time, public access to data in machine-readable format** and monitoring of all stages of the electoral process by the interested public. We will improve standards and procedures to strengthen the secrecy of the vote, the safekeeping of election material and access for domestic and international election observers, and we will make it easier to file complaints about electoral irregularities, and digitalise the process.

**We will carry out a confidential electronic and field audit of the Unified Voter Register and establish mechanisms for its continuous oversight**, with the possibility of controlled access by independent bodies, parliamentary groups and representatives of election observers, while maintaining standards for the protection of citizens' data. We will also regulate the status of inactive voters, including voters who have long since stopped living in the Republic of Serbia, with a guarantee that no citizen who meets the requirements can be arbitrarily deprived of the right to vote.

**We will make registration for voting abroad easier and centralised through an electronic system**, so that it no longer depends on the arbitrary decision of a diplomatic mission. We will prevent abuse of voting outside the polling station by strictly defining the justified reasons for it, requiring earlier registration, and moving the mechanism out of the hands of political actors and into the competence of the electoral administration, while keeping voting accessible for people who, because of their health or disability, objectively cannot get to a polling station.

**We will establish a dedicated mechanism for the safe and confidential reporting of pressure, intimidation, coercion and vote-buying**, with whistleblower protection and an obligation on the competent institutions to act on reports within short deadlines. We will provide special protection for employees of public institutions and public enterprises who report political pressure. We will tighten oversight and sanctions for unlawful conduct and the misuse of personal data for political purposes, including data held by mobile operators, healthcare institutions and other institutions or businesses.

**We will establish effective mechanisms to monitor equal media access for electoral contestants and the objectivity of reporting during the election period**, with clear and swift sanctions for breaches of the rules, and an obligation for the public service broadcaster to present electoral contestants daily and equally throughout the election campaign. At the same time, we will provide special mechanisms to protect journalists from pressure, threats and obstruction of their work during the election campaign.

**We will improve the system for reporting and overseeing political parties' income and expenditure, so that the public can obtain data on funding sources and major campaign costs in good time, not only after the elections.** We will separate public office from the election campaign by limiting the ability of public officials to use official activities, public resources, institutional infrastructure and access to the media for campaign purposes. **We will ban extraordinary one-off cash payments in an election year**, to prevent the management of public funds from being misused for political purposes.

By amending the Law on the Election of Members of Parliament, **we will reform the electoral system and introduce a mixed parallel electoral system**: one part elected in single-member constituencies of roughly equal numbers of voters (which, where possible, would follow the structure of the current local self-government units), and the other through proportional electoral lists, while considering giving voters a say in the preferential selection of individual candidates on the electoral lists themselves. Candidates in single-member constituencies would have to meet a residence requirement in the constituency where they are standing, because this ensures a direct link between candidates and the citizens they are meant to represent.

**We will introduce the option of giving signatures in support of electoral lists through the e-Government system**, as well as an online check that lets citizens see whether they have supported an electoral list, as currently exists for checking registration in the voter register. To prevent manipulation through the speed of signature collection, we will introduce a draw for the order of places on the ballot, as already exists in presidential elections.

**We will ban any information on the ballot other than the official name of the political party (or parties) or group of citizens submitting the list**, and in single-member constituencies we will ban any information on the ballot other than the personal details of the nominated candidate.
