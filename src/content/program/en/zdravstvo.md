The people of the Republic of Serbia have long faced unequal access to healthcare and unequal quality of care, difficult and delayed access to services, and a growing sense of being "left to fend for themselves". Serbia has one of the oldest populations in Europe. More than 23% of the population is over 65, while life expectancy at birth is around 76 years, roughly five years below the European Union average. This situation results from imbalances in the organisation and allocation of funds across all levels of healthcare, poor and opaque management of institutions and staff, neglected dental and pharmacy services, and the systematically forgotten protection of mental health. **Our overall goal is to establish a resilient, inclusive, modern and technologically advanced healthcare system that meets the needs of everyone living in Serbia.** The healthcare system should meet patients' needs, but it should also improve working conditions and give healthcare workers the opportunity for personal and professional development.

## Proper organisation of healthcare and a doctor in every village

Our healthcare system is upside down: clinical centres and institutes are burdened with too many patients, too many services and even surplus staff, while health centres and general hospitals are neglected and cannot perform their key role – the role of the system's foundation. Prevention is neglected, so we treat people only once they are seriously ill, instead of preventing illness in time. The emergency medical service does not guarantee everyone the same quality of service, which undermines the principle of the constitutionally guaranteed right to equal healthcare.

**Our plan is to restore strength and dignity to health centres and family doctors**, so that patients can have most tests and diagnostic procedures for chronic illnesses done right in their own neighbourhood. **We will connect all doctors through a single information system**, so that information moves without patients being sent needlessly from door to door carrying hundreds of papers. The equipment and accessibility of the emergency medical service are crucial for the lives of fellow citizens in acute danger. **How quickly an ambulance arrives will no longer depend on whether you live in the city centre or in the most remote village, because for us every life is worth the same.**

In the future, the state will stand by families when things are hardest for them, by taking responsibility for organised **palliative care for everyone who needs it.** We will strengthen the community nursing service, which will find its place closer to your home. We will restore the importance of school clinics and redirect money to health education and preventive and screening programmes for everyone. **We will introduce full digital oversight of planned interventions and diagnostic procedures, in which the only criteria are the needs of the most seriously ill patients.** This will lead to visible changes and shorter waiting lists.

## Healthcare management

Since 2015, healthcare has been run without a long-term plan or a national strategy, and party appointees are put at the head of institutions on a so-called "acting" basis, which closes the door to accountability and opens the door to abuse of office and corruption. **Obedience must not come before expertise.** As a result, patients and healthcare workers have lost trust in the system. Laws are applied loosely, while no one cares how well these managers have actually done their job.

That is why **we will adopt a ten-year healthcare development strategy**, so that the direction and order of the system are finally clear, regardless of who is in power. **We will abolish permanent "acting" directors and introduce public competitions**, so that the best among us, professionals by vocation and respected by their colleagues, take charge of institutions. Running institutions will not be a matter of dividing up the "spoils", but a responsibility requiring continuous training in economics, work organisation, the protection of patients' and employees' rights, and communication skills.

We will also **introduce independent and strict oversight of the application of all health laws, rulebooks and regulations**, in order to root out corruption and incompetence. **Everyone will be able to see how money is spent and how institutions work**, restoring respect and trust among patients and healthcare workers alike.

## People are the heart of the healthcare system

The healthcare workers we train have been leaving to work abroad for years. Our system is ageing; after their training, young healthcare workers have no security and do not know when they will get a job and a specialisation, so they are forced to seek their fortune outside Serbia, while many rural areas are left without medical staff.

To stop this, **we will introduce a clear system of employment immediately after graduation.** A National Reference Centre for Healthcare Workforce Management will direct staff to where they are needed, according to a national workforce plan. **Young doctors will get their first job, paid internships and specialisations through regular public competitions, according to the population's actual needs.** In cooperation with local governments, we will provide special financial, tax and housing incentives for those who decide to work in smaller and more remote areas. We will have a national workforce plan for each of the coming years.

We will give medical staff and associates who graduated from colleges and universities greater powers and autonomy through specific job roles, which will relieve doctors and make healthcare more accessible to patients, and **we will finally give modern, well-trained staff recognition, better working conditions and a reason to stay in their own country.**

## A time of accountability is coming

Of the six billion euros the healthcare system spends each year, citizens pay almost two billion euros out of their own pockets on top of compulsory health insurance, or around 1,200 euros for a family of four. Because the system is inefficient and waiting lists are long, the same money produces completely different results in different cities.

To change this, **we will introduce strict and public quality control of the work of every healthcare institution. Everyone will be rewarded according to measurable and concrete performance.** We will connect all healthcare institutions into **a single, safe and secure information system**, so that your medical record and test results follow you wherever you go, without duplicated tests or the hundreds of papers you carry with you, without wasted time and without unnecessary spending.

**All healthcare institutions will work as one team in which expertise and experience are available to everyone.** We are changing the way public money is spent, and we will no longer allow the health budget to be drained through inflated public procurement.

## Healthy teeth must not be a luxury

Dental health is part of overall health, but for years dentistry in Serbia has been left to people's own budgets rather than compulsory health insurance. Today, with their health insurance card, patients are entitled to little more than emergency interventions where these are available, while a full smile has become a luxury for most.

**Our goal is to bring dentistry back into health centres and expand the packages of services covered by compulsory health insurance.** In every municipality, we will provide accessible emergency dental care every day, so that in the middle of the night or at the weekend you do not have to wonder whether there is anywhere to go with a painful tooth. We will make it possible for dental treatment to be carried out not only in health centres but also in all private practices that sign a contract with the RFZO. In this way, **we will bring "a dentist on your health insurance card" to every municipality.** We will introduce clear quality standards for all services and adopt the long-awaited Law on Dental Medicine.

## Medicine is not a commodity and pharmacies are not ordinary shops

**Pharmacies should not open only where there is profit, but where people need them.** In many pharmacies there is no qualified pharmacist on some shifts at all, so instead of professional advice, patients get only a sales service.

Our plan is to **introduce an obligation to have publicly owned pharmacies**, while drawing up a new pharmacy plan that subsidises pharmacies in underdeveloped areas. We will check minimum accessibility standards by region, so that pharmaceutical care does not depend on where a person lives. We are extending compulsory insurance cover to new therapies, medical devices and services that today have to be paid for in full.

We are fighting shortages through an early warning system with a list of critical medicines and mandatory minimum stocks. **We are introducing a single price for each medicine, the same in every pharmacy, and an obligation to contract with the Republic Health Insurance Fund**, which gives small pharmacies the same conditions as large chains. We are introducing electronic checks to confirm that a qualified pharmacist is present on every shift when every medicine is dispensed. This obligation exists today only on paper, and we will enforce it. **The interest of public health must come before the interest of the market**, and a pharmacy must be a place where people get medicine when they need it, where they need it and at a price they can afford.

## No one should be left alone with the weight on their soul

The existing system cannot adequately meet citizens' needs in the field of mental health. The system is still largely institutional, stigma is still widespread, so help is often sought too late, and violations of users' human rights and dignity are common.

**Our main goal is to establish an interdisciplinary system of community mental health care based precisely on respect for human rights and dignity.** This includes improving legislation, developing a network of accessible services, and improving the position and increasing the number of professionals. Instead of closed institutions, **we will build a network of mental health centres accessible to everyone and open a National Centre for Trauma and Crisis Situations.**

Key steps also include raising mental health literacy to reduce stigma. We will talk and teach about it, from the classroom to documentary programmes on television. **Emotional pain is not weakness, but a human call for more understanding, care and love.** That is why treatment and recovery are not only the responsibility of the individual, but of the whole of society.

## Instead of an ending – a beginning

For years we were told that long queues and waiting were our reality, and that it is the same in many more developed countries. It is time to show that things can and must be different.

**We will build a healthcare system in which the doctor knows their patient. A system in which the patient trusts their doctor. A system in which emergency help arrives just as quickly in every village, and in which no one has to choose between buying medicine and buying food.**

We will build a system in which no one feels abandoned any more. **We will give healthcare workers back their profession, patients their security, and all the citizens of our country their dignity.**
