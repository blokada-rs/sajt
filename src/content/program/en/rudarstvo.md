# Mining and geology

For years, mining in Serbia has operated by rules that cannot be checked. On 23 April 2026, the National Assembly adopted the Strategy for the Management of Mineral and Other Geological Resources, but the action plan that the Strategy itself promised within ninety days has not been published, so not a single step stands between the 2022 baseline values and the 2040 targets. The institutions for exploration, exploitation and supervision exist, but they have neither the people nor the equipment to do their job. **Our goal is for it to be known what we have underground, what it is worth, who measured it and how much of it stays in Serbia**, and for **most of the mineral raw materials extracted to be processed in Serbia, including the final stage of processing.**

## Rules that tell us what we have

The 2015 Law on Mining requires resources and reserves to be classified according to the current version of the international PERC standard, and introduces a competent person who is personally responsible for the report. The rulebook that was supposed to implement this was never adopted, so rulebooks from 1979 are still applied today. They were published in the "Official Gazette of the SFRY" (former Yugoslavia), they place obligations on an "organisation of associated labour", and their list of raw materials includes neither lithium, nor boron, nor rare earths. **The number of competent person licences issued is zero, and the public register of these persons that the law provides for does not exist.**

We will adopt a rulebook on the classification and categorisation of resources and reserves in line with the international standard, with a table converting the existing categories, and stop applying the 1979 rulebooks. **We will establish a public register of competent persons and licences, so that for every figure on reserves there is a named, licensed person who answers for it.** Until that exists, no figure on reserves is anyone's responsibility.

## The fee for the use of public goods

What the public calls "mining rent" does not exist under that name in our laws. There is a fee for the use of mineral resources and reserves, set out in the Law on Fees for the Use of Public Goods, and its name says what matters: **it is a price for using something that is state property, not a tax on someone's profit.**

Today, the fee for metallic raw materials is five per cent of the revenue the payer declares itself, and the same payer also determines the quantity, the grade and the amount it owes. There is no fee based on the area of the exploitation field, no earmarked share for the local community, and how much has been collected per raw material, mine and municipality has not been published, even though by law forty per cent belongs to the municipality where the mining takes place.

**We will set the fee out in four elements.** We will separate the base from declared revenue and tie it to the published exchange quotation, with standardised smelting, refining and transport costs. **We will make the rate progressive according to the average price in the accounting period**, so that the community gets a larger share in years of high prices and a smaller one in years of low prices. We will introduce a component paid per area of the exploitation field, rising over time for rights held without work being carried out. We will introduce an earmarked component for repairing damage in the local community. And **we will publish an annual overview of the fees collected per raw material, mine and municipality**, so that every mining municipality can see its money.

## Processing in Serbia

The law in force already provides a relief for metallurgical processing, but it does not require that processing to take place in Serbia: concentrate can be smelted abroad and the relief still applies. At the same time, the only tax relief for large investments is being abolished, while a new one, linked to processing, has not been introduced.

**We will tie the relief to the level of processing carried out in Serbia and grant it only for a level higher than what is already done in the country**, so that the incentive is not a reward for something that happens anyway. It will be proven through the production records of the domestic plant and the customs declaration, in proportion to the quantity actually processed in the country. Since it involves forgoing public revenue, **the scheme will be notified to the Commission for State Aid Control before it is applied.**

## Granting rights and publishing contracts

Today, rights are granted on a first-come, first-served basis, with applications recorded to the hour and minute. The law provides for a public tender, but it is not applied, because its application has been postponed until accession to the European Union. **We will put tenders into practice without waiting for accession**, by amending that provision, and extend them from oil and gas to strategic mineral raw materials. We will also use the existing option of auctions and concessions over the reserve certificates the Republic already holds. We will not abolish the explorer's priority without compensation: **whoever has invested in exploration will receive compensation for the funds invested or a right of first refusal under publicly announced conditions.**

**All exploration and exploitation contracts will be published.** This is not a special rule for mining but the application of the general rule under which every contract the state signs becomes public, and **a contract that has not been published has no legal effect.** Publication comes first; an assessment of lawfulness and appropriateness can follow only on the basis of the published text, not before it.

## Inspection and institutions

**There are seven mining and geological inspectors, thirteen posts in the job classification, and seven hundred and sixty-eight sites under supervision.** The Geological Survey of Serbia has thirteen experts against a target of one hundred and sixty-five and spends more than three quarters of its budget on salaries.

**We will fill the posts in the job classification and increase the number of inspectors in line with the number of sites**, publish the supervision plan and the inspection findings, and require sampling and measurement at the mine exit, with a control sample kept. Without this, even the best fee formula is worthless, because quantity and grade are still determined by the one who pays. **We will give the Geological Survey back its staff, its equipment and its competence for applied research.** We will establish **a mining register: a public and free register of rights holders, administrative acts, contracts, declared quantities and inspection findings.**

## Workers

**Workers who, through a state-owned company, carry out work at another state enterprise will be given the same rights as comparable employees.** Pro TENT and Elektroprivreda Srbije have the same owner, the Republic of Serbia, so this can be done within the existing law, through founder's rights: the Labour Law makes the founder a signatory to the collective agreement at a capital company founded by the Republic. In parallel, we will ask the labour inspectorate to establish whether this work is carried out under the supervision and direction of the enterprise where it is done, because if it is, the right to equal working conditions has been in the law since 2020. **We will first establish and publish the difference in basic pay for comparable posts, and only then close it.**

We will also regulate work in mines during temporarily unprofitable periods, so that instead of termination of employment, **a temporary suspension of works is applied, with the mine maintained, workers retrained and jobs kept.** The law in force has no basis for this: all the grounds for temporary suspension in it are technical or force majeure, and the obligation to adopt a programme for dealing with redundant employees exists only for public enterprises.

## The local community and the land above deposits

**We will give local residents mechanisms to take part in decision-making, with mandatory consultation before a decision, not after it.** The earmarked part of the fee will go to the remediation and reclamation of mine waste dumps and tailings ponds, and the remediated sites and the funds spent will be made public.

**Above proven deposits outside populated areas, we will introduce a regime of spatial protection** modelled on the Slovenian approach, in which the public interest is not general but is established for a specific raw material and a specific area, with consent required for every new spatial planning act in that area. **The more land is protected in advance, in planning documents, the less resettlement, expropriation and demolition of infrastructure there will be later.**
