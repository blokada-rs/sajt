**No centre of power, whether the government, a media owner or a distributor, may be allowed to control public information.** Within the media system, we are keeping what works, while dealing with the issues that have remained inadequately or unclearly regulated: covert control of the media, the use of public funds to reward those considered "suitable", and the power of distributors to decide which media reach a wider audience. Every rule presented here has passed the same test. A rule is valid only if it can be used effectively by a journalist ordered to publish a falsehood, a citizen targeted by a false report, a local newsroom denied public funds by the authorities, or a television channel removed overnight by a distributor. For that reason, **every rule specifies a competent supervisory body, a timeframe and a mechanism for monitoring implementation.** We will adopt the legislative package within two years, with a thorough and regular review of how it works until the fourth year.

## Reality shows and child protection

**On television stations with free-to-air national frequencies, we are limiting reality shows to a maximum of 45 minutes a day, only after 11 pm, with no live broadcasts.** Reruns, "specials" and "breaking live links" fall under the same limits. To protect participants and viewers, we require **the free and informed consent of adult participants**, and we oblige broadcasters to cut programmes immediately in the event of violence, threats or hate speech. As a result, round-the-clock reality shows will remain available to viewers only by subscription, with age restrictions and parental controls.

## Telekom and cable operators

**A state-controlled operator may not own a television station, radio station or portal that produces news content**, including ownership through subsidiaries and intermediaries. With newsrooms separated from operators, their staff do not transfer to Telekom. **Television stations with general and news programming gain the right to mandatory distribution by every operator** (a must-carry system), while broadcasters become obliged to offer their signal to operators (a must-offer system).

## RTS and REM

**We will launch the legally prescribed procedures to dismiss the Director General and the Management Board of RTS.** While the procedures are under way, RTS will be run by a management with a limited mandate, and a new Management Board will be chosen through a public competition. **We will introduce public and verifiable benchmarks for selecting the management and editors of RTS and RTV.** By publishing CVs and potential conflicts of interest, expertise becomes the selection benchmark, instead of the current system of political affiliation. We are calling for an ombudsperson to be assigned to the public service broadcasters and for public annual performance assessments, and we plan to fund RTS and RTV according to statutory formulas, under a multi-year plan. **A review of the finances of RTS and RTV will be completed within the first six months.**

**The ninth seat on the REM Council will be filled as soon as possible, an anti-deadlock mechanism will be introduced immediately afterwards, and within the first six months the REM will be working at full capacity.** We will make fines for breaking the law proportionate to a media outlet's revenue, including for large broadcasters. The ultimate sanctions are the suspension of individual programmes and, where necessary, the revocation of licences.

## Public money in the media

**Every state advertising contract will be published with the contracting authority, the amount, the criteria and the beneficial owners.** Its price will be compared with the market price, and the money will be traced to its final recipient, including public relations agencies and sponsorships. The same advertising rules will apply to all users of public funds. The relevant government structures will publish the beneficial owners, funders and all public money in the media. **Project co-funding will support content the market cannot sustain**, through committees chosen for their independence and expertise. **Uniform rules on advertising and co-funding will be in force within six months.**

## Ownership, origin of capital and frequencies

**A public register of beneficial owners will show the ultimate owner of every media outlet, their ownership share, related parties, and other media owned by the same person or legal entity.** Concealing ownership through intermediaries will be a serious offence. If the beneficial owner is not disclosed, the outlet will lose access to public funds and be fined. Media will be required to disclose significant sources of funding, loans and forms of dependence.

**A broadcasting frequency is a limited right to use a public resource, not a political reward.** Immediately on taking office, we will launch a new public competition for the available national licences and for those that have expired, and we will also hold procedures for local stations. Existing licences remain valid until they expire, unless a serious breach of the law is established. **New licences will be awarded on the basis of public criteria, regardless of any political affiliation.**

## Journalists

**The state will respond urgently to attacks on journalists, threats against them and the destruction of their equipment, regardless of the editorial policy of their media outlet.** Within the first six months, we will set up a register of such attacks, and as soon as possible we will introduce a legal definition of an organised media witch hunt.

**A dedicated set of measures will provide protection against strategic lawsuits against public participation (SLAPPs)**, so that courts will dismiss manifestly unfounded cases at an early stage, before the cost and length of the proceedings become a form of punishment in themselves. Journalists will be able to invoke a ""conscience clause"", with protection under labour law, guaranteeing that **they cannot be sanctioned for refusing to publish information they know to be false.**
