**The state of the environment in Serbia is among the worst in Europe:** polluted air raises mortality, untreated wastewater flows into rivers, water supply systems do not provide enough clean and safe water, waste ends up in illegal dumps that often catch fire, and biodiversity and ecosystems are inadequately protected, critically endangered or even destroyed. Climate change is making the already alarming state of the environment worse. The causes are poor management, institutions captured by the party, investor-led urban planning and tolerance of violations.

**We protect water, air, soil and biodiversity as the most important public interest.** We base the system on personal and institutional responsibility, alignment of regulations with European standards, independent impact assessments, digital monitoring and public access to data under the Aarhus Convention, automatic responses by the competent authorities when limits are exceeded, consistent penalties, and the involvement of science, education and citizens.

## Expert institutions, accountability and funding

We are **depoliticising and professionalising the competent institutions**, namely the ministry, the Environmental Protection Agency, the Institute for Nature Conservation and the competent Secretariats, by selecting staff through competitions based on expertise and experience, with a review of their past work. By reforming the Environmental Protection Agency and the Institute for Nature Conservation, we are strengthening inspection powers, independence and measurement systems. We are linking institutions digitally and preparing the reorganisation of public utility companies for water, waste and nature. We are fully applying the existing **"polluter pays and is liable" and "user pays and is liable"** principles. Managers of protected areas will be held liable when a failure to prevent or mitigate damage is proven. We will start mapping polluters, measuring pollution and reviewing the work of inspectorates. We are strengthening national and local inspectorates with staff, equipment and training, and introducing training for the judiciary and police on environmental offences. **We are re-establishing an independent Environmental Protection Fund**, with a public plan for spending the revenue from fines, charges and fees.

## Prevention that really protects

Prevention mechanisms become the basis for decision-making from day one: **strategic assessments, impact assessments and feasibility assessments will be carried out properly and substantively, with a ban on starting works without consent.** Decisions on environmental impact assessment studies must come before the building permit. Assessment studies for projects with significant negative impacts will be re-examined by independent committees, and we are abolishing the "ministry opinion" mechanism used to bypass impact assessments. Instead of a fine, **works started without the necessary consent will be halted until the study is approved or a decision is made that no assessment is needed.** More than 150 industrial plants operate without an integrated permit, even though the deadline expired on 31 December 2024, which is why measures to obtain permits and tougher penalties for not having one are being ordered urgently. **We are ending the practice of splitting projects and of adopting special laws that abolish or postpone assessments.** We are introducing an e-portal for impact assessments and integrated permits, licensed assessors and analysis of cumulative effects.

## Laws and strategies that are applied

We will introduce a temporary suspension of the legal provisions that allow assessments to be bypassed, public participation to be limited and protected areas to be privatised, until the laws are amended and regulations aligned. We are applying the Law on the Liability of Legal Entities for Criminal Offences and tightening existing penalties, especially for endangering health. We are aligning sectoral laws, such as the Law on Planning and Construction, with the environmental protection laws. **The draft strategy for the management of mineral resources until 2040, the Spatial Plan of the Republic of Serbia currently in force and the Development Plans will be drawn up again with updated data**, with the participation of experts and the wider public, and with the public interest put first.

## Mining, infrastructure and industrial projects

**Projects that already pose a direct risk are treated as a priority.** For the lithium and boron mining project in Jadar, we will apply protective measures as a priority, including bans and the halting of projects, revocation of permits and sanctions for those responsible, in line with the legal irregularities and risks that have been established. We are aligning the solutions for mining projects with the Mining programme area, and **the state will not approve any project with an unacceptable risk to the environment and local communities.** We will apply the same rules of protection, oversight and accountability to Zijin in Bor, the steelworks in Smederevo, the mines and ash dumps at Kolubara and Kostolac, and the planned Čoka Rakita gold mine in Homolje, with no one given privileged treatment. For the EXPO 2027 complex, which is being built in the protection zone of the Sava water source, we demand a full impact assessment, and **we are abolishing the exceptions that allow it to be bypassed, split up or postponed until after the building permit is issued.** We are protecting water sources from illegal construction and pollution on the banks of Ćelije, Bovan and Gruža, and we are aligning the measures to stop small hydropower projects with the Energy programme area.

## Water is a public good

**We are declaring water a strategic public good and a human right, and banning its privatisation.** Commercial bottling of water will be permitted only in limited quantities and for a limited period, with an appropriate concession contract and fees. We are bringing water management together under the competent ministry or a coordinating body. **Water sources will get strict protection from pollution and illegal construction.** Instead of costly dam and reservoir projects, priority will go to expanding existing sources, artificial recharge and reserving space for future sources in spatial plans. **We are reducing losses, which average close to 40% of the water abstracted, through network renewal, maintenance, inter-municipal cooperation and a uniform tariff policy.** We are increasing treatment capacity, which covers around 15% of discharged municipal wastewater, in phases, according to priorities. We are requiring industry to treat its wastewater through integrated permits.

## Air becomes a public health priority

**We are combining air quality and emissions monitoring into a single national system and publishing the data in real time.** In hotspots such as Bor and Smederevo, monitoring stations will trigger an alarm and a demand for the polluter to stop operating. Thermal power plants and large industrial polluters have primary responsibility for controlling pollution, which is why we are continuing desulphurisation at TENT and Kostolac, setting fines for exceedances on the basis of actual, measured emissions, and making the use of best available techniques a key condition of integrated permits. **We will measure success by the reduction in deaths from pollution, using the World Health Organization's methodology.** We will gradually reduce electricity generation from burning lignite and other fossil fuels over the long term, in line with the Energy area. We are banning the sale of unclassified coal to private individuals, and providing socially vulnerable households with help through thermal renovation, replacement of stoves and installation of heat pumps, through socially fair subsidies, as part of the energy efficiency programme.

## Waste and land are managed responsibly

Around 60% of municipal waste ends up in 127 non-sanitary landfills and more than 3,000 illegal dumps. **We are closing and remediating the most critical ones**, and of the 26 planned centres we are completing those already started and launching the next ones by priority. We are immediately reviewing the results and costs of "Clean Serbia", with the long-term goal of collecting all municipal waste. **We are introducing a ban on imports of hazardous waste, without exception, and digital control of transit and exports.** We are updating the register of mining, industrial and construction waste and remediating the riskiest tailings ponds with money from the funds. Only around 5% of municipal waste is currently recycled, which is why we are gradually introducing a deposit return system for packaging, a national recycling programme and monitoring at municipal waste landfills.

## Nature and biodiversity are protected

**We are adopting the missing regulations on species, habitats, the ecological network and invasive species**, and preventing the privatisation of the public enterprises that look after nature. We are designating the areas whose protection is under way, including the future Beljanica–Kučaj National Park, completing the review of the Emerald Network and speeding up the establishment of the Natura 2000 network, while introducing freshwater protected areas. We are requiring managers to establish a baseline and monitor biodiversity. An independent body will assess their work, and local residents will be given priority in hiring and a fund for projects of public interest. **We are introducing a portal or app for reporting pollution and the destruction of nature.**

## Ready for climate change

Serbia has committed to **reducing greenhouse gas emissions by 40% by 2035 compared with 1990.** Through transparent subsidies, we are encouraging the installation of solar panels in households, small businesses and public institutions, insulation and heat pumps. We are planning solar and wind farms outside forests and protected areas, in dialogue with local communities, while hydropower plants are subject to a strict impact assessment. **Joining the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) is a strategic commitment**, in line with the Energy and Economy areas. We are building climate risks into regulations and plans, establishing a Climate Change Adaptation Fund, and improving protection against floods and droughts, as well as measures to protect public health during extreme heat.

## Education, public participation and the media

**Environmental education will be systematically introduced at all levels of formal education, from pre-school to university.** As a priority, broad and informed public involvement in planning and decision-making will be ensured, and the engagement and role of individuals and local communities in protecting the environment will be strengthened. Attention will be paid to **combating the organised spread of disinformation and to expanding science and information programming** on these topics.
