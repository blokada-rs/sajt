The rights of minorities in Serbia today exist only on paper. Bilingualism in municipalities is a formality, because forms, e-services and notice boards are only in Serbian, and schools teaching in minority languages operate with a shortage of teachers and with textbooks that arrive late. Culture survives on the enthusiasm of individuals through short-term funding calls, and the media have neither minimum quotas nor multi-year funding. There are few jobs in which knowing a minority language is an advantage, so young people leave.

## Official use of languages and schools

The Law on the Official Use of Languages and Scripts will get **a minimum standard of bilingualism**: forms, stamps, e-services, minutes and records in the minority language, mandatory for every municipality in which that language is in official use. **Romani will be introduced into official use in the bodies of the Republic.**

We will set out in law that **a complete set of textbooks in the minority language must be provided before the start of each school year**, under an annual staffing and textbook plan signed by the ministries and the national councils, with named responsibility. **The economic conditions will be secured for the education of Roma pupils and students from deprived backgrounds.** The segregation of Roma children into separate schools and classes will be prevented **by including them in mainstream education.**

## Minority culture and media

**Each of the 18 national minorities, from Albanians to Gorani, will get an institute for culture**, modelled on the five Vojvodina institutes. Work will be done on funding theatres, museums and galleries. **Media laws will include quotas for minority content as a condition for new licences**, under the rules in the Media section.

## Employment and housing

We will establish **a dedicated legislative framework governing the legalisation procedures, the settlement of property rights and the improvement of utility infrastructure in Roma settlements built on public land.**

Jobs in which knowing a minority language is a condition of work – at a service counter, in a school, in a court – will get **mandatory employment plans**, and private employers will get **greater incentives to employ Roma.**

## National councils and the judiciary

**Depoliticising the national councils** means that in their working bodies, professionals will have the majority and parties the minority. We are amending the Law on National Councils of National Minorities to prevent electoral abuse, with public elections and territorial distribution.

**Courts and prosecutor's offices will get interpreters for minority languages**, we will impose the strictest penalties for destroying bilingual signs, and **we are introducing dedicated mechanisms against antigypsyism.**
