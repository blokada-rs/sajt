**The cheap-labour model is exhausted**: there are no longer any workers left for foreign factories that pay the minimum wage. We are not offering a new transition in which a minority grows rich at the expense of the majority. **There will be no sell-off of strategic enterprises, the dinar will remain stable, and confiscated assets will be put to socially useful purposes.** What works in the state, we will keep and complete.

What does not work, we will change through rules that apply to everyone. **Every contract the state signs will be public. Public money will be safeguarded by institutions. Public enterprises will be run by experts from a public list.** We will introduce more progressive taxation, and incentives will go to companies that raise average wages.

**The goal is a knowledge economy based on research and technological development.**

## Every contract the state signs will be public

**The Public Procurement Portal will publish the full text of every contract**, together with the prices and the bidders' scores. Procedures with a single bid or with large annexes will be flagged on the portal and referred to the Budget Inspectorate for review. A flag does not mean a procurement is irregular, only that it must be checked by the competent authorities. **A blacklist is being introduced: anyone who breaches a contract stays on it for three years.**

We will introduce a national price list for public works, and reference prices for medicines, IT equipment, vehicles and fuel. **A reference price does not set a ceiling; it sets a benchmark, and exceeding it requires the contract to be reviewed.** If no agreement is reached, the contract is terminated. This way, we cannot again have a situation in which motorways and other public works cost several times their real price.

**Every new major project will be contracted under the Decree on Capital Project Management.** Instead of being carried out through a special law (Latin: lex specialis), projects will follow the general procedure. For projects already under way, the State Audit Institution and an independent technical auditor will publish the actual cost and the obligations falling due, and the decision to continue, modify or halt them will be made on the basis of a report by the independent Capital Investment Audit Commission (see the Infrastructure section). Serbia will not act unilaterally on international agreements already ratified, but negotiations will be opened where possible.

**For every borrowing, we will publish the terms**: how much was borrowed, for what purpose, at what price and for how long. For contracts that the new government inherits, we will publish everything the law allows, and **new contracts will not contain a price confidentiality clause.** Before we take out any large loan, we will compare it with at least two other sources of financing, so that whether cheaper alternatives exist becomes publicly available information.

**The special laws for Belgrade Waterfront, EXPO 2027 and linear infrastructure will be audited**, with a proposal to repeal or amend them, or bring them into line with the general rules. Each of these options will come with an assessment of the arbitration risk. Unlike ratified international agreements, domestic laws, decisions and permits can be changed by the new majority through the legislative procedure, following the rules in the Infrastructure section. **The practice of special laws with no expiry date ends**, so a lex specialis for an individual project will no longer be able to remain in force indefinitely.

## Who safeguards public money

The current budget reserve exists for unforeseen circumstances, not for items that no one deliberately wanted to include in the budget. **We are restoring it to 2% of revenue.** It will no longer be used for costs that could have been planned, or for the regular programmes of associations, religious communities, sports clubs and local governments. **Every decision on its use will be published within eight days**, with the named beneficiary, the amount and the justification.

**The Fiscal Council will assess the risk of every guarantee, every public-private partnership and every major capital project before the Government decides.** Instead of the Fiscal Council giving its assessment after the decision, or the authorities ignoring its opinions, the Council's position will in future come before the vote, and the Government will be required to give a written response, whatever the content of that assessment.

**The Tax Administration will decide on sending inspections on the basis of electronic risk analysis, not at the whim of officials.** For businesses, lawful operation and sound data will mean being inspected less often. Any inspection outside this system will require a written justification and a record of who ordered it. The Director of the Tax Administration will be selected through a public competition, for a five-year term.

Laws and other regulations that create special rights, conditions for entering a sector, regulated prices or state aid will first be reviewed by the Commission for Protection of Competition, so that it is known whether they close the market to other players. **In its first year, the Commission will carry out sector analyses of telecommunications, food retail, energy and insurance.**

**Revenue from permanently confiscated assets will go into a dedicated account, and at least 30% of it, under a statutory formula, will go to medical treatment, gifted children, science and social protection.** The rest is planned to go into investment, not into plugging holes in the budget. **Every confiscated item will be entered in a public register, audited every year by the State Audit Institution.**

## Public enterprises remain state-owned

**There will be no unnecessary or forced privatisation of public enterprises. Companies of strategic importance remain in state hands.** The State Ownership Strategy will be adopted by the National Assembly instead of the Government, and the law will specify the companies in which the state's share may not fall below its current level, and those in which it may not fall below 51%. Capital increases in which the state does not keep a proportionate share, as well as concessions and leases of fixed assets for more than ten years, will be treated as reductions in the state's share. **Transforming public enterprises into joint-stock companies will not mean privatising them.** The long-term goal is to write the resources and companies that may not be sold into the Constitution, as several EU countries have done.

All terms of office of directors, acting directors and supervisory boards elected under the old law, at the national and local levels, will end within 12 months of the new law entering into force. The conditions for election to these posts, which differ between the old Law on Public Enterprises and the new law on capital companies, will be aligned with the stricter conditions of the new law. Appointing acting directors will be made more difficult, and state-owned enterprises will move to two-tier governance, which will strengthen their autonomy. The government will continue to exercise its share at the shareholders' meeting, but directors will be chosen by a supervisory board whose members come from **a public national list of independent experts who have passed the state exam, have no conflict of interest and have signed a statement that they are independent of political parties.** Competitions will be run by a committee made up of the National Bank of Serbia and the Agency for the Prevention of Corruption. **The state will control the strategic direction of public enterprises, but not appointments directly**, so that they stop being spoils for political parties.

**As a rule, public land and buildings will be sold and leased only at market value and through a public procedure.** Leases outside the public sector will be time-limited. In future, **a contract that has not been published will have no legal effect**, because the state will not recognise contracts signed in secret.

## Taxes

The poorest in Serbia pay disproportionately high charges to the state because of excise goods, where the levy is based on quantity, not on people's income. Instead of cutting excise duties, which would change people's habits for the worse, we plan to change tax policy so that **labour is taxed less and large wealth more.** We are introducing more brackets for progressive income tax, abolishing the annual income tax, and applying the brackets to monthly salaries, at source. **The non-taxable portion of salary will rise from 23% to 35% of the average salary**, which will further strengthen the progressivity of the tax, so that those who have less pay less and those who have more pay more.

**Property tax will be calculated progressively, not by changing the rates, but by calculating all of a person's properties together.** Someone who owns several flats will pay more tax than before, and cities will be able to introduce an additional levy on flats that have stood empty for more than twelve months. The revenue from this tax will continue to go to municipalities. Since one taxpayer's properties may be located in several municipalities, the combined calculation will be done by the Tax Administration, and the law will set out how the revenue is then divided among municipalities, so that none loses its current revenue. Rates of tax on large inheritances will be increased, but spouses, the family home and a high tax-free amount per heir remain exempt, so the tax affects only genuinely large inheritances. **The goal is not budget revenue but making evasion harder**, because a declared and taxed inheritance becomes proof of the origin of assets that no one can later dispute, and assets that have gone undeclared for years come to light at the moment of inheritance.

## Incentives, the Development Bank and the market

From the start, the current government's model of economic development was based on cheap labour. The country was offered to foreign companies on the basis that workers were paid less than in most of Europe, and the state even subsidised this per job. **That model is demographically dead.** Its competitors are countries with far lower wages, and there are no workers left for it. **Per-job subsidies will be abolished, while existing ones will be honoured.** In future, incentives will go only to projects that raise average wages, according to thresholds. The threshold is tied to the average salary and graded in four levels according to the municipality's level of development. It is lower in underdeveloped municipalities, but wages there must still exceed it, and a project without equipment that meets EU standards will not receive incentives. Per-job incentives will remain only in devastated municipalities, as a transitional measure with a deadline written into law. This means the arrival of old-style investors will not be restricted in poor municipalities, because it is important to keep and create every job in such conditions. Elsewhere, such investments are harmful because they create unhealthy supply chains that cannot create added value or be sustainable in the long term in conditions where competitors are capable of innovation.

**Instead of searching the world for factories that need cheap labour, the new government's full focus will be on creating innovative companies that can compete on European and global markets.** By linking universities, institutes and the private sector, we will build the environment start-ups need to grow in Serbia. **We will double investment in research and development, currently around 1% of GDP, by 2032.** For each term of office, the state will choose a limited number of priority areas and direct money to them, and programmes that do not deliver results will be closed.

**The Development Fund and AOFI will be merged into the Development Bank of Serbia in the first half of the term.** As a bank, not a fund, it will have access to funding from the EBRD and other development banks, which is not available to funds. It will lend only through commercial banks and without instructions from the Government. To avoid repeating the fate of the Development Bank of Vojvodina, **its borrowing will be limited by law to four times its capital.** We will strengthen the start-up fund for domestic technology companies, and we may finance it from the revenue from confiscated assets. The state provides the initial stake and remains an investor, while management will be professional. **Support for start-ups will go through universities, the Start-up Fund and the Development Bank, not through subsidies.**

**Large retail chains above a revenue threshold set by law will publish prices per store every day in machine-readable form**, available to anyone who wants to display or compare them. The Energy Agency and RATEL will run comparison tools for electricity, gas and telecommunications offers, and the Statistical Office will publish the price of the basic consumer basket in hours of work at the median wage. **Price caps will be introduced only under a prescribed rule and for a limited time, not as a permanent measure.**

The needs test is the rule under which someone may start a business only once the state or a chamber judges that there is a need for it. Within a year, we will assess all regulations on market entry using the OECD methodology and **abolish quotas and needs tests through a single law, except on safety grounds.** Regulated professions will lose the right to decide for themselves how many new members they admit. **The Register of Administrative Procedures will become exhaustive and binding.** Once a company has given the state a piece of information, the state may no longer ask it for that information again, but must take it from the register itself. Micro and small enterprises will submit one annual report through one portal; for them, a deadline passing without a decision means approval wherever an official has nothing to assess, and every permit must pass a necessity test.

## The public sector and administration

We will replace the hiring ban in Article 27k with **a ceiling on the number of employees per budget user, written into the Budget Law.** The ceiling will be set so as to cover the minimums the law prescribes for particular services. Every budget user will know in advance how many people it may employ, and within that limit every post will be filled through a public competition. The share of trainees and probationary work without a competition will be limited. **Public sector salaries will be adjusted once a year through the Budget Law, not by a Government decision.** Regular adjustments will follow wage growth outside the public sector, and any change in pay relativities between sectors or any new employee entitlement will be adopted through the same law, with funding in the budget.

**Identity cards and health insurance cards will be free, and passports and driving licences will be charged only at the actual cost of production**, with reductions for children, pensioners and persons with disabilities. Electronic extracts, certificates and the registration of sole traders will be free of charge. **By 2028, every procedure with the state will be possible electronically**, without abolishing counter services. Where all the data is already in registers and no discretionary decision is needed, decisions will be issued automatically.

## The dinar

**We will not touch the value of the dinar.** Devaluation would help exports briefly, but it would push up prices and damage savings. The exchange rate regime is set by the National Bank of Serbia with the Government's consent, and the Government gives that consent to the existing regime of a relatively stable exchange rate with inflation targeting and will not initiate any change to it. At the same time, we will change the dinar's unhealthy foundations. Today the dinar is sustained by inflows of foreign investment, not by domestic savings. Only 22.5% of public debt is in dinars; the rest is in foreign currencies, so every weakening of the dinar automatically increases the debt. **The goal is to increase the dinar share of public debt to 30% by the end of the term**, because debt in dinars does not grow when the exchange rate falls. **We will introduce dinar savings bonds for citizens.** Guarantees and borrowing by public enterprises will be in dinars wherever the market allows.
