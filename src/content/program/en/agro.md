Agriculture is a strategic branch of the economy and the foundation of food sovereignty, but farmers in Serbia are in effect second-class citizens. Subsidies are paid late and distributed without transparency, villages are left without young people, schools, clinics and internet, and farmers' produce is held hostage by debt and by the arbitrary power of cartels that dictate terms from inputs, through storage, to purchasing and trade. Around half of farm holders are over 65, livestock numbers are falling fast, and the damage from weather extremes grows year after year.

**Our goal is a developed and planned agriculture in which the farmer is a strategic partner of the state, not a welfare category; in which land and water stay with the citizens of Serbia; in which prices are formed on a fair market, not in cartel agreements; and in which the village has a future.** Through a Law on Agriculture as a Strategic and National Interest of the Republic of Serbia, we will declare agriculture a strategic and national interest. To protect farmers, the guardians of our natural resources and food independence, we will establish **an Ombudsman of the Republic of Serbia for the Preservation of Land, Water Resources and Food Independence.**

## Equality before the law and income security

We will set out in law **deadlines for paying subsidies, with automatic statutory interest and liability of the authority for every delay**, modelled on the mandatory deadlines of the Common Agricultural Policy. Subsidies will be awarded according to public criteria known in advance, and the register of beneficiaries will be public. We are introducing a mandatory "rural proofing": every new law and measure must include an assessment of its impact on rural areas. We are also introducing a network of free or co-funded legal advice centres for farms. As soon as the government is formed, **we will protect farmers' right to organise and to express their views publicly**, and consultation with their organisations will become mandatory. We will adapt farmers' pension and social insurance to their seasonal income, and give rural areas **equal access to healthcare, education, roads, internet and legal aid.**

## The market for agricultural products

**The state will not set the price of wheat, maize or raspberries administratively, nor will it use taxpayers' money to push down purchase prices.** We are redirecting the money that went on intervention to push prices down into insurance, irrigation, storage and equipment, and we are depoliticising the state's role in the market, including the commodity reserves, and placing it under public control. The transition will be gradual, so that producers are not left without protection for a single season.

**The new system is based on public warehouses and warehouse receipts.** Every warehouse that accepts other people's goods must be registered, supervised and insured, and the producer immediately receives an electronic document of ownership, backed by actual goods. Protection comes from the warehouse keeper's guarantee, insurance of the goods and a Compensation Fund; disputes are settled by fast-track arbitration, and shortfalls in goods are penalised proportionately, up to revocation of the licence. On the basis of warehouse receipts, **farmers can obtain credit**, within a National Bank of Serbia framework and with a guarantee scheme for small producers, so they will no longer have to sell at the moment they need money most. The commodity reserves will intervene only in serious disruptions, according to public rules, and goods will be classified according to EU standards, with producers having the right to decide for themselves what to do with them.

We will modernise the Novi Sad Commodity Exchange and set up **a public market price platform available to every farm.** Before the start of the year, buyers and warehouse keepers will publish their offers and prices, and written contracts must state the price or price formula, the quantity, the quality and the payment deadline. A Law on Unfair Trading Practices, based on EU Directive 2019/633, will require **payment within 30 days for perishable products and 60 days for others**, ban unilateral changes to contracts and protect those who report violations. Following the French EGalim laws, we are banning purchases below the cost of production, and following the Italian model, we are setting up a specialised inspectorate for late payments. Within the Commission for Protection of Competition, we are establishing a unit for the agri-food chain that will monitor margins; we will monitor imports continuously, with safeguard measures in line with agreements; and we will make it easier to sell at markets and directly from the farm.

## Land and water stay with the citizens of Serbia

**We are introducing a temporary moratorium on converting agricultural land to other categories, permanently restricting its conversion to building land, and abolishing the special rules for land affected by fire.** We are putting the farm register and the records of ownership and leases in order, soil analysis is becoming mandatory, and good practices will receive incentives.

## Long-term strategy, zoning and processing

**We will adopt an agricultural development strategy covering at least ten years**, adopted by the National Assembly, with measurable targets, an annual report and independent evaluation, and we will zone production according to soil, climate and proximity to processing. We are encouraging processing linked to local producers and protected designations of origin, and adapting measures to farm size: for small farms, programmes for fruit and vegetable growing and local markets; for medium-sized farms of 5 to 20 hectares, access to state land, machinery rings and producer organisations; for large farms, a switch to technologies in line with zoning, and for those over 500 hectares, tax incentives for bioenergy and processing plants.

## Livestock farming

**We are launching a programme to rebuild livestock numbers** by supporting the purchase of breeding animals and equipment, with a special programme for small farms in underdeveloped areas, a national raw milk quality programme and **long-term contracts between livestock farmers and slaughterhouses and dairies**, with oversight of imports and safeguard measures.

## Climate, water and risk management

**We are introducing mandatory multi-risk crop insurance against drought, floods, hail and frost, with the state co-financing at least 50% of the premium for farms of up to 50 hectares**, an obligation on insurers to cover high-risk areas too, and payment of compensation within 30 days. For damage beyond the market's capacity, we are establishing a State Guarantee Fund for Extreme Risks. We are subsidising irrigation, renewal of the canal network, anti-hail nets, cold stores and precision agriculture, and we are establishing regional water boards on the Dutch model and **a State Fund for Climate Adaptation in Agriculture worth at least 50 million euros a year**, managed jointly by the ministry, the scientific community and associations. The National Afforestation Action Plan has a target of at least 10,000 hectares a year, and by 2030 one fifth of farms should cover their own energy needs. The measures will be led by a national coordinating body through five-year action plans.

## Forests, science and genetic resources

We will identify areas suitable for afforestation that do not conflict with food production, set up a carbon credit platform and a green fund for ecosystem services, allow every licensed forestry engineer to work in private forests, and depoliticise the public forestry enterprises. An independent expert body will urgently inventory the genetic resources held in institutes, and **the Gene Bank will begin working at full capacity.** State institutes will get stable funding and depoliticised management, we are introducing scholarships for agricultural courses, we are returning advisory services to professional work, and we will require tenants of state land to employ agronomists.

## Food safety and cooperation

**We will consistently apply food safety standards**, with support for small producers to introduce them, strict control of pesticides, antibiotics and GMOs, stronger laboratories and inspectorates, and "farm to fork" traceability. We are reviving agricultural cooperatives as professionally run businesses owned by their members, with favourable loans for storage, cold stores and processing, and we are tying support to how they actually function, not to their formal establishment. **We are establishing an Agri-Parliament, an umbrella body of associations, cooperatives and producer organisations, which must be consulted on agricultural regulations.**

## Villages and young people

**The village becomes a national interest and a priority of the strategy.** Local communities will gain greater powers and budget lines for rural infrastructure. Young people will receive incentives to consolidate holdings and buy abandoned farms, on condition that they stay in the village, as well as tax relief, while fictitious farms will lose support. **Roads, internet, schools, clinics and school transport will be part of investment from the start of the term.** We will increase the uptake of IPARD funds through expert assistance and pre-financing.

## Implementation timeline

**As soon as the government is formed**, we will protect the right to organise, halt the transfer of land to foreign persons and launch a review of contracts, introduce the moratorium on land conversion, end intervention to push prices down while launching warehouse receipts, and begin the inventory of genetic resources. **In the first year**, we will adopt the Law on Agriculture as a Strategic Interest, the statutory deadlines for paying subsidies and the Law on Unfair Trading Practices, and set up the register of warehouse keepers, the price platform and the first phase of mandatory insurance. **In the second year**, we will establish the institution of the Ombudsman for the Preservation of Land, Water Resources and Food Independence. During the term, we will adopt the strategy and zoning, implement the irrigation and livestock rebuilding programmes, establish the Agri-Parliament and reform farmers' pension insurance.
