Although the Constitution guarantees citizens the right to local self-government, in practice they have little influence over decision-making in their community. Municipalities depend heavily on decisions made at higher levels of government, oversight of public spending is made impossible, and public enterprises and institutions are exposed to party-based hiring. Forms of direct participation bring no concrete results or effects, power in the municipality is concentrated in the hands of mayors, and public property either fails to serve its purpose or is unjustifiably sold off.

Citizens want to shape their own surroundings and to raise the quality of life in their community through their decisions. However, decisions are most often made without their real participation.

That is why we will enable citizens, in their local communities, **to take binding decisions directly on matters that affect their surroundings**, and to elect councillors by name. **Executive power will become collective, and its work public.** Money will follow responsibilities, and public property will serve citizens' needs. Hiring and promotion will be based on expertise and public competitions, and administrations will be assessed by the services citizens receive. **We will link decentralisation to stable funding** to improve living conditions in all parts of the country.

## Councillors elected by name

Citizens often do not know who represents them in the municipality or whom to turn to about a problem in their area. That is why we are introducing **a two-round majoritarian system for electing councillors**. Each electoral district, with a roughly equal number of voters, will elect one representative by name in a two-round majoritarian system. This strengthens councillors' ties with their area and their accountability to voters, and gives local and independent candidates the chance to build support directly in the community. **Councillors will report to their voters at an open meeting at least once a year.**

## Local communities and formal citizens' assemblies

Today, citizens can submit requests about parks, playgrounds, village roads, street lighting, street improvements, kindergartens, and other similar matters, but the final decisions remain out of their reach. That is why citizens in all local communities will gain the right **to take binding decisions directly at a formal citizens' assembly** on local matters specified by law and on how to use the funds provided to them for those matters. The local community council, which citizens elect at the assembly and can recall, will act on their decisions, and the municipal administration will ensure that they are carried out professionally and lawfully. For significant changes in a neighbourhood, such as changing the use of green spaces or cutting down rows of trees, we will introduce **a right of prior consent for the formal citizens' assembly**, under a procedure regulated by law.

## Guaranteed funding for local communities and city municipalities

Citizens' decisions at the assembly can only be implemented if citizens, that is, local communities, know what funds they have at their disposal. Today, the role of local communities and city municipalities has been rendered completely meaningless by the absence of a guaranteed minimum. That is why we will **guarantee by law a minimum annual amount of funding for every city municipality and every local community**, based on objective criteria. For development projects covering several local communities, such as shared cultural centres, a large park, cycle paths, and other similar projects, separate funds will be set aside in the budget, and citizens will decide directly at the formal assemblies of the local communities which projects are funded. **Local community premises will remain in public ownership, and local communities will gain a permanent, free-of-charge right to use and manage them**, with protection against arbitrary seizure and change of use.

## Reform of local executive power

When local government comes down to one person, oversight by the assembly easily becomes a formality. **The executive powers of the municipal president or city mayor and of the municipal or city council will be transferred to portfolio committees** that the local assembly elects, oversees, and dismisses. The committees will take decisions independently and collectively in their areas, with clear responsibilities and public records of who voted how and who is implementing each decision. A single professional administration will be led by **a head of administration selected through a public competition, with guaranteed professional independence**. We will adapt the number and scope of portfolios to the size of the municipality, after first assessing costs and capacity.

## National Decentralisation Strategy

Transferring local responsibilities makes no sense unless money and people are provided for them. **In the first year, we will adopt a National Decentralisation Strategy**, with the participation of local communities and a public debate. It will determine which decisions and responsibilities are transferred closer to citizens, the deadlines, and the permanent sources of funding. We will strengthen city municipalities and regional cooperation between cities and municipalities in providing services, with special support for underdeveloped areas. **Every transfer of responsibilities will come with adequate funding and professional support.**

## Local property and public goods

Local infrastructure in municipalities is falling into disrepair, and the spaces that do exist are often not available to citizens, because their use is decided by commercial interests and political connections instead of community needs. Every municipality will be required to draw up **a complete public register of its property**, which will be publicly available, and to set out in its statute a list of assets that cannot be sold, leased long-term for a change of use, or permanently converted to another use (such as utility infrastructure, parks, kindergartens, health centres, libraries, sports facilities, local community premises, and social housing). **All lease contracts will be audited**, and future contracts will contain clear clauses on termination and on the property returning to public management if obligations are not met.

## Ending party control over hiring in public enterprises and institutions

Hiring in local public enterprises and institutions is one of the main mechanisms of party control at the local level and a lever for blackmail. The consequences are a lack of qualified and operational staff, and loyalty to the party instead of the public interest. **Directors and members of supervisory and management boards will be selected by an independent commission and may not be officials of political parties.** There will be a mandatory public competition for every job, and every enterprise and institution will publish its staffing plan and job classification once a year. A particular priority will be **protecting employees from political pressure**, through an anonymous reporting channel and whistleblower protection.
