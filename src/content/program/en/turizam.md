Public investment in tourism today is planned without enough data or clear accountability, decisions are scattered across institutions, and citizens cannot see who is responsible for the results. New accommodation overloads water supply, sewerage and transport, while local residents put up with crowds, noise and rising prices, with no say in the decisions and no share of the benefits.

## Public investment in tourism

**We will publish a single register of all subsidies, incentives and capital tourism projects**, showing the beneficiary of each award, its purpose, amount, deadline and contracted result. It will be updated every quarter, with an annual report. **Residents of tourist destinations will be able to choose local priorities publicly once a year.** We will not promise any number of facilities, users or transport lines before a costing has been published, with a baseline scenario and a reduced-budget scenario.

## Infrastructure before new accommodation

**Renovating existing buildings will take priority over new construction**, and in overloaded destinations such as Zlatibor and Kopaonik, **the state will not fund new accommodation capacity until water, transport, sewerage, electricity and internet have been sorted out.** Subsidies will depend on waste and wastewater having been dealt with, rail and bus services will be matched to demand, and seasonal services to destinations will have a published route and fare. Short-term rentals will be brought into registration, taxation and minimum standards, without a ban on renting out.

In the first phase, we will focus particularly on the Danube corridor and eastern and south-eastern Serbia. **We will revise plans that allow construction out of proportion to the character of a place, and protect public access to riverbanks and lakeshores, viewpoints and parks.** Every major project will assess climate risks over its entire lifetime, and artificial snowmaking projects will have to prove the long-term availability of water and energy. **Residents of tourist destinations will have mandatory public consultations on major projects**, and the impact of short-term rentals on housing availability will be monitored.

## Spas and villages

**Every spa seeking public support will have to adopt its own renewal plan**, setting out who owns it, what is happening to its springs and what its medical purpose is. **Priority will go to protecting springs, treatment centres and links with the health system, and apartments must not crowd out the health function.** For rural households, we will introduce advice, planning support and small grants.

## Tourism vouchers and working conditions

**Tourism vouchers will be allocated on the basis of a public social points system, according to income and household size.** Higher-value vouchers will go to people with the lowest incomes and to persons with disabilities. It will also be possible to apply at the post office, and some vouchers will be reserved for the off-season. **Incentives for employers will be tied to registered workers and regular wages**, and placements for school and university students must have a mentor, a learning plan and pay. **Short-term engagements must not replace regular workers.**
