# Infrastructure, urban planning, telecommunications and transport

Today, decisions about what gets built, where and for whom are made outside the institutions. **Contracts for the largest public projects have been declared confidential, special laws bypass the general ones, plans are adapted to suit investors, and public participation has been reduced to an online formality.** Cities are expanding without enough water supply, schools and transport. Serbia has had no spatial plan for six years, maintenance of existing structures has been neglected, and public transport is ageing and becoming less and less reliable. **We are returning decisions about construction, spatial management, networks and transport to the institutions and placing them under public control.** Contracts will be available to the public, special laws will be repealed or brought into line with the general ones, and **maintaining existing infrastructure and improving public transport will take priority over new megaprojects.**

## Construction

### Contracts and projects

**We will make the contracts for capital public projects that have been declared confidential available to the public as soon as the government is formed**, to the fullest extent the law allows. In future, secrecy will be clearly limited by law, and its abuse will be a criminal offence. **Within six months, we will set up an independent commission to review all capital public investments.** The commission will have a four-year mandate and publish reports every three months, with clear recommendations for each project on whether it should continue, be modified or be halted.

Within twelve months, **the special laws adopted for individual projects will be repealed or brought into line with the general laws**, and the projects and procedures launched under those laws will be reviewed. **No capital project will be able to enter the investment plan without a feasibility study, consideration of alternative solutions and a technical and economic analysis.** Site investigations will become a mandatory part of every investment project, with particular emphasis on funding.

We will adopt a strategy for the development of the construction sector and master plans for linear infrastructure at the national and regional levels. The law will require **a mandatory technical review of detailed design before works begin**, a mandatory structural design for all categories of buildings and a mandatory structural condition and reconstruction report, as well as **the legal and financial liability of company directors and owners and of the authorities that issue permits**, so that the greatest responsibility is not borne by the most junior engineers. We will revise the Law on Legalisation so that it separates the legalisation of buildings people need to live in from speculative construction.

We will establish **a single public list of priority projects**, which will include all public investments worth more than two million euros, with their preparation stages and implementation status clearly shown. The criteria for selecting projects will be measurable and publicly available, and the public interest will be given a clear and verifiable definition, instead of being interpreted differently from case to case. Within three years, we will also introduce **a mandatory post-completion review of every completed project worth more than ten million euros.** In this way, for each of them it will be clear what was promised, what was actually built, how long it took and how much the project cost in the end.

### Maintenance

The building inspectorate will be present on construction sites more often, to stop undeclared employment of workers and breaches of occupational safety measures. Such practices will be suppressed and penalised, because hiring workers "off the books" distorts the market just as much as direct deals do. Today there are often neither proper records nor regular inspections, so problems with the condition of a building are discovered only once the damage has been done. **For all public and capital structures, we are introducing a mandatory asset management system, in which maintenance funding will be an integral part of every public investment.** All types of buildings will have to undergo periodic inspections and condition assessments within prescribed deadlines. This obligation will also apply to building owners, because it is important to spot deterioration in time, before it endangers people's safety.

As soon as possible, we will set up a new Court of Honour panel at the Chamber of Engineers, and **every case against an engineer will have to be concluded within six months**, instead of sitting unresolved for years and finally lapsing under the statute of limitations. Within a year, we will also establish **a register of construction companies that have received negative assessments from competent domestic or international institutions**, so that contracting authorities can find out about their past work before signing a contract with them.

We will adopt a new Law on the Chamber of Engineers that separates the Chamber from the ministry; its bodies will consist only of engineers. For the minister of construction and senior staff at the ministry, we will set the requirement that they be engineers in the relevant field with at least ten years of experience. **Tenders will assess references, expertise and capacity, not only the lowest price.** We will create a database of public buildings, including architectural heritage, and introduce a legal obligation to archive design documentation.

## Space and housing

### Projects of national interest

**EXPO, "Belgrade Waterfront", the General Staff building, the Belgrade Fair, "Jadar" and other projects declared to be of "national interest" will be reviewed by the independent Capital Investment Audit Commission**, and we will launch these procedures as soon as the government is formed. This includes mandatory feasibility and impact studies, publication of all documentation, and the annulment of unlawfully adopted plans. **The decision on whether a project continues, is modified or is halted will be made on the basis of the studies' findings, by the same procedure for all, regardless of who is behind it.** The same procedure applies to all projects under the previous regime's special laws and contracts, whichever part of the programme mentions them. **No irreversible steps in either direction will be taken before the commission decides.** The status of "project of special importance" will no longer depend solely on a Government decree, but will be subject to clear criteria such as the value of the investment, the number of jobs and the expected public revenue. The decision will be preceded by independent scrutiny, and it will be possible to challenge it in court. **Spatial plans for special-purpose areas will be limited to purposes precisely listed in law and may not be adopted for populated areas**, because this instrument is increasingly used to bypass regular procedures.

An urban design project will not be able to change land use, increase a plan's parameters or replace public facilities with commercial ones, and detailed regulation plans will not be allowed to exceed the parameters of the general plan.

**Expropriation for private and commercial projects without a clearly established public interest will be banned**, and the consent of plot owners will be required from the planning stage onwards. Every change in the cadastre will be recorded in an audit trail, access to the data will be free, and owners will be notified as soon as a change affecting their plot occurs. **Public spaces will not be able to be handed over to private entities without a prior expert assessment and public scrutiny.** All public property and transactions involving it will be recorded in public digital registers, and land disposed of through proven abuse will be returned to public ownership.

### Public housing is not for sale

**Housing is a right, not an opportunity for speculation, which is why public flats will no longer be for sale.** The basic model will be long-term non-profit rental at an affordable price, and social housing will be exempt from property tax. **Forced evictions from a person's sole home will be suspended as soon as the government is formed, until a suitable alternative is provided.** As soon as the government is formed, we will set up registers of public flats, public land and all planning documents. We will bring empty public flats into use, and expand the housing stock by converting existing public buildings and by buying properties.

Tenants will get standardised contracts and protection from arbitrary termination of their lease, and rents will be aligned with average incomes in a given location. We will also set up a register of leases and a record of empty flats, so that we have a clearer picture of the market. **Short-term rental of flats to tourists will be restricted.** This means that flats intended for living in will not be used exclusively as tourist accommodation. We will tackle homelessness on the "Housing First" principle, with targeted support programmes for families at risk of losing their home, young people leaving the social care system, older people, persons with disabilities, single parents and victims of violence.

### No building in flood zones

We will protect agricultural, forest and water land from uncontrolled urbanisation, and legalisation will be possible only where it is in line with plans and environmentally justified. **We will ban new construction in flood zones and remove existing illegal buildings there.** This may be a tough measure, but people's safety must matter more than the value of any building. Part of the increase in land value that results from a change of plan, rather than from the owner's investment, will go back to the community that created that value through its decision. An energy performance certificate will be mandatory for every property sale, so that buyers know in advance what they are buying and what heating costs to expect.

**We will develop water supply and sewerage systems so that priority goes to settlements without a reliable supply of drinking water or proper wastewater treatment.**

For schools, health centres and social care institutions, we will introduce binding standards that set the required capacity, accessibility and public transport links. This will ensure that schools and clinics are within reach of people even in places where private investors have no interest in building.

**The principle of spatial justice will become a mandatory framework for laws and plans**, with a mandatory social impact assessment of every plan. The priority is to adopt a new Spatial Plan of the Republic of Serbia, which the country has been without for six years, while developing a polycentric network of settlements and strengthening small and medium-sized towns. We will introduce a digital cadastre of green spaces, a minimum share of green and permeable surfaces per plot and a minimum area of greenery per inhabitant, and double rows of trees will be planted along new roads.

**Every change to a plan will have a public participation plan, a "summary for citizens" and a reasoned response to every objection**, and participation units will be set up in municipalities. Every profession, including architects and planners, will be able to establish its own independent chamber. Energy facilities and data centres will be located according to suitability maps, with measurable limits on water and electricity consumption for data centres.

### Local communities

Today, decisions about the parks, tree-lined streets and playgrounds in your neighbourhood are made by people who do not live there. Residents often find out that trees will be cut down or a park made smaller only when the works begin. **That is why residents will directly elect the councils of all local communities, including those in Belgrade.**

## Telecommunications and digitalisation

### Mobile and fibre-optic networks

The mobile network covers a large area, but the signal often does not reach the inside of village houses, and fibre-optic internet is being rolled out to villages slowly and at great cost. **By 2027, the 5G network will cover 80% of the population and very high capacity fixed networks 97%, while the long-term goal is for all citizens to have access to these services.** Since investment in rural areas is often not profitable for operators, the state will subsidise the construction of infrastructure. Operators will also be required to install base stations in villages, with funding from European funds, RATEL resources and the budget. EPS will extend its fibre-optic network to populated areas. Elektromreža Srbije will add more fibres to its cables to substations, which it will be able to lease to operators, and for mobile coverage in sparsely populated areas we will adopt a multi-year funding plan – among other things, by allocating free frequencies with an obligation to build a certain number of rural stations each year.

Every new building will have to be ready for a fibre connection, and **permits for base stations and the installation of fibre-optic cables will be issued at a single point of contact, within clearly defined deadlines**, instead of through long and inconsistent procedures. A public database at RATEL, with data on existing infrastructure and planned works, will make it possible to lay fibre-optic cables at the same time as roads or water mains are built, instead of infrastructure being damaged again and again with every excavation. We will address concerns about radiation with verifiable data, with a public portal showing all mobile masts and measurement results. **A new Law on Non-Ionising Radiation will require measurements by an independent state laboratory**, on a random sample or in response to citizens' complaints, with severe fines for every violation. Within a year, we will train market inspectors to check telecommunications equipment on sale in shops.

### State IT systems and cybersecurity

State systems today are not connected to one another. Each has its own servers, costs and security vulnerabilities, and the cyberattacks on the Republic Geodetic Authority and EPS showed how costly this can be. **"Cloud First" becomes a legal obligation.** New state systems will be built on the state cloud, existing ones will be moved to the State Data Centre in Kragujevac, and ministries' budgets for IT equipment will go into a single fund for its expansion. **Migration is mandatory for all ministries**, and the Data Centre will publish a public price list so that every institution knows in advance how much migration will cost and how much it will save.

**Security, access and cryptographic key management, incident response and classified data remain in the hands of the state.** As a rule, data from systems maintained by an outside contractor is stored in Serbia, encrypted in transit and at rest, with limited and monitored access for the contractor's staff. Every such contract includes an exit plan, access to all data and a ban on lock-in to a single supplier.

The "Sajber svesno" (Cyber Aware) campaign and a national programme for children and vulnerable groups aim to reduce successful cyber fraud and digital violence. We are also strengthening the National CERT, the high-tech crime units and the "Čuvam te" (I Protect You) and "Sajber straža" (Cyber Guard) platforms. **Within a year, we will adopt a new National Cybersecurity Strategy for 2027–2031**, since the current one expires at the end of 2026. We are building language models for Serbian, open national datasets and high-performance computing infrastructure, supporting domestic AI companies and **adopting the first Law on Artificial Intelligence, aligned with the EU AI Act.**

We will align the Law on Information Security with the European NIS2 Directive, so that measures depend on the actual risk, and through the "Digital Renewal of the State" programme we will replace outdated systems in the most critical institutions in phases. **We will adopt a law on how the state uses information technology, requiring public bodies to share data and infrastructure.** We will entrust the leadership of the Institute for Artificial Intelligence and of the Council to people with proven scientific and international experience, and we will set up a permanent inter-ministerial body to coordinate digital policy.

## Transport

### Maintaining roads and railways

For years, transport has developed from project to project, without a binding framework; maintenance, safety and public transport have stagnated, and road maintenance has neither stable funding nor uniform standards. **As soon as possible, we will publish a public map of critical road and rail sections**, temporary speed restrictions and dangerous spots, with the order in which they will be repaired. We will draw up a national transport strategy with a published methodology for setting priorities, adopt an operational plan with named responsible bodies and an inter-ministerial body, and start publishing daily open data on queues at border crossings. **Preventive maintenance will take priority over new capacity, with multi-year programmes and dedicated funding sources.** Road safety audits will be followed by a publicly announced plan to fix the shortcomings identified, and we will align speed limits with the function of each street.

Vehicle fleets are ageing, and older vehicles pollute the most. **We will gradually introduce restrictions on importing the oldest used vehicles**, above all diesel models with lower emission standards, and only after a social and market assessment, so that the measure does not hurt people who cannot afford another vehicle. In the medium term, registration and environmental fees will be linked to a vehicle's emission class, power and weight. In city centres, some on-street parking will give way to other uses of the street, and parking revenue will be earmarked for specific purposes.

Roadworthiness tests will be electronically linked to the vehicle register, and removing filters and catalytic converters will be consistently penalised. **We will manage road safety on the principle that human error must not lead to death**, with automatic enforcement at locations of proven risk. Before approval, every major transport project will have to be compared with at least one non-investment alternative, and after it opens it will be independently evaluated after three and five years. Local governments will adopt sustainable urban mobility plans based on actual surveys, with support from a national centre for sustainable mobility.

### Public transport

For years, cities have adapted to cars, while public transport has lost both reliability and priority. **Public transport will get clear minimum standards for frequency, reliability, crowding and cleanliness**, as well as monthly data by line from the first 100 days, so that everyone can check whether their line meets the standard. Cameras will be installed on yellow bus lanes so that those who block them are fined, and in the first year a programme to restore priority to public transport will start in the largest cities. The railways will get public service contracts with standards for frequency and reliability, timetables synchronised with local transport and integrated ticketing. **The largest cities will get suburban rail, including a link to the airport where justified.**

We will introduce integrated fares and passenger rights protection in urban, suburban and regional transport, low-floor vehicles and accessible stops, and clock-face timetables on the main rail routes. We will develop ports and landings and a network of ship waste reception points on the Danube, Sava and Tisa, protect the zones around airports from illegal construction, and encourage moving freight to rail and waterways. We will introduce a single electronic customs window and publish waiting times at border crossings. We will analyse the working conditions of drivers, train drivers and pilots, and foreign drivers will work under the same labour and safety standards as domestic ones.

**Pedestrians and cyclists today have no safe and connected network.** The journey to school will become safe with 30 km/h zones, properly marked pedestrian crossings and parking control around schools, and networks of pedestrian and cycling corridors will link schools, public services and bus stops, with cyclists physically separated from cars on busy roads. The Post Office will get a five-year plan with indicators for delivery times, delivery reliability, network accessibility and complaint resolution, as well as an annual public assessment of how it is being carried out. Working conditions at the Post Office will be subject to an independent assessment of workload and pay, and the improvement plan must be measurable and agreed with the employees.
