As much as 70% of Serbia's electricity is produced in thermal power plants, while gas arrives under long-term contracts, mainly from the Russian Federation and to a lesser extent from Azerbaijan. For many years, energy companies were run unprofessionally and by party patronage, which is why we lack qualified staff, and Elektroprivreda Srbije has been ruined and needs systematic investment. **The energy sector stays in state hands, and experts take over its management. EPS and capital energy investments remain majority state-owned, and diversion-type small hydropower plants are banned.** Thermal power plants remain the backbone of the energy system for the time being, while a decarbonisation strategy is implemented. Citizens will find it easier to produce their own electricity. At the same time, we will **secure gas supplies from several directions**, so that Serbia is not overly dependent on a single geopolitical actor.

## EPS stays state-owned

We will set out in law that **all planned capital energy investments are majority-owned by the Republic of Serbia**, and the same law will define what counts as a capital investment, which does not include prosumer production or energy communities. **EPS, as the driver of development and a key factor in energy security, remains state-owned, and all hydropower projects will be owned by the Republic of Serbia.**

We will bring into EPS **qualified staff who will ensure conscientious and responsible management and introduce transparency into its operations.** We will invest systematically in the company's operations and capitalisation, while implementing measures to improve the energy market and the quality of services for end users.

**All signed contracts and projects in the energy sector**, including planned future investments, will be reviewed in the contract and capital project audit described in the Economy and Finance and Infrastructure sections. The aim of the review is to establish what was contracted, at what price, and which planned projects are worth continuing. **Only on the basis of these findings will we be able to plan further development responsibly and decide on future investments.**

## Managing energy companies

**Management positions in energy companies will be held by energy experts**, provided they have not taken part in corrupt practices, selected through the procedure described in the Economy and Finance section. **Appointment to management positions will no longer depend on party membership, but on expertise and a clean record.** Srbijagas and Gas Infrastruktura will also get professional and transparent management, based on the same criteria.

We will reform the Council of the Energy Agency so that it is made up of experts from the sector, as well as consultants from institutes and higher education institutions. **The Council will adopt conclusions of importance for the development of the energy system, produce techno-economic analyses of projects and oversee their implementation.**

**We will invest systematically in developing qualified staff** by funding study programmes and professional training in energy companies, with ongoing cooperation between higher education institutions and the companies themselves.

## Thermal and hydropower plants

**We will strictly ban all existing and future small hydropower projects that divert riverbeds into pipes**, and we will revoke privileged electricity producer status from all small hydropower plants that threaten ecosystems. Privileged status, which guarantees producers that their electricity will be bought, will be kept only by plants whose operation does not threaten rivers. **The owners of the other plants will lose the privileges they gained at the rivers' expense.**

**Thermal power plants are the backbone of the energy system today and will remain so for the time being.** We will actively maintain and upgrade them, while strengthening capacity for stable electricity production. We will consistently implement the measures set out in the National Emission Reduction Plan (NERP), the Air Protection Programme, the Integrated National Energy and Climate Plan (INECP) and the Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC), and continue to improve them. **We will draw up a Decarbonisation Strategy with gradual targets aligned with existing acts and a long-term commitment to full decarbonisation.**

On the basis of feasibility studies for conversion and fuel switching, we will decide which outdated plants should be converted and which should be mothballed with the option of being brought back into use.

**We will refurbish all remaining hydropower plants, above all the "Đerdap 2" plant**, to extend their working life. Hydropower plants play an important role both in baseload electricity production and in balancing the power system. We will draw up a conceptual plan for the development of new hydropower plants that sets priorities. First to be considered will be new large plants and cross-border projects that improve international cooperation, then reservoirs and pumped-storage plants, which allow production during periods of high demand or high electricity prices, and then cascade and dam-toe run-of-river plants, which have the least impact on the environment. **The plan will not specify particular rivers and locations in advance**, given that every large hydropower plant and reservoir significantly changes the landscape and the life of the community where it is built.

## Citizens as producers

A prosumer is a household or business that uses the electricity produced by its own solar panels for its own needs and feeds the surplus into the grid. **We will amend the Law on the Use of Renewable Energy Sources to simplify procedures for such producers**, on the basis of a prior analysis of European experience. Through the Energy Law and its implementing regulations, we will also regulate energy communities, in which citizens and local actors invest jointly in energy generation facilities and use or sell the energy produced.

**We will direct additional investment in renewable energy sources in three directions: through EPS, through local prosumer production and through energy communities**, which the law already recognises.

We will plan the development of renewable energy projects systematically, with better spatial planning, protection of natural resources and agricultural land, assessment of cumulative environmental impacts, early public involvement and clear criteria for choosing sites. **The public will be involved before a site is chosen, not only once the decision to build has already been made.**

**Rooftop solar plants on residential and commercial buildings will take priority over construction on green spaces and arable land.** This also means a stricter assessment of the impact of ground-mounted solar park projects, to ensure that their development does not threaten natural resources and agricultural production.

## Heating and energy efficiency

Energy efficiency measures require significant financial investment, but deliver results in the medium and long term. We will insist on upgrading the thermal envelopes of old buildings to the prescribed standards, so that heat is used as efficiently as possible. **Heating will be charged according to the heat actually consumed, using heat meters, instead of according to the floor area of the flat.** In this way, bills will reflect actual consumption, so those who use less will pay less.

We will set out measures to reduce losses in district heating plants and main heating pipelines that can be implemented in a relatively short time. We will also start **switching district heating plants from solid fuels to geothermal energy**, using heat pumps and shallow boreholes, as well as to biomethane.

**We will establish an independent energy efficiency institution** responsible for implementing national renovation programmes for public, multi-family and family buildings, and for providing support to households, businesses and local governments. Within this institution's work, we will also consistently apply an energy management system.

**We will simplify the procedures for awarding energy efficiency subsidies** under the support programmes for households, multi-family buildings and family houses. We will also consider introducing a revolving fund, in which repaid funds would be reinvested in new renovation projects.

We will set a deadline for the full introduction of energy performance certificates, so that every building has a clearly established energy status. The certificate will show how much energy a building uses and which measures need to be taken to improve its energy efficiency, which will also introduce a new obligation for building owners. **The goal is also a complete switch from heating oil and fuel oil heating systems to more sustainable solutions.**

## Gas and oil

**The priority capital projects in gas infrastructure include building new storage facilities and interconnectors.** We will complete the interconnector with North Macedonia, which will provide access to Greece's gas infrastructure and to the gas corridor leading to the LNG terminal in Alexandroupolis. We will also consider speeding up construction and increasing the capacity of the interconnector with Romania, as well as **building a gas storage facility in Banat, at Srpski Itebej.** We will also expand the capacity of existing interconnectors.

We will continue to extract oil from domestic sources, **charging fees without privileged treatment for the companies that extract it.** Fees will apply on equal terms to all entities extracting oil. Where an intergovernmental agreement in force provides otherwise, we will achieve this through negotiation. **Keeping the existing refineries running remains our strategic and imperative commitment**, with consistent application of environmental protection standards, regular monitoring of their impact and a stable supply of fuel to citizens, businesses and public institutions.
