## With the Roma community

The electoral will of every citizen must be free and unconditional. Yet this right was not equally protected for everyone. Despite the historic victory and the unity we showed, election day, as well as the days before and after it, was marked by numerous irregularities. We believe it is necessary to address the pressure, blackmail and attempts at vote-buying during the electoral process.

Allegations of money being offered, of social assistance and jobs being promised, of threats to withhold them, and of abuse of position by individuals employed in public institutions require serious public attention and unequivocal condemnation, followed by an urgent response from the competent authorities. Many citizens were exposed to these forms of manipulation and abuse, and members of the Roma community in particular.

We must not view these phenomena solely as isolated electoral irregularities – they are the consequence of a far deeper and longer-standing social problem. In their arrogance, the leaders of the outgoing government and their supporters do not shy away from abusing existing relations of social and economic power. In circumstances produced by long-standing, systematic and systemic discrimination, a relationship of dependence is created that makes Roma men and women particularly vulnerable to political manipulation and pressure.

Long-term exposure to uncertainty and threats to their livelihood, together with a lack of trust in institutions and the inability to rely on them, deepens a sense of powerlessness and significantly narrows the space for making independent decisions. In such conditions, the promise of immediate material help on the one hand, and the threat of losing rights and having assistance taken away on the other, are pressures that are extremely difficult to resist.

We understand that a formally secret ballot does not in itself guarantee real freedom of political choice. Real freedom exists only when every citizen can make a decision without fear of consequences for their livelihood. Responsibility must therefore not be shifted solely onto people who have found themselves in a position of vulnerability. We see and understand the pressure to which members of the Roma community were subjected. **Responsibility must be directed at those who knowingly exploit their economic and social position and vulnerability to pursue their own political interests.**

Testimonies from the field point to possible abuse of the positions of Roma coordinators, presidents of local community councils and, of particular concern, employees of centres for social work. There are allegations that information about the social status, debts and needs of Roma families was used to identify them in a targeted way and to put pressure on them. The very people who should be a support and an intermediary for the Roma community in exercising its rights are turning its social and economic insecurity into an instrument of political control.

**We demand that the competent authorities examine, without delay, all testimonies and allegations of vote-buying, threats, abuse of public authority and unauthorised use of personal data.**

At the same time, we warn of the possibility that the outgoing regime, as it retreats and tries to reduce its own responsibility, may resort to staging an incident and try to portray the students and the Student List as enemies of the Roma community. Whether these scenarios are mere speculation or not, we want to make our message completely clear:

**The Student List stands unequivocally with the Roma community and all citizens who have been subjected to pressure and intimidation.** We believe we have the opportunity to build together a society in which every citizen will have a dignified life. Such a process is inseparable from the fight against discrimination.

**Your vote is yours and belongs to you alone.**

No one has the right to buy it or to make it a condition for exercising rights that are already yours.
