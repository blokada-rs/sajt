## Constituting the National Assembly

The final election results have been announced, and **the first session of the newly elected National Assembly has been scheduled for 17 November.**

What happens next, and how is parliament constituted?

## Final election results

On **5 November 2026**, the Republic Electoral Commission adopted its full report on the results of the parliamentary elections.

Of the 250 seats in total:

- **140** – Student List – Students Win
- **103** – Aleksandar Vučić – United Serbia
- **7** – national minority electoral lists

On **6 November, the REC issued its decision allocating the mandates** to the newly elected MPs.

This fulfilled the conditions for convening the first session of the newly elected National Assembly.

## When is the first session?

**17 November 2026 at 10:00**

The constitutive session was convened by the Speaker of the outgoing National Assembly, Ana Brnabić.

The session will be held in the House of the National Assembly in Belgrade.

Under the Constitution, **the first session must be held no later than 30 days after the final election results are announced.**

Until a new Speaker is elected, the session will be chaired by the oldest MP, Stojan Radenović.

## Confirming MPs' mandates

**The first item on the agenda is the confirmation of the newly elected MPs' mandates.**

The National Assembly sets up a three-member commission, which checks that the details in the MPs' certificates of election match the official report on the conduct of the elections.

Based on the commission's report, the chair declares which MPs have had their mandates confirmed.

**MPs acquire their rights and duties in the National Assembly on the day their mandates are confirmed.**

## What does constituting the Assembly mean?

Constituting the National Assembly marks **the official start of the new parliament's work.**

Mandates are confirmed on the basis of the election documents, so **not every newly elected MP has to be present in person** at the constitutive session for their mandate to be confirmed.

Once the number of mandates required by the Constitution has been confirmed, the National Assembly is considered constituted.

At that point, **the mandate of the outgoing parliament ends and the mandate of the new parliament begins.**

## Electing the Speaker

After the mandates are confirmed and the MPs take their oath, the Assembly moves on to electing a new Speaker.

A candidate for Speaker may be nominated by at least 30 MPs.

Election requires **a majority of all MPs, that is, at least 126 votes.**

With **140 seats**, the Student List has enough MPs to elect the Speaker on its own, provided that at least 126 of its MPs vote for the nominated candidate.

## What else is on the agenda?

In addition to confirming the mandates and electing the Speaker, the session on 17 November will also include:

- the election of the Deputy Speakers of the National Assembly;
- the appointment of the Secretary General;
- the election of members of the Assembly's working bodies;
- the election of members of the standing parliamentary delegations to international institutions.

These steps allow the new parliament to organise its work and carry out its legislative and oversight functions.

## What happens after the Assembly is constituted?

**Once the National Assembly is constituted, the 90-day constitutional deadline for electing a new Government begins to run.**

The President of the Republic nominates a candidate for Prime Minister after consulting representatives of the electoral lists that won seats.

The Prime Minister-designate then presents the Government's programme and proposed members, and the National Assembly votes on the Government's election.

With **140 seats, the Student List holds an absolute parliamentary majority** and enough MPs to vote in the new Government on its own.

This secures the parliamentary conditions needed for the Government's election, which will follow the procedure laid down in the Constitution.
