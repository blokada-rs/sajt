Dear citizens of Serbia, dear fellow MPs,


It is no chronological accident, but a kind of historical necessity, that today, on the solemn and fateful occasion of the constitution of the National Assembly of the Republic of Serbia, the supreme representative body and the institutional expression of the people's will, we also mark International Students' Day. This coincidence of dates goes beyond mere protocol; it takes on the force of an existential and institutional covenant, a point where living historical conscience and statesmanlike responsibility meet.

It lends this historic moment particular ethical and axiological weight, since it gathers within it a profound lesson of civilisation and puts before us, as the direct bearers of popular sovereignty, the fundamental question of political and moral teleology: **what institutions are we founding, which values are we giving legitimacy to, and what kind of political community, as a space of freedom and justice, are we leaving as a legacy to the generations to come?**

17 November was not established merely as an occasional celebration of youth, academic life, and education. It is a day of active historical memory and moral vigilance, dedicated to those generations of students who, in pivotal and tragic eras, facing brutal forms of totalitarian and authoritarian repression, defended with their own lives the inalienable principles of freedom of thought, the autonomy of the mind and of the university, the right to civil resistance, and the inviolability of human dignity. Their sacrifice bears witness that **knowledge is, above all, an obligation to act in the name of truth and freedom.**

By their very spiritual and anthropological nature, students are the most sensitive barometer of the intellectual and moral health of any society. Precisely because they are not yet bound by the inertia of pragmatism and the compromises of everyday life, they remain the bearers of that creative restlessness without which every social and political order inevitably collapses into stagnation. Wherever young people are guaranteed the space to think freely, to methodically doubt ossified dogmas, and to critically question and challenge every authority that does not draw its legitimacy from reason and justice, there exists living hope and a community's capacity for self-examination and for spiritual and moral renewal.

## The historic significance of the student movement

The historic reach of the student movement in our homeland is not exhausted by the demands it made or by the change in the political architecture it brought about. Its true greatness lies in having achieved a deeper, civilisational, ontological turn in our people's collective self-awareness: it shattered the paralysing, fatalistic illusion that citizens are inevitably helpless in the face of legalised, institutionalised injustice; it radically delegitimised the usurping claim of alienated political castes to treat the sphere of the public good as their own patrimonial, privatised preserve; and finally, **it restored to education its original, classical, and emancipatory meaning**: not to shape submissive, instrumentalised subjects of the system, but autonomous, morally responsible individuals, capable of thinking critically, judging, and changing the world they live in.

The Student List won **140 seats**, earning the direct trust of more than **2,350,000 citizens**. In the cold arithmetic of politics, such an outcome represents a tectonic shift and a radical redistribution of power; but in the deontology and ethics of public speech, it is for us a sacred burden of debt and moral responsibility for the trust that has been placed in our keeping as a covenant.

## Nomination for Speaker of the National Assembly

In laying the foundations of a new political culture and a new ethical code for this high house, our first, most urgent, and most exalted duty is to nominate the Assembly's leadership with full intellectual, moral, and statesmanlike responsibility. At the head of the highest legislative institution, we must place a person whose authority rests not on the naked levers of party coercion, hierarchical obedience, and clientelism, but on unimpeachable moral integrity, uncompromising scientific honesty, and deep spiritual roots in the noblest ethos of our people.

It is therefore my great honour and historic obligation, on behalf of the parliamentary group "Student List – Students Win", to propose that our esteemed university teacher, **Assistant Professor Dr Aleksandar Čupić**, be elected Speaker of the National Assembly of the Republic of Serbia.

We are proposing a man in whom two fundamental existential and ethical pillars of our nation are organically united: on the one hand, the archetypal figure of the Serbian *domaćin*, in Serbian custom, the head of the household who keeps his home and community on steady and unshakeable foundations of care, responsibility, love, and fairness; and on the other, the rigorous, exacting, and steadfast bearing of a scientist and professor whose thought is devoted, with discipline, to the impartial search for truth.

Our candidate is no epiphenomenon of political calculation and pragmatic bargaining, but **an authentic thinker and teacher who has devoted his academic vocation to the emancipation of the mind**: to shaping autonomous, critically aware, and free individuals, not faceless, instrumental servants of the political order.

Unlike the previous era of institutional degradation, when the presiding officer of this hallowed house served as a mere technocratic mechanism for legalising and amnestying lawlessness and for a perfidious simulation of parliamentarism, today we are choosing a person whose moral imperative and conscience will never allow him to close his eyes in cowardly silence in the face of the suspension of rights and brutal violence against our young people. He will certainly not be a passive, conformist bystander to the dismantling of the constitutional order, nor a silent accomplice in the enslavement of the critical mind and of free public speech.

As a thinker and a *domaćin*, he carries within him that rare, classical statesmanlike virtue: the prudent, practical wisdom needed to steer the ship of state in pivotal times and to guide the homeland towards a horizon of social justice, legal certainty, institutional renewal, the non-selective rule of law, and the profound moral regeneration of the nation. His whole personality is a living embodiment of the classical ideal of *kalokagathia*, the perfect unity of ethical beauty, inner virtue, and outward dignity, through which his entire bearing far transcends mere outward appearance.

Tall and well built, with a naturally and effortlessly authoritative presence, a long beard that calls to mind the figures of the old enlighteners, thinkers, and folk sages, and a noble, perceptive gaze that reflects a deep anthropological understanding of human beings and their fragility, he does not, by his spiritual make-up, belong among those who seek political strength in deafening noise, demagoguery, and shouting each other down. Although gifted with a calming, resonant voice that carries the weight and gravity of a statesman's word, he consciously chooses the path of restraint, quiet, and unobtrusiveness, deeply convinced that **authentic authority in the highest representative house does not spring from the crude imposition of force and procedural power, but from moral integrity, the superiority of reasoned argument, and that fruitful silence which, in a storm of passions, calls for reason, measure, and dialogue.**

## The National Assembly as a constitutional beacon

His assumption of this sacred duty will be a steadfast institutional and moral guarantee that the National Assembly will never again be a gladiatorial arena of political nihilism, where naked force, repression, and violence against the country's own citizens are triumphantly greeted with ovations, but a true constitutional beacon and a temple of legality.

The National Assembly will be a public space in which **the human dignity of every individual, in keeping with Kant's categorical imperative, is an absolute, inviolable value and an end in itself**, and never a mere means of political manipulation; an order in which the legal norm applies universally and without exception to everyone, regardless of the hierarchy of power, office, or social standing; and finally, a political community in which the constitutional autonomy of the university, the freedom of the critical and dialogical mind, and the sovereignty of the citizen are protected as the highest, inalienable, and ultimate public interest.

## Aleksandar Čupić's academic and scientific work

Aleksandar Čupić is an assistant professor at the Faculty of Transport and Traffic Engineering of the University of Belgrade, where he works in the Department of Postal Traffic and Networks. His scientific and professional focus covers the operation, automation, and computerisation of postal traffic, and in his research he deals in particular with multi-criteria decision-making, the optimisation of complex systems, and the application of advanced analytical methods in logistics and transport networks. It is precisely this scientific discipline, the ability to find an optimal, rational, and fair solution in complex systems with many competing parameters, that represents valuable capital for leading a pluralist parliament.

As the author and co-author of numerous relevant scientific papers on the optimisation of postal systems, process automation, and the application of multi-criteria decision-making methods, he has covered innovative and complex topics in his publications. These include, in particular, the use of robotics and automated systems in postal processing centres, a systems analysis of combined postal delivery models, the design and structuring of transport networks using evolutionary computation algorithms, and the mathematical and spatial optimisation of post box locations so that the public service is accessible to all citizens.

In addition to his direct research work, Čupić has for more than two decades been a member of the organising committee of the international PosTel symposium, devoted to new technologies in postal and telecommunications traffic, which traditionally brings together leading experts from academia and industry for dialogue, knowledge exchange, and the advancement of the profession.

He also confirmed his academic integrity as a signatory of the "University Support" initiative, through which professors and teaching staff at universities in Serbia stood publicly and uncompromisingly behind the students' demands for a fundamental improvement of the education system and the preservation of the constitutional autonomy of the university.

## Family, work, and solidarity with the students

However, the true profile of a statesman is not exhausted by a mere catalogue of academic references and theoretical knowledge; it proves its full worth only in living, existential contact with reality. Aleksandar Čupić is a man deeply, organically, and directly rooted in the real, everyday life of our people, in their existential worries, their hard work, and their challenges.

As **the father of six children**, he and his wife, a doctor, run a family household in Sopot, where they are devoted to organic farming. His daily, hands-on work with the land is not merely an economic activity but an authentic practical ethic: by its very nature, it demands humility before the laws of nature, wise patience as the fruit ripens, and a deep intergenerational responsibility towards the land, our ancestors, and future generations.

He has shown his devotion to family values, the work ethic, and the common good of the community from the very dawn of the student struggle, not as a silent observer at a safe academic distance, but as a genuine participant and moral witness. He expressed his solidarity with the students not through empty, declaratory rhetoric, but through a direct, active deed of love and sacrifice: together with his family, he threw open the doors of his household and his greenhouses in Sopot, taking in under his roof the students who were walking to Kragujevac on their exhausting march towards freedom, and offering them refreshment, safe shelter, and that ancient, sacred virtue of Serbian hospitality and love for one's fellow human beings.

In all his public and private conduct, he is marked by rare inner composure, stoic patience, dialogical reasonableness, and quiet determination, imbued with a deeply hermeneutic ability to truly listen to others, understand different perspectives, and approach decisions of general importance thoughtfully, without passion or haste.

Through his public appearances to date and his consistent civic engagement, as one of the founders and signatories of the citizens' group "Student List – Students Win", he has become known to the widest circle of citizens, earning their undivided respect and trust as a man of rare moral wholeness, whose *logos* and *ethos* do not diverge: a man who gives full weight to the words he speaks with his own life.

## A call to fellow MPs

Dear fellow MPs, today we face a historic test of this generation's maturity and a fateful choice between two irreconcilable principles of political existence: between force without reason and authority that springs from virtue.

By voting today to elect Assistant Professor Dr Aleksandar Čupić as Speaker of the National Assembly of the Republic of Serbia, **we are not casting our vote for just one man; we are raising our hands for the return of constitutionality, academic dignity, justice, and human measure to the very heart of the life of the state.**

I therefore call on you, free of all calculation and faithful only to your own conscience and to the covenant given to the citizens of this country, to support a man whose entire life and work bear witness that politics is possible as an exalted ethical activity: a man who, with his being, his mind, and his character, is a living embodiment of that immortal Kantian synthesis of cosmological reverence and the inner moral law: **the starry heavens above me and the moral law within me.**
