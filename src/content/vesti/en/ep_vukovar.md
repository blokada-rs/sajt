We disagree with the content of the European Parliament's draft resolution on the 35th anniversary of the fall and destruction of Vukovar. In our view, the document in its current form does not contribute to an objective understanding of historical events, nor to building trust and reconciliation among the peoples of the former Yugoslavia.

## Remembering all victims

The draft resolution almost entirely ignores the suffering of the Serbian civilian population in Croatia, including killings, persecution, the destruction of property, and mass displacement. Events such as Operations "Flash" and "Storm", as well as the crimes committed against Serbian civilians during the war, cannot be left out of a complete account of this historical period.

It is unacceptable for the complex processes of the break-up of the SFRY, inter-ethnic relations, and armed conflict to be reduced to a simplified picture of the collective responsibility of the Serbian people. **Responsibility for war crimes must be individual and established on the basis of evidence and final court judgments, not attributed to entire peoples.**

## Equal standards and regional cooperation

The issues of missing persons, opening archives, and prosecuting war crimes must be resolved through sincere regional cooperation, mutual respect, and equal standards for all states. Serbia has an obligation to cooperate in clarifying the fate of the missing and establishing responsibility for the crimes committed, but **the same approach must also apply to Serbian victims and the crimes committed against them.**

European institutions, which advocate reconciliation, tolerance, and shared European values, must be consistent in their attitude towards all victims and all historical facts. Remembrance of the past must not become a means of political pressure, nor be used to deepen existing divisions in the region.

## True reconciliation requires facing all crimes

Serbia and the Serbian people have the right to remember their own victims with dignity, just as all other peoples have the right to preserve the memory of their dead. **True reconciliation is not possible by imposing one-sided interpretations of the past, but only by honestly facing all crimes, regardless of who committed them and against whom.**

For all these reasons, we believe that the draft resolution in its current form does not contribute to a balanced culture of remembrance and to reconciliation. It misses the opportunity to address, in a complete, objective, and non-discriminatory way, the suffering of all peoples during the wars of the 1990s.
