Protecting mental health must also mean **protecting the human rights, safety, and dignity of every person.**

The death of Kristian Josip Periš in a psychiatric hospital in Zagreb raises serious questions about the accountability of institutions and the position of people who turn to them for help.

We extend our sincere condolences to his family and loved ones.

This tragic event must be a reason for **an open re-examination of institutional practices, professional accountability, and the way the rights of psychiatric service users are protected.**

Experiences of humiliation, intimidation, neglect, and various forms of violence within the mental health care system must not remain invisible, and services that should be places of safety and recovery must not become a source of further suffering and trauma.

That is precisely why **developing and improving policies and practices aimed at consistently protecting the rights, safety, and dignity of all users of mental health services in Serbia**, together with a gradual shift from a predominantly institutional model to community-based mental health care, remain among the main goals of the reforms set out in the student programme.

**Humanity, transparency, accountability, and expertise** must be the founding principles of such a system, while at the same time providing support to staff and ensuring closer oversight of how institutions operate.

**No institution may be above accountability, and no one should lose their dignity because they sought help.**
