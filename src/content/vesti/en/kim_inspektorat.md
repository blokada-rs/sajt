## Statement of the Student Movement on the Draft Law on the Central Inspectorate for Cultural Heritage in Kosovo and Metohija

The Student Movement expresses concern over the adoption, at first reading, of the Draft Law on the Central Inspectorate for Cultural Heritage in the Assembly of the so-called state of Kosovo, in particular because of the possible consequences of the new inspection powers for the protection of Serbian cultural and religious heritage.

## Protecting Serbian cultural and religious heritage

The protection of cultural heritage must be based on professional principles, the rule of law, and respect for legal guarantees already in place. Introducing new mechanisms of inspection oversight must not lead to a weakening of the special protection regime for Serbian Orthodox monasteries and churches, nor to the circumvention of existing procedures and powers provided for by the Law on Special Protective Zones.

Of particular concern is the fact that these regulations were adopted **without consulting the Serbian Orthodox Church**, whose religious buildings and property are directly covered by the proposed legal provisions, which directly diminishes the rights of religious communities and the protection of cultural heritage.

## A call to the international community and to the institutions in Pristina

We call on the relevant representatives of the international community to follow this process closely, bearing in mind the existing guarantees under international law for the protection of the Serbian Orthodox Church, its property, and its cultural heritage in Kosovo and Metohija, as well as the legally binding guarantees and obligations that Pristina has undertaken in the process of normalising relations with Belgrade.

We call on the competent institutions in Pristina to ensure, in the further legislative procedure, **that the proposed provisions are fully in line with these guarantees and protection mechanisms.**

## Legal certainty and dialogue

The preservation of cultural heritage must not be subject to unilateral institutional decisions that could undermine existing protection guarantees. It requires **legal certainty, professional responsibility, dialogue, and the participation of all stakeholders.**
