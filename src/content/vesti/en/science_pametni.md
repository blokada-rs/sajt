One of the most influential scientific journals in the world, *Science*, published an article on October 29, 2026, titled “Serbia just elected the world's brainiest parliament.”

The authors, Nenad Jarić Dauehauer and Mićo Tatalović, write about the victory of the Student List in the parliamentary elections and the generation of experts who will have the opportunity to put their knowledge in the service of society.

The text particularly highlights the diversity of professions and scientific fields from which the new deputies come. Among them are an astrophysicist who studies the evolution of galaxies, an electrical engineer who participated in designing robots, an organic chemist who worked on developing COVID-19 drugs during the pandemic, as well as researchers in education, sociologists, political scientists, economists, doctors and medical researchers.

The Student List won **141 of the 250 parliamentary seats**, and more than half of the candidates on the list hold a PhD or a medical degree. The authors point out that the new assembly faces the task of forming a government and determining a new direction for Serbia's development.

According to *Science*, the key promises of the Student List include fighting corruption, establishing a system based on knowledge and merit, accountability, and the rule of law. Plans for greater university autonomy, more stable funding for science, more accessible education, and stopping the brain drain from Serbia stand out.





The article also conveys the expectations of representatives of the academic community. The Rector of the University of Belgrade, Vladan Vucković, says, “Better times are ahead of us,” while scientists emphasize that universities must be free to think, research, and criticize without fear of political consequences.

The authors also point out that some of the newly elected MPs lack political experience and that the renewal of the scientific research sector will not be an easy or quick process. Nevertheless, the interviewees emphasize that the expertise of the new MPs can be significant for reforms and for solving Serbia's institutional and economic problems.

After years in which knowledge and expertise were pushed aside in favor of party membership, Serbia has shown the world a different face.

The face of people who have dedicated their lives to science, education, and society. People who have decided to put their knowledge in the service of the country they live in.

Behind this victory is not just 141 mandates. Behind it is a generation that decided to make knowledge valuable again, and to make Serbia a country one stays in and builds a life in, not one they leave.
