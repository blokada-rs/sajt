      The Government's decision to temporarily cut excise duties on petroleum products by 25 per cent expires on 8 November. If the measure is withdrawn and the full amount is passed on to retail prices, petrol will go up by more than 21 dinars and diesel by more than 22 dinars per litre.

      What can the current government do to prevent this?

      In the current institutional situation, the direct way to extend the existing measure is a new decision by the Government in its caretaker capacity, within its legal powers.

      The next steps to solve the problem in the long term are:

      \* National Assembly – amending the Law on Excise Duties and setting out a longer-term legal solution.

      \* Members of Parliament – opening a debate on fiscal policy and the impact of excise duties on citizens and businesses.

      \* Businesses and citizens – initiatives through the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Serbia, hauliers' associations, agricultural organisations and other relevant associations.

      Until then, it needs to be established who initiated the previous extension, on the basis of which analyses and fiscal estimates, and whether longer-term models were considered.

      Next, Serbia's three-year gas arrangement with Russia expired at the end of May 2025. Since then, supplies have continued through short-term extensions, that is, annexes to the contract.

      The latest arrangement, extended on 30 September 2026, is valid until 31 December 2026, on the previous terms.

      This way of agreeing short-term extensions does not provide enough predictability for the state, the economy and the energy system.

      The institutions responsible for negotiating on this issue are:

      \* The Ministry of Mining and Energy, for shaping energy policy and representing the state's interests.

      \* Srbijagas, as the contracting party and operational negotiator with Gazprom.

      \* The Government of the Republic of Serbia, for setting state policy and taking key decisions.

      The goal should be a longer-term and more stable solution.

      We need to try to secure a multi-year contract, for example for three years, with clearly defined quantities, prices, delivery schedules, flexibility and terms in the event of supply disruptions.

      At the same time, Serbia must develop alternative supply routes and sources.

      Just some of the possible alternatives are:

      \* Azerbaijan, to continue and increase gas purchases via the Southern Gas Corridor and the interconnector with Bulgaria.

      \* The Alexandroupolis LNG terminal in Greece, to use available capacity for buying and transporting liquefied natural gas.

      \* Hungary, to buy gas through the Hungarian transmission system, while checking the actual origin of the gas.

      \* Romania, preparing to use future production from the Neptun Deep field in the Black Sea and developing the interconnector with Serbia.

      \* North Macedonia and Greece, to develop additional interconnectors and access to alternative gas sources.

      It is important to distinguish the capacity of the transmission system from the quantities of gas actually contracted and available. Every alternative route must be analysed in terms of price, availability, transport capacity and reliability of delivery.

      On 30 October 2026, the US OFAC extended the licences covering three different activities:

      \* NIS – continued operations, oil refining, crude oil imports, maintenance and financial transactions.

      \* MOL – continued negotiations on buying the majority Russian stake in NIS.

      \* JANAF – continued transport of crude oil for the Pančevo Refinery.

      The new licences are valid until 11 December 2026.

      These licences do not mean the sanctions have been lifted. NIS still depends on decisions by US institutions, and a lasting solution is tied to a change in its ownership structure and approval of the proposed transaction.

      On 16 June 2026, the Government of Serbia signed an agreement with the MOL Group on the future management of NIS, which also provides for the state's ownership stake to increase by a further five percentage points. Completion of the transaction depends on an agreement between MOL and the Russian owners and on the necessary regulatory approvals.

      What needs to be done?

      1\. Clarify the transaction and the contractual obligations

      The documentation on the sale of the Russian stake to MOL must be obtained and analysed, including:

      \* the sale and purchase agreement and its key commercial terms;

      \* the price and method of payment;

      \* the future ownership structure, including any participation by ADNOC;

      \* the agreement between the Government of Serbia and MOL;

      \* Serbia's rights and obligations as a minority shareholder;

      \* the future owner's obligations towards the Pančevo Refinery, Petrohemija and the employees.

      2\. Make use of Serbia's existing representatives on the NIS Board of Directors

      In June 2026, the Ministry of Mining and Energy named the following as Serbia's members of the NIS Board of Directors:

      \* Dragutin Matanović – Deputy Chair of the Board of Directors;

      \* Irena Vujović – member;

      \* Milan Đurić – member;

      \* Dejan Radenković – member.

      These four are an important institutional channel for obtaining information about NIS's operations, within the limits of their powers and their duty to protect confidential information.

      Among them are people with publicly known political ties to the SNS and the SPS, while no party affiliation has been confirmed for Dragutin Matanović.

      We ask Serbia's members of the Board of Directors to answer the following questions:

      \* Are they aware of all the essential elements of the MOL–Gazprom Neft transaction?

      \* Do they have access to the sale and purchase agreement or a draft of it?

      \* Are they aware of the conditions OFAC has set for completing the transaction?

      \* What guarantees are there that the Pančevo Refinery will keep operating?

      \* What is the future of oil and gas exploration and production in Serbia?

      \* What rights will Serbia keep in the future ownership and management structure?

      3\. Establish direct communication with the relevant US institutions, especially the US Department of the Treasury and OFAC

      The aim is to understand the conditions for permanently resolving NIS's sanctions status, the timeline for decisions, and whether the transaction can be carried out without endangering Serbia's energy supply.

      4\. Understand the position of the Russian owners, the terms of sale and whether the transaction can be carried out without further legal and financial disputes.

      5\. Protect Serbia's strategic interests

      A change of ownership must not be the only measure of success. Serbia's long-term interests need to be agreed and protected with regard to:

      \* operations and investment at the Pančevo Refinery;

      \* the future of Petrohemija;

      \* oil and gas exploration and production;

      \* keeping expert staff and jobs;

      \* security of supply for the domestic market;

      \* the state's rights as a significant minority shareholder.

      6\. Prepare a fallback scenario

      Serbia must have a plan in case the transaction is not completed or OFAC does not extend the operating licence.

      That plan should cover alternative import routes for crude oil and petroleum products, state reserves, transport infrastructure, refinery operations and measures to keep the domestic market supplied.

      Besides urgently resolving the NIS question, a broader debate needs to be opened on the future of hydrocarbon exploration and production in Serbia.

      Serbia has significant geological data, a long tradition of exploration and existing infrastructure. However, the oil and gas exploration and production market is not open enough to new investors and international companies.

      A model needs to be considered that would allow new investors to come in, while protecting the state's interests and keeping appropriate control over the exploitation of natural resources.

      A state cannot be run from one day to the next, waiting for Washington to extend its permits. Serbia has the resources, the knowledge and the potential to become an energy-stable and predictable country, but that requires an open market, professional institutions and people who work in the interest of citizens.

      We have taken our first step; it is time for those currently in power to take their share of responsibility.

      Today we address the public as a political actor ready to take full responsibility for governing the country in the period ahead. We consider it our absolute duty to raise issues of fundamental public importance and to call for a full, urgent and transparent review of the operations of Elektroprivreda Srbije.

      These are not political questions, but questions of everyday life that directly matter to every person in this country.

      First of all, we need to look at the state of the power system. The fact is that our energy system is largely outdated and relies heavily on thermal power plants as the main source of electricity generation, while hydropower plants make up an important part of total generating capacity. A period of low river levels further limited hydropower generation, which once again showed how vulnerable the system is and how it lacks an adequate energy strategy.

      In addition, worn-out equipment, insufficient investment in maintenance and the lack of timely overhauls call into question the reliability of generating capacity. Examples include unit A2 at the Kostolac thermal power plant, which is out of operation, and TENT B2, whose major overhaul has been postponed for two years and which is running at reduced capacity.

      That is why we ask an entirely legitimate question: what condition are Serbia's power plants in, and why is the necessary money not being invested first and foremost in maintaining and modernising existing energy capacity? Can it be that, at a time when we face serious challenges in the energy sector, priority is given to projects such as the national stadium, while significant generating capacity remains out of operation?

      It is clear that we have to cover our electricity needs through imports. This has serious financial consequences, especially if imports are made at unfavourable market prices. According to the information available to us, a budget revision has been carried out to provide additional funds to cover demand until the end of the year. However, the question is whether those funds are sufficient, and for how long?

      As the government that has run the energy system for the past 14 years, the current leadership must take responsibility and answer several key questions. Why has the power system been neglected? Why is the public not given full and timely information about the state of generating capacity? Does EPS have sufficient funds to operate stably and secure the necessary quantities of electricity?

      The public has the right to know what condition one of the country's most important public enterprises is in, what the risks are for electricity supply, and whether there is a real possibility of additional costs or possible restrictions.

      The problems are being kept quiet, and putting off their resolution is being left to the future management, so that responsibility for the consequences of years of poor management can later be attributed to the new government. Such a scenario must not be allowed. The institutions and key resources of the state are still in the hands of the current government. Accordingly, responsibility for their current state cannot be shifted onto someone who has not yet taken over. The citizens of Serbia deserve to know how this situation came about and what the consequences are of the decisions made in previous years. The Minister of Mining and Energy, Dubravka Đedović Handanović, and the management of Elektroprivreda Srbije have an obligation to speak openly and transparently about the state of the company, the existing problems and the risks citizens could face.

      Over the past decade, partocracy has seriously damaged institutions and public enterprises in Serbia. Although our country has expert and competent people in the energy field, party interests have often been put ahead of the profession. Instead of decisions being made on the basis of knowledge, experience and professional standards, the interests of individuals and political structures played the decisive role. This attitude towards the profession has undermined the professional integrity of employees, discouraged good staff and limited opportunities for development.

      Energy independence is not a goal that can be achieved overnight.

      It is a long-term process that requires a strategy, serious investment and, above all, responsible, competent and qualified people. We want a system in which knowledge and professional integrity will be the main criteria for advancement, not party membership. A system that will give young professionals a reason to stay in Serbia, develop their potential and use their knowledge to contribute to the development of the companies that manage our most important national resources.

      Finally, we call on Dubravka Đedović Handanović and the management of Elektroprivreda Srbije to come before the public and say openly what the real situation in the company is, what problems it faces and what the projections for its operations and generation are until the end of the year.

      The citizens of Serbia have the right to know whether the electricity supply is secure and whether any consequences await us in the coming period, and if so, what they are.

      As you already know, on 30 October 2026 the National Bank of Serbia revoked Alta Banka's operating licence because the bank was undercapitalised and illiquid.

      However, Alta Banka's problems did not arise overnight, and they require a thorough investigation.

      There are indications that this situation results from the failure of the regulator and of the leadership of the National Bank of Serbia, headed by Governor Jorgovanka Tabaković, to react in time, as well as from the prosecution's failure to act and to freeze corruptly acquired assets.

      As regards the stability of the financial system, there are lawful ways to prevent further instability, and we urge that they be taken to preserve financial stability at this moment:

      \* Enhanced monitoring of the liquidity of all banks operating in Serbia.

      \* Readiness of the National Bank of Serbia to provide liquidity instruments (funds) to all solvent and soundly run banks, in order to preserve the stability of the healthy part of the banking system.

      \* Temporary measures at banks where it is established that there is an intention to bring them into an illiquid position.

      \* A rapid response by the competent authorities and the prosecution, to establish individual responsibility for abuses, corruption and lack of oversight.

      \* Timely informing of the public about the actions taken to preserve financial stability.

      As regards Alta Banka:

      \* Alta Banka's problems require a detailed investigation of its business over a number of years. In June 2026, the bank reported to the NBS a capital adequacy ratio of 17.92%, well above the statutory minimum. It must be established how its financial condition deteriorated, whether the financial statements faithfully reflected the risks, and whether the NBS reacted in time.

      \* The assets of the Alta Group must be examined. We demand a review of all large loans to related parties, of guarantees issued, and of the possibilities for collecting receivables or recovering unlawfully transferred funds, in order to give depositors and creditors the greatest possible protection.

      \* It must be established who made decisions about the money of public enterprises. In particular, we demand an explanation of the decision to move the collection of Elektroprivreda Srbije's bills to Alta Banka from July 2025, and of the terms on which EPS and other public enterprises did business with the Alta Group.

      \* Responsibility must be individual and complete. We demand an independent analysis of the loan portfolio and the publication of a chronology of the NBS's supervisory measures, so that the responsibility of the owners, the management and the regulator can be separated. If criminal offences are established, those responsible must be prosecuted, with penalties that reach 10 to 12 years in prison for the gravest forms of certain abuses.

      Today we repeat our call to all domestic and international banks operating in Serbia, and to their parent institutions, to step up the monitoring and control of cross-border financial transactions and potential capital outflows from the country.

      In particular, we urge that international payments, especially those involving public funds, state-owned enterprises and public institutions, be made only in accordance with the regulations in force, with the appropriate documentation, proof of the basis for payment and confirmation that contractual obligations have been fulfilled.

      We trust that the banking sector will continue to act in line with the highest standards of professional responsibility, in the interest of the financial stability of Serbia, its citizens and its international partners.

      Through people close to it, the outgoing regime has shown that it is willing to endanger the entire system, out of anger at citizens over losing the election. We will take all necessary measures to preserve the banking system, and we call on the NBS and all other institutions to take an active part in this process.

      We call on citizens to stay calm, and we also call on all analysts, experts and those who present themselves as such not to spread unverified and inaccurate information aimed at causing panic.

      For us this is not a question of politics, but a question of the state and its citizens.

## Press conference: key issues for Serbia's financial and energy stability

On Wednesday, 4 November, starting at 2 pm, we will hold a press conference at the "Miljenko Dereta" venue on current issues of importance for Serbia's financial and energy stability.

We will look at the situation in certain key sectors, the responsibility of the competent institutions, and decisions that may have direct consequences for citizens, the economy and the country's future. We will also present our positions and concrete steps for resolving open issues and protecting the public interest.

The speakers at the conference will be Dejan Radivojević and Slobodan Vukosavić.

We invite members of the press to attend the conference and hear our positions and proposals.
