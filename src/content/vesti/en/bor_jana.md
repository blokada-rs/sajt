To the Agency for Spatial Planning and Urbanism of the Republic of Serbia, Kralja Milutina 10a, 11000 Belgrade
<br />

### Request to suspend the Early Public Inspection of the Draft Spatial Plan for the Special Purpose Area for the exploitation of the "Jama Bor" copper and gold deposit

**Submitted by:** Candidates and contributors to the programme of the Student List – Students Win

On behalf of the **Student List – Students Win**, which won a majority of seats in the National Assembly of the Republic of Serbia in the parliamentary elections held on 25 October 2026, we address the Agency for Spatial Planning and Urbanism of the Republic of Serbia, as the body responsible for preparing the Spatial Plan for the Special Purpose Area of the "Jama Bor" Mine – Phase II of Mine Development, with the following request:

1. That the Agency immediately suspend the **EARLY PUBLIC INSPECTION concerning the preparation of the SPATIAL PLAN FOR THE SPECIAL PURPOSE AREA OF THE "JAMA BOR" MINE – PHASE II OF MINE DEVELOPMENT**, which began on 26 October 2026.
2. That the Agency, within its remit, submit an **initiative to the future Government of the Republic of Serbia to revoke the Decision on the preparation of the said spatial plan.**
3. That you **inform us in writing**, as soon as possible, of the action taken on this request.

<br />

### Statement of reasons

The outgoing government has unequivocally lost the trust of citizens, among other things because it sacrificed the public interest and public health for the profit of foreign investors. Of particular concern is the fact that the Agency began this early public inspection of a spatial plan with long-term environmental consequences only one day after the parliamentary elections. The Plan provides for the further development of the underground mine using the caving method beneath the old town centre of Bor, which contains residential, educational, healthcare, and religious buildings, as well as the town's infrastructure. The Plan also provides for the construction of a new flotation plant. According to the study prepared for the early public inspection, it also envisages increasing production to 18 million tonnes of ore per year, as well as the possible relocation of the village of Brezonik and of part of the Bor–Mala Krsna railway line because of expected ground movement.

We regard the haste with which the inspection was launched ahead of the change of government as an **irresponsible move that favours a foreign investor**, and as a continuation of the practice of adapting spatial planning to the individual interests of companies instead of setting clear conditions and limits for the development of mining.

According to the findings of international and domestic organisations, the mining activities of **"Zijin Copper"** in Bor and Majdanpek have already had devastating consequences for the environment and for citizens' rights and property. Numerous court judgments bear witness to this. In proceedings for economic offences, representatives of these companies justified their unlawful works by the urgency of expanding the mine and increasing production.

We expect **"Zijin Copper"** and **"Zijin Mining"** to conduct their activities in accordance with the laws of the Republic of Serbia from now on. Consistent compliance with environmental regulations and standards will be strictly monitored.

In addition, we wish to inform the officials responsible at the Agency for Spatial Planning and Urbanism that their work will be subject to a thorough review and to the establishment of responsibility, in line with the powers of the National Assembly and the Government.

### Conclusion

Far-reaching plans of this kind, which carry significant risks to the environment and to the quality of life of the citizens of Serbia, **should be decided on by the new National Assembly and the new Government.**

For the reasons stated, we call on the Agency to s**uspend the early public inspection without delay and to act on this request**.
