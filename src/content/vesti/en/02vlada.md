The constitutive session of the National Assembly began on 17 November, and on 19 November Aleksandar Čupić was elected its Speaker.

Six Deputy Speakers of the National Assembly were also elected, and Mirjana Radaković was appointed Secretary General.

The next important step in forming the new institutions is the election of the Government.

**Who nominates the Prime Minister-designate, how is the Government put together, and who decides on its election?**

## Who nominates the Prime Minister-designate?

Under the Constitution, the candidate for Prime Minister, the Prime Minister-designate, is nominated by the President of the Republic after consulting representatives of the electoral lists that won seats.

In the current circumstances, this power is exercised by Aleksandar Čupić, who, as Speaker of the National Assembly, is temporarily acting in place of the President of the Republic.

Under the Law on the President of the Republic, he is required to nominate a candidate who can secure the election of the Government.

**The Student List holds 140 of the 250 seats**, which allows it to secure a parliamentary majority without forming a coalition.

From the constitution of the National Assembly, the constitutional deadline of **90 days for electing the Government** begins to run.

## How is the Government put together?

The Prime Minister-designate proposes the programme and composition of the future Government, including the candidates for ministers and Deputy Prime Ministers.

Talks on candidates, the programme, and the allocation of portfolios can begin even before the Prime Minister-designate is formally nominated.

The Government consists of the Prime Minister, one or more Deputy Prime Ministers, and the ministers. It may also include ministers without portfolio.

The number and remit of the ministries are set by the Law on Ministries, which is passed by the National Assembly. Reorganising them requires an amendment to that law.

Members of the Government cannot be MPs at the same time. If a sitting MP is nominated as a minister, their mandate as an MP must end before they take up the post in the Government.

## How is the future Government presented to the Assembly?

The Prime Minister-designate sets out the future Government's programme before the National Assembly and proposes its composition.

The programme covers the goals, priorities, and measures that the future Government intends to implement.

At the same time, the candidates for Prime Minister and for members of the Government are presented.

After the presentation, a debate opens in the Assembly, during which MPs consider the proposed programme and composition of the Government.

Once the debate is over, the Speaker of the National Assembly sets a date for the vote.

## How is the Government elected?

The National Assembly votes at the same time on the Government's programme and on the election of the Prime Minister and the members of the Government.

This means that MPs do not vote on each minister individually, but on the proposal as a whole.

Electing the Government requires a majority of all MPs, that is, **at least 126 votes**.

Under the Rules of Procedure of the National Assembly, voting is secret unless the Assembly decides that it should be public. In that case, the vote is held by roll call.

With 140 seats, the Student List has the majority needed to elect the Government on its own, provided that at least 126 of its MPs support the proposed composition and programme.

## When does the new Government take office?

After the National Assembly elects the Government, the Prime Minister and the members of the Government take the oath of office before the MPs.

**The new Government's term officially begins on the day it takes the oath.**

This is followed by the practical handover of duties, during which the new ministers take charge of the ministries.

The new Government then begins implementing its programme and takes on responsibility for the work of the executive.

Its work is overseen by the National Assembly, to which the Government is accountable.

The election of the Government completes one of the key steps in forming the new institutions, while the procedure for electing the President of the Republic takes place separately.
