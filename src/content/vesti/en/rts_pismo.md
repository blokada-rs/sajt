**Open letter to the management and the Management Board of RTS, the Council of REM and the National Assembly of the Republic of Serbia**

To Whom It May Concern,

Based on the students' programme, which sets the unblocking and depoliticisation of RTS, and its liberation from undue political influence, as one of its key tasks, we demand that responsibility be taken for serving the public interest in the field of public information.

Citizens have the right to truthful, complete, impartial and timely information on matters of public importance. When the public service broadcaster subordinates the exercise of this right to the interests of political power, it undermines public trust, deepens divisions in society and departs from its basic role.

The demands we are making are based on the principles of editorial independence, political and social pluralism, transparency of work and accountability of institutions, as established by the regulations on public information, public service media and electronic media.

### 1\. To the management, the news programme editors and the Management Board of RTS

We demand the immediate resignation of the Director General of RTS, his deputy, the editors-in-chief responsible for the news programme and all editors of that programme.

We base this demand on our assessment that the management and editors, through their actions and their failure to protect professional standards, have seriously undermined trust in the public service broadcaster. We assess their responsibility in line with each person's individual powers over the organisation of work, programme editing, the protection of editorial independence and the pursuit of the public interest.

The reasons for our demand are:

1. **\- Selective presentation of events of public importance.** In our assessment, silence about such events, late reporting on them and downplaying their significance deprive citizens of the chance to form a complete and credible picture of what is happening in society.

2. **\- Undermining political and social pluralism.** We consider that the privileged position of government representatives, and the insufficient space given to the critical public, to different social groups and to relevant independent experts, are not in line with the role of a public service broadcaster.

3. \**- Insufficient critical scrutiny of the work of public office holders.** A public service broadcaster should check officials' claims, ask questions about their accountability and investigate matters of public importance. In our assessment, passing on the government's messages without proper verification and context is a serious departure from professional standards.

4. \- **Inadequate presentation of the students' demands and of citizens' discontent.** Students and other citizens have the right to have their views and demands presented accurately and in the appropriate context. Their side must also be heard, with respect for the rights of reply and correction provided for by law.

5. \- **Insufficient protection of personal dignity and a lack of critical distance from stigmatising messages.** We find it particularly unacceptable that insulting labels and messages aimed at students and other citizens because of their views have been passed on uncritically. We demand that the specific content be examined in light of the prohibition of incitement to discrimination, hatred or violence and the protection of personal dignity, taking into account the context in which it was published.

We demand that, after the resignations of the management and editors, the uninterrupted work of RTS be ensured under an interim management, whose composition would be proposed by the Student List in agreement with the representative trade unions of RTS, and whose appointment and powers would be regulated in accordance with the law.

The task of the interim management would be to ensure the continuity of the public service broadcaster's work, editorial independence and the protection of the professional dignity of journalists and other staff, until a new management is selected through a lawful and transparent procedure.

### 2\. To the Council of the Regulatory Authority for Electronic Media

We demand that REM act professionally, independently, promptly and without selectivity, and that all unjustified obstruction of its work stop.

We demand that four members of the REM Council publicly explain why, after previously expressing support, they voted against including Informer in election monitoring. We also ask for an explanation of reports that, after proceedings against this television station were launched unanimously, they disputed findings of the expert service to which they had previously raised no objections, while the Council did not adopt the decisions that would have concluded those proceedings.

Unless such changes of position are based on new facts and convincing reasoning, we regard them as an indication of possible obstruction and as grounds for examining suspicions of external pressure or instructions. We demand the publication of the relevant documentation and of the individual voting results, as well as a statement from these members on any influence on their decision-making.

We demand that the REM Council:

1. \- Consider complaints and conduct proceedings without undue delay, within the prescribed deadlines, and adopt reasoned decisions.

2. \- Ensure effective oversight of the media service providers within its remit, including public service media and commercial providers, applying the regulations consistently and taking into account the specific legal obligations of public service media.

3. \- Examine allegations of incitement to discrimination, hatred or violence, violations of personal dignity and other breaches of the regulations, and, where it finds that the legal conditions are met, impose the measures within its powers and take the other actions provided for by law.

4. \- Ensure transparency of its work and the availability of minutes, voting results, decisions, the reasoning behind them and oversight reports, to the extent and in the manner provided for by law, while respecting data protection and other interests protected by law.

We ask that, within seven days of receiving this letter, you provide us with a public and reasoned statement on these demands, including information on the steps taken and planned, and their deadlines. This is the deadline we request for a reply to this letter; it does not replace the statutory deadlines for action in individual cases.

For us, the depoliticisation of REM means independent decision-making, resilience to political and other undue influence, and consistent application of the law. The regulator must be held accountable with full respect for its institutional independence and for the autonomy of the members of its Council.

### 3\. To the National Assembly of the Republic of Serbia

We inform the public that, as part of the preparations for the constitution of the new convocation of the National Assembly, the Student List candidates will be instructed to make it a priority of their parliamentary work to initiate the procedure for filling the vacant seats on the REM Council and to ensure that it has its full complement of nine members.

We will work to ensure that the procedure is lawful and transparent, and based on the expertise and independence of the candidates, with respect for the rights of the authorised nominators. The public will be kept informed of the steps taken and the deadlines.

The aim is an independent and effective regulator that applies the law free from political pressure and from interference in decisions on individual cases.

Belgrade, 27 October 2026
