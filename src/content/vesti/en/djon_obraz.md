Outgoing Minister of Agriculture Dragan Glamočić is leaving agriculture in a difficult position. For years, his statements widened the gap between the state and farmers, and now he claims he is not responsible for decisions that directly threaten their livelihoods.

Subsidies for crop production have not been paid, and all five of the City of Belgrade's public calls for subsidies have been cancelled, even though the preliminary and final ranking lists had already been published.

Glamočić cited the Government's caretaker status and the outgoing government's lack of political legitimacy as the reason for postponing the payments. However, **caretaker status does not release the Government and the ministries from institutional responsibility.**

Decisions made earlier must be implemented, and obligations must be paid according to the set schedule. The funds for subsidies have already been provided for in the budget, and the payment deadlines were set before the Government became a caretaker government.

Cancelling the City of Belgrade's calls puts farmers at even greater risk, as they will have to use the available legal remedies to protect their rights. That means lengthy proceedings and extra costs for people who already bear most of the production risk.

By acting this way, the outgoing government is putting political interests ahead of farmers' livelihoods and undermining trust in institutions.

Agriculture is Serbia's national interest and the foundation of its food independence.

Farmers must be strategic partners of the state, not a welfare category whose livelihood depends on the political will of those in power.

That is why we will establish **an "Ombudsman of the Republic of Serbia for the Protection of Land, Water Resources, and Food Independence", as well as local agricultural parliaments and a national umbrella Agricultural Parliament.**

They will enable institutional dialogue between farmers, buyers, storage operators, and processors, which is essential for regulating the market and creating fairer conditions for production and sales.

Agriculture **must not be a tool of political manipulation, and people's livelihoods must not be a means of blackmail.**

Public money is no one's private property.

**A stable, developed, and strong agriculture – a free, independent, and strong Serbia!**
