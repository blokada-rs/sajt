According to the Higher Public Prosecutor's Office in Niš, Đorđe Aleksić, Dušan Ljubić, and Miloš Ljubić, the director and owners of the company "Vodogradnja", have been arrested on suspicion of having siphoned tens of millions of dinars out of their own company over a period of years, while at the same time winning contracts in municipal tenders.

## Public money in the hands of family and party networks

While people in southern Serbia are told every day that there is no money for basic infrastructure, safety, and a dignified life, tens of millions of dinars end up in networks of family and party interests. Cases like this are not isolated incidents; they point to the consequences of a system in which institutions are subordinated to the interests of politically connected individuals.

The fact that those arrested are the closest relatives of the Mayor of Doljevac Municipality raises further questions about how local governments have for years operated as private property. Public money flowed into private pockets through tenders and deals with the municipality, while institutions failed to react in time.

## Power maintained through pressure and intimidation

This mechanism was kept going not only by money and tenders, but also by open intimidation, pressure, and threats. People who opposed the regime or challenged such arbitrary rule in any way were subjected to attempts to silence them. Fear was used as a means of preserving local centres of power and preventing any serious criticism.

When public office is used to protect private interests, and political and family ties become more important than the law, it is citizens who end up paying the price.

## The election victory is not the end of the fight for change

Although the Student List won the elections, **not all the levers of power have been taken over yet**. The SNS government is in a caretaker capacity, and Serbia still faces presidential elections. That is why the election victory is not the end of political change, but the beginning of a process in which institutions at all levels must be freed from party control and put at the service of citizens.

Arrests in cases like this show that questions of accountability are now being raised even within structures that operated for years under SNS rule. However, individual arrests are not enough. The entire chain of responsibility must be established, including those who made the decisions, enabled the abuses, or protected those involved.

**Change must mean that no one will be above the law ever again – not because of their office, their surname, or their party membership card.** Everyone found in lawful proceedings to have abused public resources, rigged tenders, and damaged the citizens' budget must answer for it before free and independent institutions.
