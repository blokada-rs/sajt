**The contract has been terminated, but the fight for Generalštab is not over yet.**

Two years after the Government of the Republic of Serbia, contrary to legal procedures and expert opinion, decided to lift the protection of the Generalštab complex, the former Yugoslav Army General Staff building, the contract with Jared Kushner's company has been officially terminated. According to a statement from the competent ministry, the contract was terminated with no mutual claims.

This is a great victory for citizens, students, experts, and everyone who persevered over the past two years in defending one of the most important works of our modern architecture. **Generalštab has become a symbol of resistance to the arbitrary use of power, the dismantling of institutions, and the disposal of public assets outside the law and against the interests of citizens.**

However, **terminating one contract does not undo the consequences of all the decisions** that made the attempt to demolish Generalštab possible.

## Full transparency and legal protection

We demand that **all contracts, agreements, Government conclusions, and other documents** relating to this project, including the documents on its termination, be made available to the public without delay.

One of the first tasks of the new National Assembly must be **to repeal the special law (*lex specialis*) on Generalštab**. All steps provided for by law must also be taken to remedy the consequences of the Government's decision of November 2024 and to ensure full legal protection for the complex.

We will work to ensure that Generalštab, in line with expert assessment and legal procedures, **receives the cultural heritage status it deserves.**

## Establishing accountability

At the same time, it must be possible to establish, without obstruction, the accountability of everyone who took part in any unlawful actions. **Terminating the contract cannot be a reason to give up on establishing accountability.**

## Restoring Generalštab and its future use

Our goal is not only to prevent the demolition of Generalštab, but to ensure **its restoration**. It must be based on expert research, modern conservation principles, and a public debate on the future use of the complex.

**Generalštab will stay where it is, be restored, and be open to the citizens who defended it.**

Its defence showed that citizens can stop decisions that threaten the public interest. Its restoration should show that the state can once again become a responsible guardian of cultural heritage.

**Generalštab has been defended. Now it is time for it to be legally protected and restored.**
