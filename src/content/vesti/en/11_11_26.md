On the eleventh day of the eleventh month of 1918, after the signing of the armistice in a railway carriage in Compiègne, a ceasefire came into effect in Europe at eleven o'clock. Four years of the deadliest destruction were brought to an end with an epilogue of over 16 million military and civilian deaths and more than 20 million wounded.

The Central Powers were defeated militarily; some of them capitulated, while others even collapsed. Four empires disappeared, and in their wake, new states were born and a new order, the Versailles order, was established. The Kingdom of Serbia was the greatest casualty of the war and, proportionally, suffered the highest number of casualties. It lost almost a third of its population, and around 60 per cent of its adult male population did not live to see the end of the war. 

Men fell to bullets and bayonets, typhus and other diseases, during the Albanian Golgotha and at the hands of the occupying forces. The Serbian soldiers secured the liberation together with their allies. With the breakthrough of the Salonika Front, in a very short time, they liberated an entire nation.
