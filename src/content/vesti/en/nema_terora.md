**The Higher Public Prosecutor's Office in Belgrade has dropped the indictment against nine students accused of preparing criminal offences against the constitutional order and security of the Republic of Serbia.** The decision came after the courts ruled that part of the evidence, obtained through special evidentiary actions, was unlawful. The prosecution concluded that there is no longer enough evidence to continue the proceedings.

For a year and a half, our colleagues were dragged through the regime media and branded terrorists and enemies of the state, while the charges against them were presented as unquestionable truth.

Now, after all of that, the court has found that the evidence on which part of the indictment rested was obtained unlawfully. The excluded evidence was the only evidence against one of the students, and the only basis for part of the indictment in these proceedings. On the strength of that evidence, our colleagues were portrayed for months as a threat to the state, while the public was assured that their guilt had practically been proven.

Why did this take so long, and who will answer for the consequences the students have suffered all this time?

**We have known from day one that our colleagues are not guilty**. We knew it while they were being called terrorists, while their photos were being shown on television, and while they were being turned into enemies of the state. Today, when the prosecution admits that it does not have enough evidence to continue the proceedings, we refuse to let this end as if nothing had happened.

**Consistent application of the Constitution and laws of the Republic of Serbia should have protected them from the very beginning**. The lawfulness of evidence is not a matter of political timing, nor something that may depend on who is in power. The courts are obliged to rule on the basis of the law, not under pressure from politicians and media who handed down their verdicts long before them.

Who will answer for a year and a half of public targeting? Who will answer for destroying our colleagues' reputations, for the fear they and their families were exposed to, and for everything they had to endure because of charges the prosecution can no longer sustain?

We are not asking the court for mercy. **We are asking for justice**. Justice for the nine students, and for all our colleagues who ended up in the dock of the former regime without grounds.

Withdrawing the indictment does not end the responsibility of those who took part in their persecution. A year and a half of their lives cannot be erased by a single decision.

**Our colleagues did not deserve to wait for the law to start protecting them. They deserve justice, and we will not give up on it.**
