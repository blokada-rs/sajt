**Revolutionary terror: how are unrelated events linked into a story of an organised threat, and political opponents cast as the ones behind it?**

## Fear as a campaign message

In mid-October, during the election campaign, a story began to spread about a "revolutionary terror" that would supposedly follow a victory by the Student List.

Citizens are given the impression that a change of government could lead to violence, political persecution and a loss of freedom.

No adequate evidence has been presented for the claim that the Student List is planning organised violence. Even so, this assumption became part of the campaign messaging.

## How do the campaign warnings "come true"?

After the Student List's election victory, the same narrative takes on a new role.

Demands for resignations, dismissals and scrutiny of how institutions work are presented as proof that the predicted persecution has already begun.

Among the examples Informer calls "revolutionary terror" is the initiative to revoke that television station's licence.

However, the initiative was submitted to the REM by 32 organisations and signatories, who asked the regulator to act on allegations of breaches of the law and of programming obligations.

## When the question of accountability is called a "witch hunt"

After the election, the persecution narrative extends to questions about the possible accountability of former office-holders.

Public debate about responsibility for the canopy collapse is presented as an organised witch hunt against Aleksandar Vučić.

This interpretation shifts attention to supposed political persecution, while questions of fact and accountability are pushed into the background.

The existence of political debate, criticism or demands for an investigation does not in itself prove that anyone is being treated unlawfully. Establishing responsibility requires evidence and lawful proceedings.

## When even the work of the judiciary becomes "persecution"

The case of the headmaster of Novi Sad's Jovina Grammar School has also been drawn into the story of "revolutionary terror".

His arrest has been presented as political persecution and as pressure by the new government on the judiciary.

The key context is left out: the proceedings were opened on suspicion that the headmaster had failed to comply with final court rulings ordering four teachers to be reinstated.

## When every new event becomes part of the same story

By 29 October, the term "revolutionary terror" was being used for an ever wider range of events, including disputes in educational institutions.

In this way, the dispute over the election of the Dean of the Faculty of Medicine was also presented as another case of "revolutionary terror".

The faculty's management made serious allegations about the disputed decision on the election of the dean. Those allegations need to be examined in the proper procedure.

## Who is presented as the protector of Serbia?

The "revolutionary terror" narrative takes on a new role on 29 October, with the announcement that the SNS parliamentary group will be called "Serbia Against Terror".

The earlier accusations of persecution become the basis for the party's political positioning.

By choosing such a name, the SNS presents itself as the protector of the whole country, while its political opponents are linked to a threat from which Serbia must be defended.

In this way, the interests of one political organisation are symbolically tied to the interests of the whole of society, and the existence of "terror" is taken for granted in the name itself.

## Let us not allow fear to prevail over the truth

We believe the citizens of Serbia can recognise political manipulation and attempts to spread fear.

We have said many times that we will carry our struggle through to the end peacefully, non-violently and with dignity. We stand by that.

After the Student List's election victory, we will continue to respect the will of the citizens and to work for independent institutions and responsible media. Public service broadcasters and television stations with national frequencies must serve the public interest and provide truthful and impartial information.

We call on you not to accept accusations without evidence, to look at information critically, and to recognise attempts at political manipulation.

For a society in which institutions do their job and citizens have the right to the truth – without fear and without giving up.
