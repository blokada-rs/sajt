<section>
The Student List has won.

**The Student List won the parliamentary elections, taking 140 of the 250 seats in the National Assembly.** Electing a new Government requires 126 votes.

## So why hasn't the new Government started work yet?

In the elections on 25 October, citizens elected Members of Parliament, not a prime minister and the sectoral ministers. That is why the Student List, even though it has a majority, cannot take power straight after election day.

First, the new National Assembly has to start work. It will confirm the mandates of the elected MPs, and then elect the Speaker of the Assembly. A candidate for prime minister will be proposed, and the Assembly will vote on the new Government.

All of this has to be done according to the rules that apply to every electoral list, no matter how many votes it won.

Below we explain what happens next, how long the process may take, and whether anyone can prevent the Student List from forming a Government.
</section>

<section>
<div class="details-list">

<details>
<summary>**1.** Now that the RIK has announced the results, what are we waiting for?</summary>
<div class="content">
![](@assets/images/1000002856.webp)

<br />

On 5 November, the Republic Electoral Commission (RIK) adopted the overall report on the results of the parliamentary elections.

**The Student List won 140 seats.** The "Aleksandar Vučić – United Serbia" list won 103, while the remaining seven seats went to national minority lists.

Next comes the allocation of mandates to the elected candidates. The RIK has ten days from the publication of the overall report to decide which candidates from the electoral lists receive seats.

Each elected candidate receives a certificate of election as a Member of Parliament.

After that comes the first session of the National Assembly, at which these mandates are confirmed.
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**2.** When will the new Assembly convene?</summary>
<div class="content">
![](@assets/images/1000002808.webp)

<br />

The first session of the new Assembly is convened by the Speaker of the previous Assembly. In this case, it's Ana Brnabić.

Under the Constitution, **the first session must be held no later than 30 days after the final election results are announced.**

This is the latest permitted deadline, which means the session can also be convened earlier.

At this session, the mandates of the elected candidates of the Student List, as well as those of candidates from other lists, will be confirmed.
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**3.** What happens at the first session of the Assembly?</summary>
<div class="content">
The first session is chaired by the oldest elected MP. If they cannot or do not wish to chair the session, the next oldest MP present takes their place.

They are assisted by the Secretary General of the Assembly and the four youngest MPs, one from each of the four lists that won the most seats.

At the start of the session, a three-member committee is elected. It checks whether the MPs' certificates of election match the official election results.

Once this check is complete, **the MPs' mandates are confirmed and the MPs take their oath.**

The Speaker of the National Assembly is then elected and takes over the chairing of the session. The Deputy Speakers are also elected and the Secretary General is appointed.
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**4.** When does the new Assembly officially start work?</summary>
<div class="content">
The National Assembly has 250 MPs.

For the new Assembly to be officially constituted, the mandates of **at least 167 MPs** must be confirmed.

This is called the constitution of the Assembly.

From that moment, the term of the new Assembly begins and the term of the MPs from the previous Assembly ends.

**This is also when the 90-day deadline for electing a new Government starts to run.**
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**5.** Can a boycott prevent the Student List from constituting the Assembly?</summary>
<div class="content">
No, as long as the Student List ensures that enough MPs are present and follows the prescribed procedure.

Holding the first session requires the presence of **at least 126 MPs.** This is called a quorum.

The Student List has 140 seats, which means it can secure enough MPs present on its own.

For example, if 140 Student List MPs attend the session and the other 110 decide not to, the session can still go ahead.

MPs do not have to be in the chamber in person for their mandates to be confirmed. That is why the absence of MPs from other lists does not prevent the Student List from taking part in constituting the Assembly.
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**6.** How is the Speaker of the National Assembly elected?</summary>
<div class="content">
A candidate for Speaker must be nominated by at least 30 MPs.

MPs then debate the candidates and vote.

Electing the Speaker requires **at least 126 votes.**

The Student List has 140 seats, so it can elect the Speaker on its own if at least 126 of its MPs vote for its candidate.

The Speaker then convenes sessions and runs the work of parliament.
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**7.** Who proposes the new prime minister?</summary>
<div class="content">
The candidate for prime minister is called the prime minister-designate.

The prime minister-designate is the person who proposes the future ministers and the Government's programme.

**They are proposed to the National Assembly by the President of the Republic, after consultations with representatives of the electoral lists.**

The law requires the President of the Republic to propose a candidate who can win enough votes for the Government to be elected.

Electing the Government requires 126 votes. The Student List has 140 seats and therefore has the majority needed to back its own candidate.
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**8.** Who is currently acting as President of the Republic?</summary>
<div class="content">
Aleksandar Vučić resigned as President of the Republic on 27 September.

Since then, the duties of the president have been carried out on an interim basis by Ana Brnabić, as Speaker of the National Assembly.

The Constitution provides that in such a situation, the President of the Republic is replaced by the Speaker of the National Assembly for a maximum of three months.

The person temporarily performing the duties of president also has the power to propose a prime minister-designate.

**That is why, in the current circumstances, the election of a new Speaker of the National Assembly also matters.**

Presidential elections are a separate procedure and must be held within the deadline set by the Constitution. **Forming a new Government does not have to wait for presidential elections to be held.**
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**9.** Can anyone refuse to propose the Student List's prime minister-designate?</summary>
<div class="content">
The law obliges the President of the Republic to propose a candidate who can secure the election of a Government.

The Student List has the majority needed to elect a Government, but its candidate also has to be formally proposed to the National Assembly.

There is one important point: **the law does not set an exact number of days within which the President of the Republic must propose a prime minister-designate after the Assembly is constituted.**

Delaying this step could therefore slow down the election of the Government.

If the President of the Republic refused to propose a candidate who has a majority, the question would arise of whether they were breaching their legal obligation. In that situation, the resulting legal dispute would have to be resolved.
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**10.** How are the prime minister and ministers elected?</summary>
<div class="content">
Once the prime minister-designate has been proposed, they come to the National Assembly.

There they present their programme and the people they propose as ministers. This programme is called the keynote address.

MPs debate the proposed Government and then vote.

**The whole Government is voted on at once. MPs do not elect each minister separately.**

For the Government to be elected, at least 126 votes are needed.

With 140 seats, the Student List has a sufficient majority to elect the Government on its own.

Future ministers do not have to be MPs. However, a person who becomes a member of the Government cannot remain an MP at the same time.

After the election, the prime minister and ministers take their oath before the National Assembly.

**On that day, the new Government officially starts work.**
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**11.** Who runs the country until all this is finished?</summary>
<div class="content">
Until the new Government is elected, the outgoing Government continues to carry out its duties.

It is in a so-called caretaker capacity. This means its regular term has ended, but it keeps working so that state services do not come to a halt.

Such a Government can only carry out day-to-day business.

This means that schools, hospitals, ministries and other state services keep working, and the state's obligations are met in accordance with the law.

A caretaker Government also has limits. It cannot routinely propose new laws or adopt regulations, except in special situations permitted by law, for example when there is a statutory deadline or an urgent need of the state.

It cannot appoint civil servants to senior positions, and it is restricted in other appointments too.

It also cannot propose the dissolution of the National Assembly.
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**12.** Can the outgoing Government refuse to hand over power?</summary>
<div class="content">
Once the National Assembly elects the new Government and its members take their oath, **the new Government takes office.**

This does not require the consent of the outgoing prime minister or ministers.

Handing over documents and work in the ministries may take some time, but it does not delay the start of the new Government's term.
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**13.** Can the Student List work in the Assembly while waiting for the new Government?</summary>
<div class="content">
Yes.

Once the new National Assembly is constituted, its MPs can work even before a new Government is elected.

**The Student List will be able to propose laws and take part in the National Assembly's decisions.**

So it is possible for the Student List to already have a majority in the Assembly while the outgoing Government is still carrying out its legally limited duties.
</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary>**14.** How much time is there to elect a new Government?</summary>
<div class="content">
From the day the new Assembly is constituted, **a 90-day deadline for electing the Government begins.**

This is the latest deadline. The new Government can also be elected earlier.

If the Assembly does not elect a Government within 90 days, under the Constitution it must be dissolved and new parliamentary elections called.

Those elections must be completed no later than 60 days after they are called.

That is why it is important for the procedure of proposing a prime minister-designate and electing the Government to be completed within the prescribed deadline.
</div>
</details>

</div>

<section>
## What happens next?

The Republic Electoral Commission has adopted the overall report on the election results. Next comes the allocation of mandates to candidates from the electoral lists.

After that, the first session of the National Assembly will be held, at which the mandates of the elected MPs will be confirmed.

Then the Speaker of the Assembly will be elected, a prime minister-designate proposed, and a vote held on the new Government.

**The Student List has the majority needed to elect a Government.** What remains before it takes office are the steps prescribed by the Constitution and the law.

**Winning the election decides who has the majority in the National Assembly. The Government starts work once that Assembly elects it and its members take their oath.**
</section>
