**The records must be preserved. Institutions must answer.**

The ALTA Bank case must not go unanswered, and important records must not be allowed to disappear before it is established what really happened and who is responsible.

That is why we are launching an initiative to submit requests for access to information of public importance to the competent institutions. The aim is to establish how they have handled ALTA Bank's records and electronic data, what the National Bank of Serbia found during its supervisory inspections, and what information the Administration for the Prevention of Money Laundering holds.

We call on all citizens to fill in and submit the prepared requests. **Anyone can submit them, without having to explain why they are asking for the information.**

</section>

<section>
## Why is it important to act now?

The National Bank of Serbia revoked ALTA Bank's operating licence on 31 October 2026, and the Commercial Court in Belgrade opened bankruptcy proceedings on 3 November 2026.

In these circumstances, records and information systems pass into the control of the bankruptcy administrator, equipment and archives are taken over, and staff leave. This is exactly when there is a risk of data being lost. It does not have to happen deliberately, but it can be prevented.

Article 95 of the Law on the Prevention of Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism provides not only for an obligation to retain certain data, but also for its deletion once the prescribed retention periods expire. That is why it must be established now whether the data has been preserved, where it is, and what measures have been taken to protect it.

**If we do not request the documents and official answers now, it may later be impossible to establish what was preserved and what was lost.**
</section>

<section>
## What are we asking for?

We have prepared four separate requests, addressed to three institutions that hold the relevant records or are responsible for keeping and overseeing them.

### 1. To the Deposit Insurance Agency

The Deposit Insurance Agency is acting as bankruptcy administrator, so it is important to get information from it on how it took over, and how it is keeping, ALTA Bank's records and information systems.

We are asking for information on **the acts governing the handling of electronic data, whether a forensic copy of the data has been made, the inventory of IT equipment and archives, and the measures taken to prevent their destruction, formatting, or removal.**

### 2. To the National Bank of Serbia

From the National Bank of Serbia, we are asking for the records on the **revocation of ALTA Bank's operating licence**, as well as information on the **supervisory inspections** carried out from 2020 until the licence was revoked.

We are particularly interested in inspections of the bank's **anti-money laundering systems**, their findings, any **irregularities** identified, and the measures or **sanctions** imposed on the bank.

We are also asking whether the National Bank of Serbia informed the **Administration for the Prevention of Money Laundering** or the competent prosecutor's office about the irregularities it found, whether it took over or secured copies of the bank's electronic data, and how it arranged the safekeeping of the records with the Deposit Insurance Agency.

### 3. To the Administration for the Prevention of Money Laundering

From the Administration for the Prevention of Money Laundering, we are asking for data on **the reports ALTA Bank submitted to it** concerning suspected money laundering or terrorist financing, broken down by year for the period from 2020 to 2026.

We are also asking for comparative aggregate data for all banks in Serbia, the number of orders to temporarily suspend transactions involving accounts at ALTA Bank, and information on cases in which data from the bank was passed on to other competent authorities.

In addition, we are asking **whether the Administration informed the National Bank of Serbia about irregularities**, whether it requested the preservation of records and electronic data after the licence was revoked, and whether it secured data from the bank's anti-money laundering software.
</section>

<section>
## What are these requests not asking for?

The requests have been drafted to concern **how the institutions acted and whether records were preserved**, not citizens' private data.

**We are not asking for data on individual clients, specific transactions, or personal data.** From the Administration for the Prevention of Money Laundering, we are asking for aggregate, statistical data, without revealing the identity of individuals or the content of specific reports.

The aim is to find out what the competent institutions did, what they established, what measures they took, and whether they secured records that may be important for establishing responsibility.
</section>

<section>
## How can you take part?

Below are four prepared requests. Each is addressed to a different institution and can be submitted separately. You can submit one, two, or all four.

Fill in the required details in the relevant form and follow the submission instructions. **You do not need to state your interest or the reason why you are asking for the information in order to submit a request.**

Once it receives a properly completed request, the institution must act on it within 15 days, subject to the exceptions provided for by law. If the request is refused or ignored, you can lodge an appeal with the Commissioner for Information of Public Importance and Personal Data Protection.
</section>

## Accountability cannot wait

The election victory has opened up the possibility of fundamental change, but **not all the levers of power have yet passed into the hands of the new political majority**. **The SNS government is in a caretaker capacity**, and Serbia also faces presidential elections.

However, that must not be a reason to leave questions of accountability for some other time. Institutions still have an obligation to act in accordance with the law, and records that may be important for the public interest must be protected now.

**Transparency is not a favour institutions do for citizens. It is their obligation.**
</section>
