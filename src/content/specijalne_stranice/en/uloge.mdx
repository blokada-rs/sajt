<section>
Election Day doesn’t run itself. Behind every vote that is counted correctly, every irregularity that is reported, and every citizen who knows who to turn to when something goes wrong, there is someone who chose to get involved that day and become part of the victory.

There is no single role that everyone has to take on. Some people are suited to spending the entire day at a polling station, others to canvassing their area for a few hours, calling people who have already expressed a willingness to help, running a campaign stand in their neighbourhood, or knocking on their neighbours’ doors. There is a place for everyone on this team – you just need to find yours.
</section>

<section>
## 1. People Who Protect Each Vote

On Election Day, millions of people will cast their ballots and go home. Election monitors stay.

They arrive before the first voters and leave only after the last result is recorded.

They monitor voting, check that procedures are followed, note any irregularities, and protect each vote citizens cast that day.

Any citizen of the Republic of Serbia who is of age can be an election monitor. Everyone goes through training to become familiar with the voting materials, procedures, and how to recognize and report irregularities.

On Election Day, they arrive before the polling station opens and stay until the end. After the station closes, they attend the vote count and follow the election materials to the competent election commission.

None of them is alone. A team works together, supporting each other and protecting the electoral will.

**Your one day can protect the votes of the entire neighborhood.**
</section>

<section>
## 2.​ On the Ground, Eyes Wide Open

While voting is underway, mobile teams are out on the streets and around polling stations.

Throughout Election Day, they visit polling sites, monitor key locations, and document any irregularities they spot. They immediately forward photos and videos to the relevant headquarters so every incident is recorded and action can be taken promptly.

Their presence ensures events outside polling stations don't go unnoticed. They provide support to election monitors and other teams observing Election Day from start to finish.

You don’t have to commit the entire day. A few hours are enough to tour part of the city, check the situation around polling stations, and support the people already on the ground.

When we are everywhere, there is less room for pressure, manipulation, and abuse.

Every pair of eyes matters. Every recording can serve as evidence. Every patrol means greater security for the people coming out to vote.

Join a mobile team.

Be where you are needed on Election Day.

## 3.​ Making the Call

Thousands of people have already shared their details with us. They have supported the student movement and shown a desire to help. Now, we need to call them.

Operators connect all parts of our network. They speak with those who have already offered support, listen to what they can and are willing to do, answer their questions, and connect them with teams in their city or municipality.

After a single call, someone might become an election monitor. Someone else might join a mobile team, staff an information booth, or help with organization. Some have a whole day to spare; others, just a few hours. There is a place for everyone.

Many people want to help but don’t know where to start. Your call could be their first step.

That is how support turns into active participation. That is how thousands of individuals connect to form a team that can cover every location in Serbia.

Call. Listen. Connect.

On the other end of the line might be the very person we’ve been looking for.

STUDENTS WIN.
</section>

## 4.​ That Is Where Change Begins

The stand is where we meet people in our local area.

It’s where citizens ask questions, voice concerns, sign a petition, pick up materials, or learn how to get involved. Since the student movement began, we have built trust and met people at these stands—people who later became part of actions in their own cities.

From a single conversation and a single signature, local initiatives, new teams, and actions mobilizing entire streets have emerged.

At the stand, we listen to what our neighborhood lacks, what people want to change, and what we can do together. It’s where people who, until yesterday, were merely following events say for the first time: “Count me in.”

All it takes is setting aside a few hours to listen and help them find a way to get involved.

Someone might decide to take that first step precisely because they met you. Next time, you’ll be standing there together.

Come to the stand. Get to know your neighborhood.

Mobilize the people around you.

STUDENTS WIN.
</section>

## **5.​ Door-to-Door, Across All of Serbia**

We will visit every corner of Serbia—every town, city, and village. That is the promise of the student initiative “A Student in Every Village.”

To reach every neighborhood, we need people who will knock on their fellow citizens’ doors and talk to them face-to-face.

Social media can spread the message, but trust is built when people meet, look each other in the eye, and listen. That is why we visit homes, talk to neighbors, and listen to what troubles them, what they want to change, and what they expect from their country.

In this way, we also reach those who do not use social media, do not attend rallies, or feel no one is listening to them. By going door-to-door, we show that no place or person has been forgotten.

Every conversation brings a new opinion, piece of advice, or an idea. Some will offer support, some will want to get involved, and others will have the chance—perhaps for the first time—to say what matters to them.

All it takes is setting aside a little time to visit your local area and listen to the people who live there.

Join the door-to-door campaign.

See you in your neighborhood.

STUDENTS WIN.
