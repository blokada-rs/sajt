Election night, April 24-25, 2016, Hercegovačka Street, in the very heart of Savamala. Dozens of people dressed in black — their faces hidden by balaclavas, armed with batons and powerful flashlights — descended upon this part of the city as if seizing territory in wartime. Excavators followed them. In the span of two hours, the duration of the entire operation, approximately one thousand square meters of buildings were demolished: restaurants, private and commercial properties belonging to people who had spent years building something of their own there.

The masked men secured the area and demanded identification from passersby. A security guard from nearby premises was tied up and left helpless while the excavators did their work. Horrified by what they were witnessing, citizens called the police. The police did not respond. Calls were redirected to the municipal police, who replied that the matter was outside their jurisdiction.

Saša Janković, the Ombudsman at the time, determined that the order barring police from deploying to the scene had come “from the top” of the police command structure. Footage from a nearby camera spans four hours and seventeen minutes. It clearly shows the excavators arriving from the direction of the “Belgrade Waterfront” construction site, a project that would soon become a symbol of everything this government represents. The same footage also captures the moment the streetlights were turned off.

The ex-wife of Siniša Mali, who was the Mayor of Belgrade at the time of the demolition, claimed Mali boasted to her about organizing a nighttime “site clearance” to remove structures that were obstructing the “Belgrade Waterfront” project.

Aleksandar Vučić, the Prime Minister at the time, stated that the city’s top leadership was behind the demolition and called the person responsible “a complete idiot” for doing it at night instead of in broad daylight.

Out of that entire apparatus — the thirty or so masked men, those who ordered the excavators, and those who instructed the police to stand down — exactly one person has been convicted to date: Goran Stamenković, the then shift commander of the Belgrade police duty service. His sentence? A five-month suspended sentence for professional misconduct. He later admitted he confessed only because Dijana Hrkalović promised him a promotion and the legalization of a building he owned in Mirijevo.
