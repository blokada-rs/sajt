title: Pardon
---

In 2025, Serbian President Aleksandar Vučić exercised his **power of pardon** in two cases involving violence against students during protests, in which two female students sustained serious injuries.

One case involved **M.S.**, against whom proceedings were initially conducted for **attempted murder after she struck a student, K.V., with her car** in New Belgrade.

The second case involved **four activists from the Serbian Progressive Party accused of attacking students in Novi Sad**.

**Class in Nature**

The first incident took place on January 24, 2025, on **Jurija Gagarina Street** in New Belgrade, during a high school walk known as **“Class in Nature”**. Footage shows that after being struck, K.V. ended up on the hood of the car, which continued moving for several more meters toward a large group of walk participants. She sustained severe head injuries after falling from the vehicle.

Proceedings against M.S. were initially conducted for **attempted murder**. Later, the charge was downgraded to **a serious offense against public safety**, and the case was transferred to the Third Basic Court in Belgrade. Following the President’s announcement, **a decision to grant a pardon** was issued, thereby concluding the case.

**Batons**

The second incident occurred on the night of January 27-28, 2025, **in Novi Sad**. A group of students was putting up posters calling citizens to join a protest and bridge blockades when they were attacked. During the attack, a female student named **A.V. suffered a broken, or rather, dislocated jaw**.  

Four activists from the Serbian Progressive Party, identified in court records by the initials **K.S., R.D., B.N., and D.N.**, were accused of the attack. They were charged with **inflicting serious bodily injury and violent behavior**. The trial was already underway, and A.V. had testified about the attack, her injuries, and the consequences she suffered. On July 2, 2025, the President of Serbia issued **a decision to pardon them**, thereby closing the case.

The legally controversial aspect of this affair is not the existence of the pardon mechanism itself, but **the manner in which it was exercised**. A pardon is an extraordinary **legal mechanism that enables the President of the Republic to grant a specifically named individual exemption from criminal prosecution, full or partial exemption from a sentence to a lighter penalty or a suspended sentence, rehabilitation, and the removal or reduction of certain legal consequences of a conviction**.

When used before a judicial conclusion, a pardon **ceases to function as a legal corrective and instead becomes a means of halting criminal proceedings before a verdict is reached**. In such a situation, the court is denied the opportunity to fully establish the facts, liability, and the status of the injured parties. At the same time, **a political decision** usurps the role independent institutions ought to play in a state governed by the rule of law.

The long-term consequence is **eroded trust in equality before the law**. When the outcome hinges on political will rather than institutional functioning, the message to citizens is that rules remain in force only until they inconvenience those with political protection.
